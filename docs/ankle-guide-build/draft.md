# Ankle Examination Playbook — draft

**Status: draft. Not verified. Not built. Do not publish.**

Written against `sources.md` and nothing else. Same four states as the hip and knee guides:
**unmarked** (the named source's freely readable text carries that exact value), **`Convention`**,
**`Contested`**, or **`No traceable source`** stated in place.

**The ankle is the best-served joint in this series.** Two freely readable graded guidelines cover
it — lateral ligament sprains with chronic instability, and midportion Achilles tendinopathy —
between them carrying 34 current graded recommendations. Every recommendation below was taken from
its guideline's **front-matter Summary of Recommendations**, which is the current set; the body
reprints prior revisions and one of them reversed direction, so era was checked by position rather
than assumed. See `sources.md`.

## 1 · Checklist — the examination in order

*Lede: one decision comes before everything else — whether this ankle needs an x-ray. Get it right
and get it early, and apply the rule entire.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Apply the Ottawa Ankle Rules first | And apply them **in their entirety**. Omitting malleolar tenderness and relying on inability to bear weight and take four steps dropped sensitivity to **88%**. `src: Martin 2021` |
| What the rules are for | Ruling out. Pooled **sensitivity 0.92 (0.91–0.93)**, **specificity 0.35 (0.34–0.36)**, **&minus;LR 0.13 (0.09–0.19)**, post-test probability of fracture after a negative around **1%**. `src: Sharifi Razavi 2026` |
| Red flags | Fracture, syndesmotic injury, Achilles rupture, infection, neurovascular compromise. `Convention` |
| Screen above | The knee and the proximal fibula — a Maisonneuve fracture is missed by looking only where it hurts. `Convention` |
| Classify from the history | Mechanism, time since injury, and crucially **first sprain or recurrence**, because prevention evidence differs for each. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Resting state | Swelling and where it sits, ecchymosis, point tenderness, standing foot posture. |
| What they can do alone | Gait and weight bearing, active range, **weight-bearing lunge for dorsiflexion**, single-limb balance eyes closed, Star Excursion Balance Test, timed single-limb hopping. `src: Martin 2021` |
| What you do to them | Passive against active, **talar translation and talar inversion**, accessory glide at three joints. `src: Martin 2021` |
| What is short | Gastrocnemius against soleus; plantar fascia and the windlass. |
| What is weak | The four directions, fibularis specifically, and **hip abduction, extension and external rotation**. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Where is it | Lateral ligaments, deltoid, syndesmosis, fibula along its length, fifth metatarsal base, Achilles. |
| What is it | Anterior drawer **plus** reverse anterolateral drawer and anterolateral talar palpation. `src: Martin 2021` |
| What you do | Two graded guidelines apply here — the most evidence any section in these four guides rests on. |

## 2 · Numbers — and what each one is worth

*Lede: the ankle has the strongest evidence in this series and still only one test family with
published accuracy. That contrast is the section.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Ottawa Ankle Rules, pooled | **Sn 0.92 (95% CI 0.91–0.93)**, **Sp 0.35 (0.34–0.36)**, **+LR 1.76 (1.46–2.13)**, **&minus;LR 0.13 (0.09–0.19)**, **DOR 16.21 (10.15–25.89)**. `src: Sharifi Razavi 2026` |
| What that buys you | A negative rule drops the probability of fracture to about **1%**, which is why it safely reduces radiography. A positive rule barely moves it — +LR 1.76. **Rule-out, not rule-in.** `src: Sharifi Razavi 2026` |
| The specificity is not one number | The guideline reports it across studies as **7.8% to 68%** against a sensitivity of **92%–100%** — a fourteen-fold spread. Quoting a single pooled specificity hides how much it varies by setting. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Apply them entire | Dropping malleolar tenderness and using only inability to bear weight and take four steps gave sensitivity **88%** — worse than the rule applied whole. `src: Martin 2021` |
| In children | The low-risk ankle rules' sensitivity of **85.7%** is **inferior** to the Ottawa rules' **100%** in that population. `src: Martin 2021` |
| What implementing them achieves | Reduced cost, wait time, length of stay (median **20 minutes**) and imaging, **without sacrificing outcomes**. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Anterior drawer accuracy | **`No traceable source`.** The guideline recommends the test at Grade B and does not publish its accuracy. Any Sn/Sp you have seen for it did not come from here. |
| Talar tilt accuracy | **`No traceable source`.** The only mention of "talar tilt" in the ankle guideline is inside its **literature search-strategy appendix** — a list of query terms, not a finding. |
| Normal ankle ROM | **`Convention`.** No free normative source, as at the hip and knee. `No traceable source` |
| What is traceable about that | Measured against a national probability sample, textbook values for hip and knee were off by **as much as 18°**. That paper does not cover the ankle, so it supports the *argument* here and is not an ankle figure. `src: Roach 1991` |
| Laxity and function come apart | Over six weeks after a sprain, self-reported function improved significantly while **anterior drawer laxity did not change at all** (P > .05). A patient can feel much better with an unchanged drawer. `src: Martin 2021` |
| An early MRI finding that predicts a slow course | A medial joint bone marrow contusion on tibia and/or talus within two weeks meant **25 versus 16 days** to normal walking (P = .0002) and **92 versus 56 days** to sport (P = .0001). `src: Martin 2021` |
| What predicts chronic instability at 6 months | **Inability to complete jumping and landing tasks within 2 weeks** of injury, poorer dynamic postural control, and lower self-reported function. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Modifiable risk for a repeat sprain | **Not using prophylactic bracing** and **not doing a balance-training programme** are both risk factors for a subsequent sprain. `src: Martin 2021` |

## 3 · Posture and resting alignment

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Swelling | Assess and document it — **Grade A**, at baseline and **two or more times** over an episode of care. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Where the swelling sits | Lateral, medial, or diffuse; and whether it tracks distally. `Convention` |
| Ecchymosis and point tenderness | Both feed the Ottawa decision and the severity classification. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Standing foot posture | Observe weight-bearing alignment and hindfoot position. `Convention` — no threshold making a posture abnormal is traceable here. |

## 4 · Active range and what they can do alone

*Lede: the guideline does something unusual here — it names the specific instruments, not just the
domains. Use the ones it names and your measurements compare to something.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Active range, four directions | Dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion, eversion. Compare sides; the norms are `Convention`. |
| Dorsiflexion, specifically | Measure it **weight-bearing, with the lunge test** — the instrument the guideline names. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Single-limb balance | **Static, on a firm surface, with eyes closed.** `src: Martin 2021` |
| Dynamic balance | **Star Excursion Balance Test**, in the **anterior, anteromedial, posteromedial and posterolateral** reach directions. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Hopping | Include **single-limb hopping under timed conditions** where appropriate — Grade B. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Why hopping matters beyond function | Inability to complete jumping and landing tasks within two weeks predicted chronic instability at six months. It is a prognostic test, not only a discharge test. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Gait | Can they bear weight and take four steps? That question is part of the Ottawa rule, not separate from it. `src: Sharifi Razavi 2026` |

## 5 · Passive range, end feel and length

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Passive against active | Compare each direction; passive exceeding active points to weakness, guarding or pain inhibition. `Convention` |
| Talar translation | Assess and document — **Grade A**. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Talar inversion | Assess and document — **Grade A**. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Accessory glide | Talocrural anterior-posterior, subtalar, and distal tibiofibular. `Convention` |
| Gastrocnemius against soleus | Dorsiflexion with the knee **straight**, then **bent**. The difference is the gastrocnemius contribution, because it crosses the knee. `Convention` |
| Plantar fascia | Windlass mechanism on great toe extension. `Convention` |

## 6 · Strength

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| The four directions | Dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion, eversion, against resistance. `Convention` |
| Fibularis specifically | The evertors are the lateral ligament's active partner, and the guideline supports **dry needling of the fibularis group** with proprioceptive training in chronic instability (**Grade C**). `src: Martin 2021` |
| Hip strength | In chronic ankle instability, **assess and document hip abduction, extension and external rotation strength**, two or more times over an episode of care — Grade C. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Why the hip is in an ankle guide | Because the guideline puts it there. A distal problem with a proximal contribution is not a theory here; it is a graded recommendation. |
| Strength norms | **`No traceable source`.** Use side-to-side difference and serial change. |

## 7 · Special tests

*Lede: the ankle's special tests are recommended without published accuracy. That is an unusual
and honest position for a guideline to take, and this section says so rather than filling the gap.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| The recommended set — Grade B | Use special tests **including the reverse anterolateral drawer test and anterolateral talar palpation, in addition to the traditional anterior drawer test**, alongside a thorough history and examination. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Note what that recommendation does | It says the traditional drawer **is not enough on its own** — the other two are added to it, not offered instead of it. |
| Accuracy of any of the three | **`No traceable source`.** Recommended, not quantified. |
| Talar tilt | **`No traceable source`**, and see §2 — its only appearance in the guideline is in a search-strategy appendix. |
| What the drawer does not tell you | Laxity and function move independently: function improved significantly over six weeks while drawer laxity did not change. **A stable-feeling ankle can still have a lax drawer, and vice versa.** `src: Martin 2021` |
| Classifying an acute sprain — Grade B | Use **level of function, ligamentous laxity, haemorrhaging, point tenderness, total ankle motion, swelling and pain**. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Identifying chronic instability — Grade B | Use a **reliable and valid discriminative instrument — the Cumberland Ankle Instability Tool or the Identification of Functional Ankle Instability** — plus a battery of functional performance tests with established validity to separate people with CAI from healthy controls. `src: Martin 2021` |
| The point of that row | Chronic ankle instability is identified by a **questionnaire and performance tests**, not by a laxity test. That surprises most students. |
| Interpreting a likelihood ratio | `Convention` for the familiar bands. What is sourced: a rule with &minus;LR 0.13 and +LR 1.76 is a rule-out. `src: Sharifi Razavi 2026` |

## 8 · Syndromes and what you call it

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Acute lateral ankle sprain | Classified by the seven clinical findings in §7 — Grade B. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Chronic ankle instability | Identified by CAIT or IdFAI plus validated functional performance tests — Grade B. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Midportion Achilles tendinopathy | Managed by loading first; see §10. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| Movement diagnosis | Name the movement fault and its direction under load. `Convention` |
| Relative stiffness | A stiff ankle borrows from the knee and the midfoot. `Convention` |
| Plantar heel pain | Deliberately **not covered**. The only reachable guideline for it is a confidential stakeholder-review draft, and this guide does not quote drafts. See `sources.md`. |

## 9 · Palpation

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Lateral ligaments | ATFL, CFL, PTFL in turn. `Convention` |
| Point tenderness | One of the seven findings the guideline names for classifying severity — Grade B. `src: Martin 2021` |
| The malleoli | Posterior edge and tip of each, because that is what the Ottawa rule asks about — and dropping it cost 12 points of sensitivity. `src: Martin 2021` |
| The fibula along its length | And the base of the fifth metatarsal. Both are Ottawa zones; neither is where the patient points. `Convention` |
| Achilles | Midportion against insertion — the guideline covers **midportion** only. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| What palpation cannot do | Grade a ligament or confirm instability. Chronic instability is a questionnaire-and-performance diagnosis. `src: Martin 2021` |

## 10 · Prevention, mobilisation, exercise and dose

*Lede: the best-evidenced section in any of these four guides, and the only one containing Grade A
**prevention**. Every letter below comes from a front-matter Summary of Recommendations, checked by
position, so it is the current revision rather than a superseded one.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Prevention, first sprain — **A** | **Prophylactic bracing** to reduce the risk of a first-time sprain, particularly in those with risk factors. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Prevention, never-injured — **C** | Prophylactic **balance training** may be recommended to people who have not yet sprained. Note the weaker grade than bracing. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Prevention, after a first sprain — **A** | **Prophylactic bracing *and* proprioceptive and balance-focused exercise**, targeted at the impairments found on examination, to reduce subsequent injury. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Acute — **A** | Advise **external supports (brace or taping)** and **progressive weight bearing**. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Acute — **A** | A structured therapeutic exercise programme: protected active ROM, stretching, neuromuscular training, postural re-education and balance training, **in clinic and at home**, matched to severity, impairments, preferences, learning needs and social barriers. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Acute manual therapy — **A** | **Lymphatic drainage, active and passive soft tissue and joint mobilisation, and anterior-to-posterior talar mobilisation within pain-free movement**, alongside exercise, to reduce swelling, improve pain-free mobility and normalise gait. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Return to work — **B** | A return-to-work schedule, a brace early in rehabilitation, occupational or sport-related training, and/or work hardening. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Acute — **A, against** | **Do not use ultrasound** for acute ankle sprains. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Acute — **C** | Ice with a therapeutic exercise programme; pulsed shortwave diathermy for oedema and gait deviation; low-level laser for early pain; NSAIDs where practice acts allow. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Chronic instability — **A** | **Proprioceptive and neuromuscular exercise** for dynamic postural stability and patient-perceived stability. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Chronic instability — **A** | **Manual therapy: graded joint mobilisations, manipulations, and weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing mobilisation with movement**, to improve weight-bearing dorsiflexion and dynamic balance **in the short term**. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Chronic instability — **B, against** | **Do not use bracing or taping as a stand-alone intervention** to improve balance and postural stability. Note it is Grade A *with* exercise and Grade B *against* alone — the brace is an adjunct, never the plan. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Chronic instability — **B** and **C** | Multiple interventions may supplement balance training, guided by patient values and clinical judgement (**B**); dry needling of the fibularis group with proprioceptive training (**C**). `src: Martin 2021` |
| Psychological — **E** | Psychologically informed techniques such as **motivational interviewing**, to maximise self-efficacy and address psychological correlates of injury adjustment. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Outcome measures — **A** | **PROMIS** physical function and pain interference, the **Foot and Ankle Ability Measure**, and the **Lower Extremity Functional Scale**. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Coping and fear — **C** | Pain Self-Efficacy Questionnaire acutely and post-acutely; **TSK-11** and **FABQ** for fear of movement and fear-avoidance in chronic instability. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Achilles — **A** | **Tendon loading exercise with loads as high as tolerated, as first-line treatment**, to improve function and decrease pain in midportion Achilles tendinopathy. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| Achilles — **B** | **Complete rest is not indicated.** Patients should continue activities **within their pain tolerance**. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| Achilles — **B** | Education and counselling, with either a **pain-science or a pathoanatomic focus**, combined with loading. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| Achilles — **C** | Heel lifts to temporarily reduce dorsiflexion during activity; multimodal treatment to enhance the benefits of exercise. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| Achilles — **C, against** | Do **not** use low-level laser; do **not** use therapeutic ultrasound alone. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| Achilles — **E** and **F** | Elastic tape and rigid taping (**E**); manual therapy and intramuscular dry needling for calf pain and stiffness (**F**). `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| The tape recommendation reversed | The **2018** guideline said *should not* use elastic tape (**F**); the **2024** revision says *may* (**E**). The current position is **may**. Quoting the older one would contradict the guideline while citing it. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| Achilles — **D** | **No recommendation is possible on orthoses**, because the evidence is contradictory. A guideline declining to recommend is itself information. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| The reassessment marker | Name it before treating. Swelling, ROM, talar translation, talar inversion and single-leg balance are the Grade A set, at baseline and **two or more times**. `src: Martin 2021` |

## 11 · Drill

1. Give the Ottawa Ankle Rules' pooled sensitivity and specificity. What is a negative worth?
2. Why must the rules be applied in their entirety, and what happens if they are not?
3. In children, which performs better — the low-risk ankle rules or the Ottawa rules?
4. What is the anterior drawer test's sensitivity?
5. Function improved over six weeks. What happened to drawer laxity?
6. How is chronic ankle instability actually identified?
7. Which three special tests does the guideline recommend, and what does that imply about the drawer alone?
8. Name the four Star Excursion Balance Test reach directions the guideline specifies.
9. Which hip movements should be tested in chronic ankle instability, and at what grade?
10. Bracing for chronic instability: Grade A or Grade B against? Explain both.
11. What is first-line treatment for midportion Achilles tendinopathy, and at what load?
12. Is rest indicated for Achilles tendinopathy?
13. Elastic tape for Achilles tendinopathy — should not, or may? Which revision said which?
14. What does the Achilles guideline say about orthoses?
15. What predicts chronic instability at six months?
16. What did an early medial bone marrow contusion mean for time to walking and to sport?
17. Why is plantar heel pain absent from this guide?
