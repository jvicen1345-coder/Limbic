# Ankle Examination Playbook — scope

The competencies the guide covers, in the order the examination is performed. This is the author's
decision, not the literature's. Sourcing applies to the *values* inside each item, not the choice
of items.

Section shape matches the shoulder, hip and knee guides: checklist, numbers, posture, AROM,
PROM & length, MMT, special tests, syndromes, palpation, mobs & exercise, drill, refs.

## Should I be examining this ankle at all?
1. Ottawa Ankle Rules — decide about imaging before anything else, and apply them entire
2. Red flags — fracture, syndesmotic injury, Achilles rupture, infection, neurovascular compromise
3. Screen the knee and the proximal fibula
4. Classify from the history — mechanism, time since injury, and whether this is a first sprain or a recurrence

## What is the resting state?
5. Swelling, and where it sits
6. Ecchymosis and point tenderness
7. Standing foot posture and weight-bearing alignment

## What can they do on their own?
8. Gait, including whether they can bear weight and take four steps
9. Active range in all four directions
10. Weight-bearing dorsiflexion by the lunge test
11. Single-limb balance, eyes closed
12. Dynamic balance by the Star Excursion Balance Test
13. Single-limb hopping under timed conditions

## What can I do to them?
14. Passive range against active
15. Talar translation and talar inversion
16. Accessory glide at the talocrural, subtalar and distal tibiofibular joints

## What is short?
17. Gastrocnemius against soleus — knee straight versus bent
18. Plantar fascia and the windlass mechanism

## What is weak?
19. The four ankle directions
20. Fibularis (peroneal) strength specifically
21. Hip abduction, extension and external rotation — the proximal contribution to a distal problem

## Where is it?
22. Lateral ligaments — ATFL, CFL, PTFL
23. Medial: deltoid ligament
24. Syndesmosis, the fibula along its length, and the base of the fifth metatarsal
25. Achilles, its midportion and insertion

## What is it?
26. Ligament tests — anterior drawer, reverse anterolateral drawer, anterolateral talar palpation
27. Syndesmosis tests
28. Achilles integrity
29. Interpret as likelihood ratios; cluster from the hypothesis

## What do I call it?
30. Acute lateral ankle sprain, graded
31. Chronic ankle instability, and how it is actually identified
32. Midportion Achilles tendinopathy
33. A movement diagnosis and where the relative stiffness sits

## What do I do about it?
34. Dose from irritability and severity
35. Prevention — the one place in these four guides with Grade A prophylaxis evidence
36. Acute management
37. Chronic instability management
38. Achilles tendinopathy management
39. Define the reassessment marker

## What this guide can and cannot do

Unusually for this series, the ankle is **well** served: two freely readable graded guidelines
cover lateral ligament sprains with chronic instability, and midportion Achilles tendinopathy.
Sections 30-39 are the best-evidenced in any of the four guides.

What it still cannot do: give normative ankle range of motion (expected untraceable, as at the hip
and knee), or give accuracy figures for the anterior drawer and talar tilt tests — the ankle
guideline recommends the drawer at Grade B without publishing its accuracy. Those get the
recommendation and no numbers.
