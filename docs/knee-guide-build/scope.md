# Knee Examination Playbook — scope

The competencies the guide covers, in the order the examination is performed, grouped by the
question each phase answers. This is the author's decision, not the literature's: it is what a
student is examined on and the order a clinician actually works in. Sourcing applies to the
*values* inside each item, not to the choice of items.

Section shape matches the shoulder and hip guides: checklist, numbers, posture, AROM, PROM &
length, MMT, special tests, syndromes, palpation, mobs & exercise, drill, refs.

## Should I be examining this knee at all?
1. Screen the hip and lumbar spine — knee pain refers from both
2. Ottawa Knee Rule — decide whether this needs a radiograph before it needs a special test
3. Red flags — septic arthritis, fracture, extensor mechanism rupture, DVT, malignancy
4. Classify from the history — traumatic or atraumatic, mechanical or inflammatory, and time since injury

## What is the resting state?
5. Standing alignment — varus, valgus, recurvatum
6. Effusion, and how to grade it
7. Quadriceps bulk and vastus medialis

## What can they do on their own?
8. Gait, including stance-phase control
9. Active range, flexion and extension
10. Extension lag versus a fixed flexion contracture
11. Squat and step-down
12. Functional composites and hop testing

## What can I do to them?
13. Passive range against active
14. End feel, including a springy block
15. Patellar mobility and tilt
16. Accessory motion — tibiofemoral and patellofemoral glide

## What is short?
17. Quadriceps length — Ely and prone knee bend
18. Hamstring length
19. Gastrocnemius, since it crosses the knee

## What is weak?
20. Quadriceps — and why inhibition dominates here
21. Hamstrings and the hamstring-to-quadriceps ratio
22. Hip abductors, because the knee follows the hip
23. Limb symmetry, and what it does and does not license

## Where is it?
24. Joint line, tibiofemoral
25. Patellar borders, tendon, tibial tubercle
26. Collateral ligament origins and insertions

## What is it?
27. Ligament tests — Lachman, anterior drawer, pivot shift, lever sign, varus and valgus stress
28. Meniscal tests — and why the answer changes with time since injury
29. Patellofemoral tests
30. Interpret as likelihood ratios; cluster from the hypothesis

## What do I call it?
31. A movement diagnosis
32. The common syndromes — ACL, meniscal, patellofemoral pain, OA, tendinopathy
33. Relative stiffness above and below

## What do I do about it?
34. Dose from irritability
35. Match intervention to impairment
36. Post-arthroplasty management, where the one graded guideline applies
37. Define the reassessment marker

## What this guide will not be able to do

Recorded here so the draft does not quietly overreach. Unlike the hip, the knee has no reachable
graded examination guideline; no free normative range of motion; no bedside diagnostic criteria
set; no Q angle threshold or reliability; no accuracy for patellofemoral tests; and no reliability
for the hand effusion tests. Items 5, 6, 15, 29 and parts of 9 will therefore be carried by
`Convention` and `no traceable source` rather than by citations, and the guide will say so in
place. Item 36 is the one section with graded evidence behind it.
