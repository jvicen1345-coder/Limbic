# Knee guide — verification

Step 4, run the same way as the hip's: every abstract re-fetched rather than read back from
`sources.md`, because on the hip two of four defects were already sitting in the source bank and
the draft had inherited them faithfully.

**Result: 4 defects in 48 cited claims.** One provenance, two omissions that flattered a
guideline, one conflation. No factual transcription errors this time — which is a real difference
from the hip, and the likely reason is that the knee bank was built after the hip's lessons, with
"does not support" written for each paper as it was read rather than afterwards.

---

## Pass 1 — transcription

Every figure re-checked against a freshly fetched abstract, with first author and journal
confirmed:

- **Kazemi 2023** — 18 studies, 6,702 patients, Sn 0.98 (0.96-0.99), Sp 0.43 (0.42-0.45), +LR
  1.56 (1.39-1.75), &minus;LR 0.12 (0.05-0.26), AUC 0.54, and the phrase "high diagnostic
  performance". All exact. Clean.
- **Nguyen 2025** — anterior drawer 77.5/86.1, Lachman 87.6/88.9, pivot shift 65.9/94.4, lever
  sign 93.8/94.4, 165 patients indicated for arthroscopy. All exact. Clean.
- **Norris 2025** — 101 participants, MRI reference, &gt;3 weeks, &kappa; 0.529 (0.368-0.691),
  +LR 3.02 (1.60-5.69), &minus;LR 0.45 (0.28-0.73), surface and fist position associated with
  outcome while effusion, force and pain were not. All exact. Clean.
- **Rinonapoli 2025** — 255 patients aged 20-45, two blinded surgeons, T1 at 5-7 days and T2 at
  4-5 weeks, 188 confirmed tears, McMurray Sn 91&rarr;80% with Sp 55&rarr;79% and PPV 92% at T2,
  McMurray+Apley best. All exact. Clean.
- **Bove 2026** — every quoted recommendation matched against the extracted full text, **including
  its modal verb**. "Should" and "may" are not interchangeable in a graded guideline, and the
  draft preserves each one.
- **Roach 1991** — the 18&deg; textbook discrepancy and the 3-5&deg; age difference. Note this
  paper covers hip **and knee**, so it is legitimately a knee source, not borrowed from the hip.

**Defect 1 — a sourced number wearing an unsourced label · provenance.** The likelihood-ratio row
said "a rule with a &minus;LR of 0.12 and a +LR of 1.56 **is a rule-out**", citing Kazemi. The
numbers are the paper's; the phrase "rule-out" is the reading they force, and is not a phrase the
paper uses — its own summary word is "high diagnostic performance". Quoting an inference inside a
citation blurs exactly the line this guide exists to hold. **Fixed:** numbers and label separated,
with the paper's own word named beside them.

**Recorded, not fixed:** the FAB sign (Sn 81.4%, Sp 97.2%) was available in Nguyen and left out.
That is a scope decision — it is not in `scope.md` and is rarely taught — not an oversight, and it
is written here so the omission is deliberate rather than silent.

## Pass 2 — selective quotation

Same question as the hip's second pass: does the draft mislead using only true numbers, by
choosing which to carry? Same answer, in the same place — the section built on a clinical practice
guideline, quoted for what it recommends.

**Defect 2 — harms omitted entirely · material omission.** The guideline states benefits, risks,
harms and costs **for every recommendation**. The draft carried the recommendations and none of
the harms. That inverts where the clinical judgement actually lives: a grade says how much
evidence exists, a harm statement says what it costs to be wrong. **Fixed**, with a row saying so
outright and the specific harms attached to the recommendations they belong to.

**Defect 3 — a recommendation-against quoted without its stated benefit.** The draft said "do not
routinely brace or splint". The guideline also records that **bracing into extension may have
short-term effects on pain and function** — and its stated harm is specific (prolonged bedrest,
restricted early mobility and gait training). Reported as a flat prohibition, it reads as
"bracing does not work"; reported properly, it is a recommendation against *routine* use by a
group that acknowledged a real benefit. **Fixed.**

**Defect 4 — manual therapy's own caveat dropped.** The guideline notes that its **effectiveness
may vary depending on therapist skill and the techniques used**. For a guide teaching students to
perform manual therapy, that is arguably the most useful sentence in the recommendation, and the
draft had cut it. **Fixed.**

**Also added:** Kinesio taping for swelling, a moderate recommendation the draft had omitted
altogether — and one worth having precisely because the guideline hedges it in its own words,
"however, evidence is mixed regarding its benefit". A guide that only reports unhedged
recommendations teaches that guidelines do not hedge.
