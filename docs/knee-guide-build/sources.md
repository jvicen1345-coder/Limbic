# Knee Examination Playbook — verified source bank

Same contract as `docs/hip-guide-build/sources.md`. Every entry was looked up in Europe PMC and
read. **Supports** is what the freely readable text actually says; **Does not support** is what a
reader might expect it to say and it does not. The second column is the point of the file.

## Guidelines — and what is not reachable

### Logerstedt DS, Scalzitti D, Risberg MA, Engebretsen L, Webster KE, Feller J, Snyder-Mackler L, Axe MJ, McDonough CM. Knee Stability and Movement Coordination Impairments: Knee Ligament Sprain Revision 2017.
*J Orthop Sports Phys Ther* 2017;47(11):A1-A47 · PMID 29089004 · **abstract only**

- **Supports** — that the guideline exists, that it was produced by the Orthopaedic Section of
  the APTA within the WHO ICF framework, and that it reviews recent literature to make
  recommendations on knee ligament sprain.
- **Does not support** — **anything else.** The abstract is a purpose statement. It contains no
  recommendation, no grade, no dose, no accuracy figure and no threshold. Citing it for any
  clinical value would be attaching a real guideline to a number it does not contain, which is
  precisely the failure the hip verification pass caught twice.
- **Consequence for this guide** — the knee cannot be written the way the hip was. There is no
  free graded guideline to hang the treatment section on.

### Bove AM, Carroll LA, Cone S, Dibblee P, Hensley CP, Lenington K, Manner PA, Scalzitti DA, Tompkins J, Bade MJ. Clinical practice guideline for physical therapist management of total knee arthroplasty: revision 2026.
*Phys Ther* 2026;106(7):pzag058 · PMID 42506877 · PMC13403188 · **free full text**

- Located and free, **not yet read for values.** Covers post-arthroplasty management. Useful for
  a post-surgical sub-section at most; it is not a knee examination guideline and must not be
  quoted as though it were.

## Ligament tests

### Nguyen LT, Truong SV, Nguyen UTP. Value of clinical tests in diagnosing anterior cruciate ligament tears: what is new?
*Rev Bras Ortop* 2025;60(5):s00451813005 · PMID 41383911 · PMC12695428 · **free full text**

- **Supports** — cross-sectional study, **165 knee-injury patients** indicated for arthroscopy
  (Jan-Dec 2022), clinical examination compared against arthroscopy as the gold standard.
  Sensitivity and specificity respectively:
  **anterior drawer 77.5% / 86.1%** · **Lachman 87.6% / 88.9%** · **pivot shift 65.9% / 94.4%** ·
  **lever sign 93.8% / 94.4%** · **forced active buckling (FAB) sign 81.4% / 97.2%**.
  Authors' conclusion: the lever sign is a useful test for diagnosing ACL tear.
- **Does not support** — these figures generalising to a clinic. **Every patient was already
  indicated for arthroscopy**, so pretest probability is high and the spectrum is narrow; that is
  the same selection effect that inflates the shoulder guide's surgical-population clusters.
  It also carries no likelihood ratios, no confidence intervals, and no reliability data.
- **The useful shape** — pivot shift is the specific one (94.4%) and the least sensitive (65.9%);
  Lachman is the balanced one. That ordering is the teaching point, not the decimals.

## Meniscal tests

### Rinonapoli G, Lucchetta L, Ancillai G, Manfreda F, Ceccarini P, Caraffa A. Clinical reliability of 6 meniscal tests: a diagnostic accuracy study of 255 patients.
*Acta Orthop* 2025;96:452-458 · PMID 40557851 · **abstract only**

- **Supports** — **255 patients aged 20-45** with knee trauma, examined by **two orthopaedic
  surgeons blinded** to history, MRI and each other's examination, at **5-7 days (T1)** and
  **4-5 weeks (T2)**. Arthroscopy confirmed **188 meniscal tears**.
  **McMurray** had the most balanced performance: **sensitivity 91% at T1 falling to 80% at T2**,
  while **specificity rose from 55% to 79%**, with the highest PPV of **92% at T2**.
  Combining **McMurray and Apley** gave the best accuracy and minimised false positives.
  McMurray and hyper-flexion were more sensitive to medial chondropathy; Thessaly, Ege and
  hyper-flexion were more influenced by anterior knee pain.
  Authors' conclusion, verbatim in substance: **no single clinical test was sufficiently reliable
  for independent diagnosis.**
- **Does not support** — **individual figures for Apley, Thessaly, Ege, joint line tenderness or
  hyper-flexion.** Only McMurray's numbers appear in the free abstract. Quoting a Thessaly
  sensitivity from this paper would be inventing it. Nor does it support these figures in a
  general population: 188 tears in 255 trauma patients is roughly 74% prevalence.
- **Why this is the most teachable finding in the knee bank** — the same test has different
  accuracy depending on **when** you perform it. McMurray is sensitive and non-specific in the
  first week and becomes specific and less sensitive by a month. Almost no student is taught that
  a test's operating characteristics move with time since injury, and it changes how an acute
  knee should be interpreted.
