# Knee Examination Playbook — draft

**Status: draft. Not verified. Not built. Do not publish.**

Written against `sources.md` and nothing else. Same four states as the hip guide: **unmarked**
(the named paper's freely readable text carries that exact value), **`Convention`**,
**`Contested`**, or **`No traceable source`** stated in place.

The knee has a thinner reachable literature than the hip, and this draft is shaped by that.
There is no free graded examination guideline, no normative range, no bedside criteria set, no Q
angle threshold, and no accuracy for the patellofemoral or effusion tests. One graded guideline
exists and it covers arthroplasty only. Rather than hide that behind confident prose, the guide
says it.

---

## 1 · Checklist — the examination in order

*Lede: two questions come before any special test — is this a knee problem at all, and does it
need an x-ray before it needs a hand on it.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Screen above and below | Hip and lumbar spine first. Knee pain refers from both, and a knee examined for referred pain reads normal. `Convention` |
| Decide about imaging early | Apply the Ottawa Knee Rule before the special tests. It is a **rule-out**: pooled sensitivity **0.98**, &minus;LR **0.12**. `src: Kazemi 2023` |
| Red flags | Septic arthritis, fracture, extensor mechanism rupture, DVT, malignancy. An acutely hot, exquisitely painful knee with fever is not a examination problem. `Convention` |
| Classify from the history | Traumatic or atraumatic, mechanical or inflammatory — and **how long ago**, which changes what the tests are worth (§7). |
| Resting state | Standing alignment, effusion, quadriceps bulk. |
| What they can do alone | Gait, active range, extension lag versus fixed flexion, squat, step-down, hop if appropriate. |
| What you do to them | Passive against active, end feel, patellar mobility, accessory glide. |
| What is short | Quadriceps (Ely, prone knee bend), hamstrings, gastrocnemius. |
| What is weak | Quadriceps first, and expect inhibition rather than weakness. Then hamstrings, then hip abductors. |
| Where is it | Joint line, patellar borders and tendon, tibial tubercle, collateral origins. |
| What is it | Ligament tests, meniscal tests, patellofemoral tests — interpreted as likelihood ratios, clustered from the hypothesis. |
| What you call it | A movement diagnosis, the syndrome, and where the relative stiffness sits. |
| What you do | Dose from irritability, match intervention to impairment, name the reassessment marker before treating. |

## 2 · Numbers — and what each one is worth

*Lede: the knee has fewer trustworthy numbers than the shoulder and fewer reachable ones than the
hip. What it does have is one excellent screening rule, decent ligament figures from an inflated
population, and a meniscal finding almost nobody is taught.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Ottawa Knee Rule | Pooled **sensitivity 0.98 (95% CI 0.96–0.99)**, **specificity 0.43 (0.42–0.45)**, **+LR 1.56 (1.39–1.75)**, **&minus;LR 0.12 (0.05–0.26)**, from **18 studies and 6,702 patients**. `src: Kazemi 2023` |
| What that actually means | **A rule-out and nothing else.** A +LR of 1.56 barely moves probability, and the HSROC **AUC is 0.54** — close to chance. The authors' own summary calls it "high diagnostic performance"; read the numbers rather than the phrase, or you will over-read a positive rule. `src: Kazemi 2023` |
| Lachman | **Sn 87.6% · Sp 88.9%** — the balanced ligament test. `src: Nguyen 2025` |
| Anterior drawer | **Sn 77.5% · Sp 86.1%**. `src: Nguyen 2025` |
| Pivot shift | **Sn 65.9% · Sp 94.4%** — the specific one, and the least sensitive. A positive is meaningful; a negative is not. `src: Nguyen 2025` |
| The caveat on all three | Those figures come from **165 patients already indicated for arthroscopy**. High pretest probability and a narrow spectrum inflate accuracy; expect worse in clinic. `src: Nguyen 2025` |
| Lever sign | **`Contested`.** One study reports **Sn 93.8% · Sp 94.4%** and calls it useful. `src: Nguyen 2025` Another, in a general traumatic-knee population against MRI, found only **moderate interrater agreement (&kappa; 0.529, 95% CI 0.368–0.691)** and, at best, **+LR 3.02 (1.60–5.69), &minus;LR 0.45 (0.28–0.73)** — "limited clinical utility". `src: Norris 2025` |
| Why they disagree | Norris says it outright: reviews naming the lever sign the best ACL rule-out rest on studies whose limitations "could bias the test outcome, potentially overestimating its clinical utility". One cohort was pre-selected for surgery; the other was not. `src: Norris 2025` |
| The lever sign's stranger finding | Its result depends on **the surface the leg rests on and where the examiner puts their fist** — both significantly associated with the outcome. Effusion grade, force applied and pain reported were **not**. `src: Norris 2025` |
| McMurray, in the first week | **Sn 91% · Sp 55%** at 5–7 days after injury. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| McMurray, at a month | **Sn 80% · Sp 79%**, with a **PPV of 92%**. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| The point of those two rows | **A test's operating characteristics move with time since injury.** Early, McMurray is sensitive and non-specific; by four to five weeks it is specific and less sensitive. A positive McMurray in week one means much less than the same positive a month later. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| Best meniscal combination | **McMurray with Apley** gave the best accuracy and minimised false positives, in 255 patients with 188 arthroscopically confirmed tears. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| Other meniscal tests | **`No traceable source`** for individual figures. Thessaly, Ege, Apley, joint line tenderness and hyper-flexion were compared but no accuracy numbers appear in the free text. Thessaly, Ege and hyper-flexion were noted as more influenced by anterior knee pain. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| The honest frame for the whole section | The authors' conclusion: **no single clinical test was sufficiently reliable for independent diagnosis.** `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| Normal knee ROM | **`Convention`.** No free normative source — **`No traceable source`**. The tables everyone quotes are paywalled at the table. |
| What is traceable about that | Measured against a national probability sample, normative hip **and knee** values differed from textbook estimates **by as much as 18&deg;**, and age-group differences were only 3–5&deg;. `src: Roach 1991` |
| Q angle | **`Convention`.** Published values are population-specific — separate figures for different national cohorts, for sedentary adults versus athletes — with **no universal norm and no reliability study** reachable — **`No traceable source`**. The familiar 15&deg; is not quoted here. |
| Knee OA bedside criteria | **`No traceable source`.** The knee has no reachable equivalent to the hip's Grade A criteria set. |
| Patellofemoral test accuracy | **`No traceable source`.** Searched twice; nothing gives accuracy for a bedside patellofemoral test. |
| Effusion test reliability | **`No traceable source`.** Searched twice; the literature is MRI volumetry, not the sweep and ballottement tests students are examined on. |
| Quadriceps limb symmetry index | **`Convention`.** Widely used as a return-to-sport threshold; the cutoff's origin is not reachable here. Cohorts apply it, none establish it. |

## 3 · Posture and resting alignment

*Lede: the shortest section in the guide, because almost nothing here has been measured in a way
a reader can check.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Standing alignment | Observe varus, valgus and recurvatum, weight-bearing, from front and side. `Convention` — no threshold that makes an alignment abnormal is traceable here. |
| Effusion — how to test | Sweep/stroke test for small effusions; ballottement for large. Grade and compare sides. |
| Effusion — how much to trust it | **`No traceable source`** for reliability or accuracy of either hand test. Record what you found and re-measure it yourself rather than citing a grade. |
| Quadriceps bulk | Compare girth and vastus medialis definition side to side. Atrophy is a chronicity marker. `Convention` |

## 4 · Active range and what they can do alone

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Active flexion and extension | Compare sides before comparing to a book — the norms are `Convention` (§2) and the other knee is the better reference. |
| Extension lag versus fixed flexion | If passive extension exceeds active, it is a **lag** — a quadriceps problem. If both are equally limited, it is a **contracture** — a joint problem. `Convention`, and the single most useful distinction in this section. |
| Gait | Observe stance-phase control, quadriceps avoidance and thrust. `Convention`; no free accuracy figures for gait observation at the knee. |
| Squat and step-down | Depth, symmetry, frontal-plane control, and whether the symptom reproduces. `Convention` |
| Hop testing and limb symmetry | Used as a return-to-sport gate. The threshold is `Convention` — cohorts apply a limb symmetry cutoff, none reachable here establishes one. |

## 5 · Passive range, end feel and length

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Passive against active | Passive exceeding active points to inhibition or weakness; both equally limited points to the joint. `Convention` |
| End feel | Capsular, bony, springy, empty, guarding. A **springy block** to extension is the classic displaced-meniscus finding. `Convention` — taught universally, no reachable reliability at the knee. |
| Patellar mobility and tilt | Glide medially and laterally in slight flexion; assess tilt. `Convention` — no traceable threshold for what counts as hyper- or hypomobile. |
| Accessory motion | Tibiofemoral anterior and posterior glide, patellofemoral glide. Grade resistance, compare sides. `Convention` |
| Quadriceps length | Prone knee bend or Ely's test; watch for a compensatory pelvic rise. `Convention` |
| Hamstring length | Passive straight-leg raise or 90/90 knee extension. `Convention` |
| Gastrocnemius | Ankle dorsiflexion with the knee straight against with it bent — the difference is the gastrocnemius contribution. `Convention` |

## 6 · Strength

*Lede: at the knee the important question is usually not how weak the quadriceps is but whether
it is weak at all, as opposed to inhibited.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Quadriceps | Test through range and against resistance; prefer a dynamometer so the number repeats. |
| Weakness or inhibition? | Retest after reducing pain and effusion. A grade that improves at once was inhibition. `Convention`, and the distinction the rest of the knee plan depends on. |
| Why it matters here specifically | The one graded guideline available recommends **daily NMES to the quadriceps at the highest tolerable intensity** after arthroplasty, at moderate evidence quality — an intervention aimed squarely at inhibition rather than at load. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Hamstrings | Prone knee flexion against resistance; note the hamstring-to-quadriceps relationship. `Convention` |
| Hip abductors | Test them. The knee follows the hip in the frontal plane, and a valgus collapse on step-down is often a hip finding. `Convention` |
| Strength norms | **`No traceable source`** for knee strength norms in this bank. Use side-to-side difference and serial change. |

## 7 · Special tests

*Lede: two things make this section different from the shoulder's. The ligament figures come from
a population pre-selected for surgery, and the meniscal figures move depending on when you
examine. Both change how the numbers should be used rather than merely qualifying them.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Run tests from a hypothesis | Classify first — traumatic or atraumatic, and how long ago. A battery run on everyone produces false positives at the rate prevalence dictates. `Convention` |
| Lachman | **Sn 87.6% · Sp 88.9%** — the workhorse. `src: Nguyen 2025` |
| Anterior drawer | **Sn 77.5% · Sp 86.1%**; less sensitive than Lachman for the same structure. `src: Nguyen 2025` |
| Pivot shift | **Sn 65.9% · Sp 94.4%** — rule-in. Hard to perform on a guarding patient, which is part of why sensitivity is low. `src: Nguyen 2025` |
| Lever sign | `Contested` — see §2. Treat a positive as a prompt, not a diagnosis, and standardise your surface and fist position. `src: Nguyen 2025` `src: Norris 2025` |
| The population caveat | All the above except the lever-sign contradiction come from patients already scheduled for arthroscopy. `src: Nguyen 2025` |
| McMurray | Time-dependent: **Sn 91% / Sp 55%** at 5–7 days, **Sn 80% / Sp 79%** and PPV **92%** at 4–5 weeks. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| Meniscal clustering | **McMurray with Apley** minimised false positives and gave the best accuracy. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| Thessaly | **`No traceable source`** for its accuracy in this bank, and it was noted as more influenced by anterior knee pain. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| Patellofemoral tests | **`No traceable source`** for the accuracy of any of them. Use them to reproduce and localise the symptom, not to confirm a diagnosis. |
| Varus and valgus stress | **`No traceable source`** for accuracy in this bank. Graded by end feel and opening compared to the other side. `Convention` |
| Interpreting a likelihood ratio | `Convention` — the familiar bands (+LR over 10 decisive, 2–5 a small shift) are a teaching heuristic, not a value traced to any source here. What *is* sourced: a rule with a &minus;LR of 0.12 and a +LR of 1.56 is a rule-out. `src: Kazemi 2023` |
| The section's honest summary | No single knee test is reliable enough to diagnose alone. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |

## 8 · Syndromes and what you call it

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| ACL rupture | Suspected from mechanism and effusion timing, screened with Lachman, confirmed by neither examination nor the lever sign alone. `src: Nguyen 2025` `src: Norris 2025` |
| Meniscal tear | McMurray with Apley is the best available combination, read against time since injury. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| Patellofemoral pain | A diagnosis of pattern and exclusion here, because no bedside test has reachable accuracy. **`No traceable source`** |
| Knee osteoarthritis | **`No traceable source`** for a bedside criteria set. Unlike the hip, there is no Grade A rule to apply. |
| After arthroplasty | The one syndrome in this guide with graded evidence behind its management — see §10. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Movement diagnosis | Name the movement fault and its direction under load, not only the tissue. `Convention` |
| Relative stiffness | A knee that will not flex borrows from the hip and the ankle. Look above and below before treating the knee. `Convention` |

## 9 · Palpation

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Joint line | Tibiofemoral, medial and lateral, knee flexed. Tenderness raises meniscal suspicion but its accuracy is **`No traceable source`** in this bank. `src: Rinonapoli 2025` |
| Patella | Borders, facets, tendon, tibial tubercle. `Convention` |
| Collaterals | Origins and insertions of the medial and lateral collateral ligaments. `Convention` |
| What palpation cannot do | Distinguish a tear from degeneration, or confirm any of the syndromes above. Use it to localise. |

## 10 · Mobilisation, exercise and dose

*Lede: this section is narrower than the hip's on purpose. The knee has no free graded guideline
for examination or for non-surgical management. It has one for **arthroplasty**, and that is the
only place in this guide where an evidence grade can be attached to a recommendation.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| What this section can and cannot do | **`No traceable source`** for graded non-surgical knee management. The knee ligament sprain guideline exists but its free abstract is a purpose statement carrying no recommendation, grade, dose or threshold. `src: Logerstedt 2017` |
| Where grades do apply | Post-arthroplasty, from an APTA guideline whose full text is free and grades each recommendation by evidence quality. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Strength training — **high** | Design, implement and teach progressive strength training beginning in the **early postacute** period, to improve function, strength and ROM. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Cryotherapy and positioning — **high** | Prescribe cryotherapy and teach elevation of the surgical limb at **30–90&deg; of knee flexion** in the early postoperative period. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Digital health tools — **high** | Consider them either alongside in-clinic care or as an alternative to it. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Continuous passive motion — **high, against** | Do **not** use CPM after primary uncomplicated arthroplasty. `src: Bove 2026` |
| NMES — **moderate** | Apply to the quadriceps **at least daily**, from the early postoperative period, **at the highest tolerable intensity**, for strength, gait and performance outcomes. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Range of motion exercise — **moderate** | Include passive, active-assistive and active ROM exercise. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Manual therapy — **moderate** | May be used with exercise and/or devices to augment active-assistive exercise for ROM early. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Pain — **moderate** | Cryotherapy early; TENS in acute and subacute phases, Kinesio taping in the acute phase, manual therapy and psychologically informed techniques for pain after arthroplasty. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Activity — **moderate** | Encourage early activity and progress it against safety, functional tolerance, physiological response and shared goals. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Delivery — **moderate** | Group-based or individual sessions are both acceptable. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Bracing and splinting — **moderate, against** | Do **not** routinely brace or splint early to gain knee ROM. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Swelling — **low, against** | Do **not** routinely use manual lymphatic drainage, compression dressings or CPM for swelling; they are unproven. `src: Bove 2026` |
| What is striking about that list | **The strongest evidence in it is negative.** The high-quality recommendation is what to stop doing, and the moderate and low grades add bracing, splinting, lymphatic drainage and compression to the same list. `src: Bove 2026` |
| The reassessment marker | Name it before you treat. Extension lag, effusion grade, a timed functional measure — whichever the impairment was. `Convention` |

## 11 · Drill

Recall prompts. Answers are the rows above.

1. What is the Ottawa Knee Rule's specificity, and what does that make it good for?
2. Its AUC is 0.54. How does that square with "high diagnostic performance"?
3. Give Lachman, anterior drawer and pivot shift sensitivities. Which is the rule-in test?
4. Why should you discount all three slightly?
5. Two studies disagree about the lever sign. Give both results and the reason for the gap.
6. What two things change the lever sign's result that have nothing to do with the knee?
7. McMurray at one week versus one month — give both sensitivity and specificity.
8. Why does that matter for an acute knee?
9. Which two meniscal tests combine best?
10. What is Thessaly's sensitivity?
11. Extension lag or fixed flexion contracture — how do you tell, and what does each implicate?
12. What is the normal knee range of motion?
13. By how much did textbook values differ from a measured population sample?
14. What is a normal Q angle?
15. Which intervention after knee arthroplasty has high-quality evidence *against* it?
16. What is the NMES recommendation, and at what intensity?
17. Where does the limb symmetry threshold come from?
