# Neurologic Examination guide — scope

Step 1 of `docs/joint-playbook-template.md`. **This file is mine, not the literature's.** It
decides which competencies the guide covers and what order they are performed in; `sources.md`
then decides which of the values attached to them can actually be traced.

## What makes this one different from the four joint guides

The joint guides examine a region. This one examines a *system*, and that changes two things.

**The order is not head-to-toe, it is gating.** Each phase decides whether the next phase can be
believed. A patient who cannot sustain attention cannot give you a reliable sensory exam, so
cognition is screened before sensation, not after it. A patient with a dense proprioceptive loss
will fail coordination testing for reasons that have nothing to do with the cerebellum, so
sensation precedes coordination. This dependency is the spine of the guide and the reason the
checklist is in the order it is.

**The finding is localization, not tissue.** A shoulder finding routes to a tissue. A neuro
finding routes to a *level* — cortex, brainstem, cord, root, plexus, peripheral nerve, muscle,
or the neuromuscular junction — and the last column of every table has to carry the discriminator
that separates two levels, not a restatement of the test.

## Sections the guide will carry

Twelve content sections plus the drill and the references, following the template's shape:

1. `checklist` — the examination in order, one tick box per competency above
2. `numbers` — the key-numbers grid
3. `subjective` — interview, onset tempo, red flags
4. `vitals` — vital signs, orthostatics, autonomic dysreflexia
5. `cognition` — arousal, attention, standardized screens, communication
6. `cranial` — the twelve nerves, tested in order, with the discriminators
7. `sensory` — the four modalities plus cortical sensation
8. `tone` — tone, spasticity, reflexes, pathological reflexes
9. `motor` — patterned vs selective, graded selective control, UMCT, strength
10. `coordination` — non-equilibrium, equilibrium, Romberg
11. `perception` — fields, neglect, praxis
12. `function` — task analysis and the core outcome measures
13. `localize` — the synthesis table: findings to level
14. `drill` — recall questions
15. `refs` — references

## What this guide will not do

- **It is not a stroke guide.** Stroke is the commonest referral, but the examination is the same
  one performed on a demyelinating, degenerative, traumatic or compressive presentation, and
  writing it around stroke would make the localization tables wrong for everyone else.
- **It does not carry an intervention section.** The four joint guides end in treatment; the
  neurologic evidence for intervention is a different literature (locomotor training, CIMT, dose)
  and attaching a thin version of it to an examination guide would be worse than omitting it.
- **It does not reproduce a copyrighted instrument.** The MoCA, the Berg and the Fugl-Meyer are
  named, scored and interpreted here; their item content is not reproduced.

## Known risk before sourcing starts

Large parts of the classical neurologic examination are **taught everywhere and measured
nowhere.** The tuning-fork frequency, the order of the cranial nerve exam, "sharp/dull," the
0–4 reflex scale and most of the coordination tests are conventions of the physical
examination rather than validated instruments. The provenance machinery exists for exactly this
case: the expectation going into `sources.md` is that this guide carries **more `Convention`
marks than any of the four joint guides**, and that a large minority of values will end up
flagged as having no traceable source. That is the honest result, not a failure of searching.
