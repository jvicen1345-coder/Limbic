# Joint Mobilization Playbook — scope

**This is not a regional examination guide, and the shape had to be redesigned.** The shoulder,
hip, knee and ankle guides walk a body region in the order it is examined. This one walks a
*decision*: whether to mobilise, where, how hard, and how you would know it worked.

## The competencies

### Should I be mobilising this joint at all?
1. Screen for contraindications before anything else
2. Establish that the restriction is **articular** rather than muscular, neural or pain-driven
3. Decide what you expect to change, and how you will measure it
4. Decide whether the evidence supports mobilisation **for this region** — because it varies

### What do I need to know before choosing a technique?
5. Active range, and where in the range the problem lies
6. Passive range against active, and end feel
7. Accessory motion: which glide is restricted, and in which direction
8. Joint position — resting/open-packed versus close-packed
9. Convex-concave: what it predicts and how much to trust it

### How hard, how long, and where do I stop?
10. Force relative to tissue resistance — the vocabulary the guidelines actually use
11. Amplitude and oscillation
12. Where in the available range the technique is applied
13. Dose: repetitions, duration, frequency, and what the guidelines specify
14. **The grading systems: what they are, and what cannot be traced about them**

### What are the techniques?
15. Sustained glides
16. Oscillatory mobilisation
17. Long-axis distraction
18. Mobilisation with movement
19. Manipulation, and how it differs from mobilisation
20. What cavitation is and is not evidence of

### How do I do it safely and reproducibly?
21. Patient position and joint pre-positioning
22. Stabilisation of the proximal segment
23. Hand placement and force direction
24. Communicating with the patient, and what to ask during

### What does it actually achieve?
25. Effects on range, and how long they last
26. Effects on pain, and whether the same dose produces both
27. The evidence by region, with grades attached
28. Reassessment: the marker, and when to re-measure

## What this guide can and cannot do

**Can:** state, with grades, what five clinical practice guidelines recommend for manual therapy
across four regions and after arthroplasty; state the one dose scale that is defined and has
outcomes attached; and be honest that the evidence is regional rather than general.

**Cannot, unless a source turns up:** define a Maitland or Kaltenborn grade, cite its origin, or
give any reliability figure for whether two clinicians produce the same grade. That is not a gap in
the guide — it is the guide's most useful finding, and section 06 is built around it.
