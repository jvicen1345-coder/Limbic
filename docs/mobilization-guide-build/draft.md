# Joint Mobilization Playbook — draft

**Status: draft. Not verified. Not built. Do not publish.**

Written against `sources.md` and nothing else. Same four states as the hip, knee and ankle guides:
**unmarked** (the named source's freely readable text carries that exact value), **`Convention`**,
**`Contested`**, or **`No traceable source`** stated in place.

**This guide is shaped by an absence.** Three of the conventions that decide how a mobilization is
performed — the grading systems, open- and close-packed positions, and the concave-convex rule —
could not be traced to any reachable definition, derivation or reliability figure. Meanwhile the
things that *are* measured are modest: two clinicians agree on an accessory glide at ICC 0.64, the
only poolable proprioception result is null, and no neuroimaging study links the cortical change it
finds to the clinical improvement it observes. The guide's job is to put those two columns side by
side honestly, and to hand the reader the one dose vocabulary that *is* defined and has outcomes
attached.

Every guideline recommendation below was taken from its **front-matter Summary of Recommendations**,
confirmed by character position, because these documents reprint superseded revisions in the body.
See `sources.md`.

## 1 · Checklist — the reasoning sequence

*Lede: mobilization is a decision before it is a technique. The sequence below is the decision, and
the first two steps disqualify most of the joints you were about to put your hands on.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Screen before you touch | Contraindications first, every time. The reachable harms data is reassuring about *common* events and silent about rare ones — it cannot tell you screening is unnecessary. `src: Daniels 2026` |
| Is the restriction articular at all? | Mobilization treats a joint. If the limit is muscular, neural, or pain-driven guarding, the technique is aimed at the wrong tissue. `Convention` |
| Does the evidence support it **here**? | **This is regional, not general.** Grade A at the hip and ankle, expert opinion (F) for Achilles, *weak* recommendations after knee arthroplasty, and **absent entirely** from the knee meniscal and cartilage guideline. `src: Koc 2025` `src: Martin 2021` `src: Chimenti 2024` `src: Bove 2026` `src: Logerstedt 2018` |
| What do you expect to change? | Range, pain, or both — and they do not respond to the same dose. High force moved range; low force moved pain pressure threshold. Decide which you are buying. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Which glide, which direction? | Assess accessory motion, then name the restricted direction before choosing a technique. `Convention` |
| How hard? | Use the force vocabulary that is defined: **stretching exceeding resistance / stopping at the point of resistance / no resistance**. `src: Koc 2025` |
| How much? | The hip guideline's own conclusion is that **a specific dosage "could not be made"** — five of ten studies were at high risk of bias. Its stated range for mild-to-moderate hip OA is **1 to 3 times per week over 6–12 weeks**. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Re-measure immediately | The measured effects are immediate and short-term. If you do not re-test in the same session, you have no evidence you did anything. `src: Hadjisavvas 2026` `src: Martin 2021` |
| Then load it | Mobilization appears in every guideline that recommends it **alongside exercise**, never instead of it. `src: Koc 2025` `src: Martin 2021` `src: Bove 2026` |
| What you may not claim | That a grade number describes your dose, that the pop means anything, or that a central mechanism explains the result. None of the three is traceable. `src: Sillevis 2025` `src: Jahromi 2026` |

## 2 · Numbers — and the ones that do not exist

*Lede: this section is unusual because half of it is empty on purpose. The empty half is the
finding.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Maitland grades I–IV | **`No traceable source`.** No reachable paper defines them, derives them, or reports whether two clinicians produce the same one. Searched directly; also absent as a definition from all five guideline PDFs held. |
| Kaltenborn grades I–III | **`No traceable source`.** Same searches, same result. Zero mentions across all five guidelines. |
| Whether a nominated grade is reproducible | **`No traceable source`.** No force or amplitude reliability study was reachable. This is the single most important gap in the guide. |
| The one guideline mention of a grade | The ankle guideline refers to "anterior-to-posterior **Maitland grade III** joint mobilizations" while summarising someone else's trial. **It uses the grade as a label and never defines it.** `src: Martin 2021` |
| Open-packed / close-packed positions | **`No traceable source`.** Four search-phrase variants returned nothing that defines, derives or tests them — and the hip guideline performs its distraction "in **open packed position**" without defining that either. `src: Koc 2025` |
| The concave-convex rule | **`Convention`**, and now **`Contested`**. The one clinical paper that engages it measured 36 (3) mm of **posterior** femoral translation from 90° to 150° of knee flexion and concluded the findings "invite review of the concave-convex rule as it might apply to manual therapy of the knee". `src: Scarvell 2019` |
| Inter-rater agreement on an accessory glide | **ICC 0.64** (SEM 2.8°, MDC 4.7°) for the posterior talar glide test between a novice and an experienced rater. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| Intra-rater agreement, same test | **ICC 0.97** (novice) and **0.94** (experienced). One clinician is highly consistent with themselves; two clinicians are not consistent with each other. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| What fixed it | Adding a **referential horizontal plane** raised inter-rater agreement to **ICC 0.78**. Not skill, not vocabulary — an external measurable datum. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| The noise floor | **MDC 3.3°–4.7°.** A difference smaller than roughly four degrees between two examiners is not a finding. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| High-force long-axis distraction, hip | Flexion **10.6°**, extension **8.0°**, abduction **6.4°**, adduction **3.3°**, ER **5.6°**, IR **7.6°** — all significant (p < .05) against the low-force group. Three sessions, 1-week follow-up. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Low- and medium-force, same study | **No significant change in hip ROM at all.** `src: Koc 2025` |
| But low force won on pain | Largest effect on pain pressure threshold, **d = 2.0**. High force had the largest effect on physical function, **d = 0.5–0.7**. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Single-session hip MWM | Immediate: pain **d = 1.9**, flexion ROM **d = 3.0**, IR ROM **d = 1.4**, TUG **d = 1.0**, 30-second chair stand **d = 1.7**, 40-m walk **d = 1.5** (n = 40, mean age 78 ± 6). `src: Koc 2025` |
| Mulligan MWM in rotator cuff disease | Pain at rest MD **&minus;1.19** (I² = 0%), pain on activity MD **&minus;2.25** (I² = 67%), function MD **&minus;14.71**, ROM MD **19.92**, joint position sense MD **&minus;3.31**. Quality of life: **no significant difference**. `src: Alqallaf 2025` |
| Mulligan SNAGs in chronic low back pain | Pain SMD **&minus;0.78**, disability SMD **&minus;0.60**, lumbar extension ROM SMD **0.75**; flexion ROM **not significant**. `src: Shager 2026` |
| Read that last row with its heterogeneity | **I² = 92%, 83% and 93%.** The authors rate certainty **moderate for pain and low for disability and mobility**. A pooled estimate at that dispersion describes no single population. `src: Shager 2026` |
| Proprioception, the only poolable result | Knee joint position sense error at 60° in patellofemoral pain, two trials: **Hedges' g = &minus;0.21, 95% CI &minus;1.36 to 0.94, I² ≈ 83% — not significant.** `src: Hadjisavvas 2026` |
| Adverse events, older adults | **412** study-related events, **9 severe**, **none catastrophic**; mostly soreness, stiffness, numbness, weakness, fatigue, headache, dizziness. `src: Daniels 2026` |
| What that count is not | **Not an incidence.** There is no denominator, several included studies lacked adverse-event definitions, and the review excluded the database designs that detect rare events. `src: Daniels 2026` |

## 3 · Before you touch — screening and contraindications

*Lede: the honest position is that common harms are well described and mild, and the rare ones are
undescribed rather than absent. Those are different statements and the difference is the section.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| What actually happens, commonly | Changes in pain quality, muscle or joint soreness or stiffness, numbness, weakness, fatigue, headache, dizziness or lightheadedness. **Mild to moderate.** `src: Daniels 2026` |
| Severe events, in the trial and cohort data | **9 of 412**, and **none catastrophic**, across 6 RCTs and 4 observational studies in adults over 55. `src: Daniels 2026` |
| Severe events, in the case reports | **6 severe adverse events, four of them vascular.** Twenty of the case reports involved spinal manipulation. `src: Daniels 2026` |
| The technique that generated no case reports | **None** were reported following soft tissue techniques, exercise, or other modalities. Every case report involved a thrust, a modality, or repetitive shoulder abduction. `src: Daniels 2026` |
| Why "no catastrophic events" is not reassurance | The review **excluded administrative database studies**, and its authors name that as a limitation precisely because those "may detect rare AEs". The design could not have found a catastrophic event. `src: Daniels 2026` |
| One more caveat on that source | It was **partially funded by Clinical Compass and the NCMIC Foundation**, both chiropractic organisations. A harms review funded by the profession under review is a conflict to weigh, not to ignore. `src: Daniels 2026` |
| A validated pre-treatment screening test | **`No traceable source`.** Nothing reachable validates a screening procedure against the event it is meant to prevent. The nearest hit was a survey of what chiropractic faculty *teach* about cervical artery dissection — a study of teaching, not of screening. |
| Harms in the hip guideline | **"No harms of manual therapy were reported."** Stated plainly, in a Grade A recommendation. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Modify for the person in front of you | Grade F: clinicians *may* modify procedures and **force amplitude** according to bony hip morphology and **tissue tolerance/irritability**. `src: Koc 2025` |
| The ankle guideline's own constraint | Acute-phase talar mobilization is specified **"within pain-free movement"**. That is a safety boundary written into the recommendation itself. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Absolute contraindications | Fracture, instability, infection, malignancy, and any undiagnosed progressive neurological deficit. `Convention` — universally taught, no reachable source lists or tests them. |
| What to do when unsure | Treat "I cannot rule this out" as a reason to examine further, not as a reason to proceed gently. Grade of force does not convert an unscreened joint into a screened one. `Convention` |

## 4 · What the joint does on its own

*Lede: active range is where you decide whether there is an articular problem to mobilise. Get this
wrong and everything after it is a well-executed answer to the wrong question.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why active comes first | It is the patient's own report in movement — willingness, pain arc and available range in one observation. `Convention` |
| What a full active range tells you | That the joint surfaces travel. A joint with full active range rarely needs accessory mobilization for range. `Convention` |
| What a limited active range does not tell you | Which tissue limits it. Active limitation is consistent with capsular restriction, muscular restriction, pain inhibition and unwillingness, and mobilization is indicated for only one of those. `Convention` |
| Where in the range the problem sits | Note the arc, not just the endpoint. A mid-range catch and an end-range block are different problems. `Convention` |
| Weight-bearing versus non-weight-bearing | The ankle guideline names **weight-bearing** techniques and **weight-bearing lunge** measurement specifically — position changes both the technique and the measurement. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Normative range values | **`No traceable source`** for every joint in this series, as the hip, knee and ankle guides each found independently. |
| The measurement you can trust | A within-session change on the same measure, taken by the same person. Intra-rater reliability is where the agreement actually lives — ICC 0.94–0.97 against 0.64 between raters. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| So who re-measures? | **You do, and immediately.** Handing the re-test to a colleague discards most of the reliability the measurement had. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| What to record | The starting value, the technique and force level, and the value after. Without the first and third, the second is anecdote. `Convention` |

## 5 · Accessory motion and end feel

*Lede: this is the assessment mobilization is built on, and it is the one place where a real
reliability figure exists. It is not flattering, and it points at the fix.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| What accessory motion is | The glide, spin and roll between joint surfaces that a patient cannot perform voluntarily. `Convention` |
| Why assess it before treating | The direction you find restricted is the direction the technique is aimed. `Convention` |
| Can you reproduce your own finding? | **Yes, well.** Posterior talar glide test intra-rater **ICC 0.97** (novice) and **0.94** (experienced), SEM 2.2–2.4°. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| Can two clinicians reproduce each other's? | **Much less.** Inter-rater **ICC 0.64**, described as poor to good. Experience did not close the gap — the novice and the experienced rater were equally self-consistent. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| What improved agreement | A **referential horizontal plane** — an external datum — raised inter-rater ICC to **0.78** and cut SEM to 2.3°. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| The lesson to take from that | Reproducibility came from **measuring against something outside the hands**, not from more skill or a finer grading vocabulary. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| The test's own standing | The posterior talar glide test is, in the authors' words, "recommended for ankle sprain assessment, but it has **limited scientific support**". They do not name which guideline recommends it. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| What counts as a real difference | **MDC 3.3°–4.7°.** Below that, two examiners disagreeing means nothing. `src: Nguyen 2026` |
| End feel | **`Convention`.** Universally taught, and no reachable source defines the categories or reports agreement between examiners on them. |
| Capsular pattern | **It does not hold.** In 200 patients the pattern Cyriax describes was "not present as a distinct pattern", and it "cannot be regarded as a valid test for the diagnosis of OA of the hip or knee". `src: Bijl 1998` |
| Which glide direction to choose | The concave-convex rule is the conventional answer, and it is **`Contested`** — see section 7. In practice, the restricted direction you assessed is a better guide than the rule that predicts it. |

## 6 · Force, amplitude, and where you stop

*Lede: the section the whole guide is built around. The vocabulary students memorise cannot be
traced; the vocabulary the guidelines use is defined, reproducible from its own description, and has
different outcomes attached to each level.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| The grading systems | **`No traceable source`** for origin, definition, or reliability. Not a gap in this guide's searching — a gap in the literature. |
| Say that precisely | Maitland I–IV and Kaltenborn I–III are taught universally and used as vocabulary — the ankle guideline names a "Maitland grade III" mobilization while summarising a trial. Nothing reachable defines them or shows two clinicians produce the same one. `src: Martin 2021` |
| What the guidelines use instead | **Force relative to tissue resistance.** High force = **stretching exceeding resistance**. Medium force = **stopping at the point of resistance**. Low force = **no resistance**. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Why that vocabulary is better | It is **defined by where the force stops relative to the tissue**, so it is reproducible from the description alone — and each level has a different measured outcome. `src: Koc 2025` |
| High force buys range | Significant ROM gains in all six hip directions against the low-force group, after **three sessions** with 1-week follow-up in 60 patients. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Low force buys pain relief | Largest effect on pain pressure threshold, **d = 2.0** — larger than either heavier level achieved. `src: Koc 2025` |
| So the dose is a choice, not a default | You cannot maximise both. Pick range or pain pressure threshold and set the force accordingly. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Medium force | Won neither outcome in that comparison — no significant ROM change, and not the largest pain effect. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Do not translate between scales | These three force levels **are not** Maitland or Kaltenborn grades and must not be presented as equivalents. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Name which scale any "grade" belongs to | "Grade III" in the hip literature is as likely to mean **Kellgren-Lawrence radiographic severity** as anything about mobilization. The 60 patients above had **Grade III hip OA** — a radiographic grade, not a treatment grade. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Dose, per the guideline that studied it | **1 to 3 times per week over 6–12 weeks** for mild-to-moderate hip OA. `src: Koc 2025` |
| The dose caveat, in the guideline's own words | Across 10 studies and 768 participants the common dosages were 10–30 minutes per session, 2–3 times weekly for 2–6 weeks — but **five of the ten studies were at high risk of bias**, and "recommending a specific manual therapy dosage for those with hip OA **could not be made**". `src: Koc 2025` |
| Amplitude and oscillation | **`No traceable source`.** No reachable study specifies or compares oscillation rate or amplitude. |
| Where in range to work | The only defined answer available is the force scale above, which *is* a statement about where in range you stop. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Position for the technique | The hip studies performed long-axis distraction **in open packed position** — a position the guideline names and does not define. `src: Koc 2025` |

## 7 · The techniques, by category

*Lede: five families, and the honest ranking is not by sophistication. It is by how much of each one
is actually measured.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Long-axis distraction | The only technique here with a **dose-response study**: three defined force levels, six directions of measured ROM change, and separate pain and function effects. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Sustained glides | Recommended within the guidelines' broader "joint mobilization" recommendations; **no reachable source isolates sustained from oscillatory glides** by outcome. `src: Koc 2025` `src: Martin 2021` |
| Oscillatory mobilization | Same position. Named in practice, not separated by evidence. `No traceable source` for any comparison against sustained technique. |
| Mobilization with movement | **Grade A** at the hip as part of the manual therapy recommendation, and **Grade A** at the ankle in chronic instability, weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing. `src: Koc 2025` `src: Martin 2021` |
| MWM, single session at the hip | Immediate pain **d = 1.9**, flexion ROM **d = 3.0**, IR ROM **d = 1.4**, TUG **d = 1.0**, chair stand **d = 1.7**, 40-m walk **d = 1.5**. Large, and immediate. `src: Koc 2025` |
| MWM at the shoulder | Four RCTs, 160 participants: pain, function, ROM and joint position sense all improved; quality of life did not. `src: Alqallaf 2025` |
| The design limit on that shoulder result | **Add-on, no sham.** Mulligan *plus* conventional therapy against conventional therapy alone cannot separate the technique from the extra contact time. `src: Alqallaf 2025` |
| Mulligan's proposed mechanism | **"Correcting positional faults"** is the model's claim. **No included trial measured a positional fault.** `src: Alqallaf 2025` |
| SNAGs at the lumbar spine | Seventeen RCTs, 716 participants, significant pooled effects on pain, disability and extension range — with I² of 83–93% and certainty rated low for two of the three. `src: Shager 2026` |
| Manipulation | High-velocity thrust. Distinct in execution, and **`No traceable source`** separates it from mobilization by outcome — the reviews that cover both pool them as "articular manual therapy". `src: Hadjisavvas 2026` `src: Jahromi 2026` |
| Cavitation — what the pop is | Consistent with tribonucleation, inferred from joint-space change rather than imaged directly. `src: Sillevis 2025` |
| Cavitation — what it is evidence of | **Not of a successful technique.** "All subjects **regardless of audible sound** increased in joint space" after MCP and MTP manipulation. The joint separated whether or not it made a noise. `src: Sillevis 2025` |
| Is the pop simply being heard? | No — after a T7 thrust, the audible group showed significant EEG change in frontal, parietal and occipital lobes **but not the temporal lobes**, where the auditory centres are. `src: Sillevis 2024` |
| How much weight those two studies carry | **They share a first author and method.** Treat them as one research line, not as independent replication. `src: Sillevis 2024` `src: Sillevis 2025` |
| The authors' own verdict on the pop | "The clinical usefulness of audible sounds remains questionable." `src: Sillevis 2025` |

## 8 · Matching technique to presentation

*Lede: the match is made on irritability and on what you are trying to change, not on the joint's
name.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Highly irritable, pain-dominant | Low force — no resistance. It produced the **largest** pain pressure threshold effect (d = 2.0), so this is not a compromise. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Stiff, low irritability, range-dominant | High force — stretching exceeding resistance. It is the only level that moved range. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Acute lateral ankle sprain | **Grade A**: lymphatic drainage, soft tissue and joint mobilization, and anterior-to-posterior talar mobilization **within pain-free movement**, alongside exercise. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Chronic ankle instability | **Grade A**: graded joint mobilizations, manipulations, and weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing MWM, to improve **weight-bearing** ankle dorsiflexion and dynamic balance **in the short term**. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Mild-to-moderate hip OA | **Grade A**: soft tissue and/or joint mobilization including high- and low-force long-axis distraction and MWM, to increase ROM, decrease pain and improve function. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Early after total knee arthroplasty | Evidence quality ***moderate***, but **recommendation strength *weak*** — the guideline downgraded it "due to limited evidence". Manual therapy *may* be used with exercise and/or devices to augment active-assistive exercise for ROM. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Midportion Achilles tendinopathy | **Grade F** — expert opinion. *May* manipulate or mobilise muscles, joints and connective tissues **where mobility deficits exist**. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| Meniscal and articular cartilage lesions | **No manual therapy recommendation at all.** Not a negative recommendation — an absence. `src: Logerstedt 2018` |
| Bony morphology that limits the technique | **Grade F**: modify procedures and force amplitude to the patient's bony hip morphology and tissue tolerance. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Suspected proprioceptive deficit | Immediate joint position sense improvements exist in individual trials, but the **only poolable result was null**, and no trial linked the change to pain or function. Do not promise this one. `src: Hadjisavvas 2026` |
| What never changes with presentation | Mobilization appears **with exercise** in every recommendation that carries a grade. `src: Koc 2025` `src: Martin 2021` `src: Bove 2026` |

## 9 · Position, stabilisation and hand placement

*Lede: almost everything in this section is convention. It is written down anyway, because a
convention you can name is safer than one you mistake for evidence.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Resting / open-packed position | **`No traceable source`.** Tabulated identically in every textbook; nothing reachable derives or tests the values. |
| Yet the guidelines use it | The hip distraction studies were performed **"in open packed position"** — named, used, undefined. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Close-packed position | **`No traceable source`.** Same four search-phrase variants, same absence. |
| Why the convention still earns its place | It gives two clinicians a shared starting position. That is worth having even unsourced — provided nobody claims it is evidence-based. `Convention` |
| Stabilise the proximal segment | Otherwise the force is shared between the joint and everything proximal to it, and the dose you intended is not the dose delivered. `Convention` |
| Hand placement | As close to the joint line as the technique permits — a longer lever multiplies force at the joint unpredictably. `Convention` |
| Force direction | Along the assessed restricted glide. `Convention` |
| The conventional direction rule | Concave-convex, and it is `Contested` — see the next row and section 7. `src: Scarvell 2019` |
| What contests it | In deep knee flexion the femur translated **posteriorly by 36 (3) mm** from 90° to 150°, finishing **23 (3) mm posterior** to the tibial centre, with external rotation from 8° to 16°. Flexion and translation were coupled at **r = 0.96**. `src: Scarvell 2019` |
| How far that goes | **It does not refute the rule.** The authors say the findings "invite review" of it, and they studied **kneeling in 25 healthy adults over 45** — no mobilization, no patients. `src: Scarvell 2019` |
| What to do with it | Assess the glide rather than deducing it from surface geometry. The rule predicts; the assessment measures. `Convention` |
| Communicating during the technique | Ask about pain quality and location during, not only after — "within pain-free movement" is a limit you can only enforce by asking. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Why mechanisms will not settle hand placement | A named-author commentary argues mechanisms of force-based manipulation interact with unmeasured variables and are "influenced by time, clinician, environments and a litany of other mediators". **A commentary, not data.** `src: Cook 2026` |

## 10 · What the evidence supports, by region

*Lede: one table, five guidelines, two grading scales. It is the most useful page in the guide and
the least comfortable.*

| Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|
| Hip OA | **Grade A** — soft tissue and/or joint mobilization, including high- and low-force long-axis distraction and MWM, to increase ROM, decrease pain and improve function in mild-to-moderate hip OA. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Hip OA, second recommendation | **Grade F** — *may* modify procedures and force amplitude to bony hip morphology and tissue tolerance/irritability. `src: Koc 2025` |
| Lateral ankle sprain, acute | **Grade A** — lymphatic drainage, active and passive soft tissue and joint mobilization, anterior-to-posterior talar mobilization within pain-free movement, alongside exercise. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Lateral ankle sprain, chronic instability | **Grade A** — graded joint mobilizations, manipulations, weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing MWM, for **weight-bearing** ankle dorsiflexion and dynamic balance in the short term. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Lateral ankle sprain, adjunct | **Grade B** — multiple interventions *may* supplement balance training, guided by patient values and clinician judgement. `src: Martin 2021` |
| Total knee arthroplasty, range | Evidence quality ***moderate***, **strength *weak* ♦♦◊◊**, "downgraded due to limited evidence" — *may* use manual therapy with exercise and/or devices to augment active-assistive exercise for ROM in the early postoperative period. Manual therapy itself rests on **1 high-quality study**. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Total knee arthroplasty, pain | Evidence quality ***moderate***, **strength *weak* ♦♦◊◊**, "downgraded due to low certainty of evidence" — *may* use manual therapy among several options (with TENS, Kinesio Taping, psychologically informed techniques) to decrease pain; **time frame not specified**. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Midportion Achilles tendinopathy | **Grade F** — *may* use manual therapy where mobility deficits exist. Expert opinion. `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| Knee meniscal and cartilage lesions | **None.** No manual therapy recommendation appears in the guideline. `src: Logerstedt 2018` |
| The headline finding | **The evidence is regional, not general.** "Manual therapy is evidence-based" is true in two of these five documents, weak in two, and **absent from** the fifth — absent, not rejected. `src: Koc 2025` `src: Martin 2021` `src: Chimenti 2024` `src: Bove 2026` `src: Logerstedt 2018` |
| Two scales, not one | Four guidelines grade A–F; the arthroplasty guideline reports **Evidence Quality *and* Recommendation Strength separately**. **A *moderate* is not a *B*** — and quoting its evidence quality alone hides that both of its manual therapy recommendations are rated ***weak***. `src: Bove 2026` |
| Where dose is specified | Always functionally — force amplitude relative to tissue tolerance, within pain-free movement, in the short term, early postoperative, where mobility deficits exist. **Not one guideline says "grade III".** `src: Koc 2025` `src: Martin 2021` `src: Bove 2026` `src: Chimenti 2024` |
| Duration of effect | The ankle guideline's own qualifier is **"in the short term"**, and the proprioception evidence is entirely **immediate post-intervention** with no follow-up. `src: Martin 2021` `src: Hadjisavvas 2026` |
| What happens centrally | Across 26 neuroimaging studies, most found post-intervention change in motor cortex excitability, functional connectivity and sensorimotor integration. `src: Jahromi 2026` |
| The sentence to remember | "Although many studies reported reductions in pain intensity, **none of them linked cortical changes directly to clinical improvements**." `src: Jahromi 2026` |
| Reassessment | Re-measure the marker you chose, in the same session, yourself. Immediate change is what the evidence describes; it is also all you can honestly claim. `src: Nguyen 2026` `src: Hadjisavvas 2026` |

## 11 · Drill

1. Name the three force levels the hip guideline defines, in terms of tissue resistance. Which one produced the largest effect on pain pressure threshold, and which on range?
2. A colleague says their patient's talocrural glide "improved from stiff to normal" after your assessment yesterday found it restricted. What is the inter-rater ICC for that test, and what is the smallest difference between you that would mean anything?
3. What is the origin of the Maitland grading system, and what is its published inter-rater reliability?
4. In how many of the five guidelines examined here does manual therapy reach Grade A? In how many does it appear at all?
5. Your patient's joint popped loudly during a technique. What did the pop tell you about whether the joint gapped?
6. A meta-analysis reports SMD −0.78 for pain with I² = 92%. What does the I² change about how you read the −0.78?
7. Why is "no catastrophic adverse events were reported" not the same as "catastrophic adverse events are rare"?
8. Which position were the hip long-axis distraction studies performed in, and what is the evidence base for that position?

1. High = stretching exceeding resistance, medium = stopping at the point of resistance, low = no resistance. Low force won pain pressure threshold (d = 2.0); high force won range and physical function. `src: Koc 2025`
2. Inter-rater ICC 0.64 for the posterior talar glide test; MDC 4.7°. A difference under about 4–5° between two examiners is noise. `src: Nguyen 2026`
3. Neither is traceable to any reachable source. That is the answer, and it is the guide's central finding.
4. Grade A in two (hip OA, lateral ankle sprain). It appears in four of five; the knee meniscal and cartilage guideline carries no manual therapy recommendation at all.
5. Nothing. Joint space increased in all subjects regardless of whether a sound occurred. `src: Sillevis 2025`
6. At I² = 92% the pooled estimate describes no single population; the authors themselves rate certainty moderate for pain and low for disability and mobility. `src: Shager 2026`
7. Because the review excluded the administrative database designs that detect rare events — the authors say so. Absence of detection is not evidence of rarity. `src: Daniels 2026`

9. 36 (3) mm of posterior femoral translation from 90° to 150° flexion, finishing 23 (3) mm posterior to the tibial centre, with external rotation 8° → 16°. The authors concluded the findings "invite review" of the rule — not that it is wrong. `src: Scarvell 2019`

11. It is an add-on design with no sham: Mulligan plus conventional care against conventional care alone, so the extra contact time is not controlled. `src: Alqallaf 2025`
