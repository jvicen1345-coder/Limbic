Knee Examination Playbook

A full knee screen in the order you would perform it, with every value marked for what it is worth. The knee's recurring lesson is that a test's accuracy depends on more than the test: the ligament figures come from patients pre-selected for surgery, McMurray's accuracy moves with time since injury and differs sharply between orthopaedic and primary care hands, and the lever sign's result shifts with the surface the leg rests on. Two graded guidelines cover meniscal and cartilage lesions and arthroplasty; ligament management has none, and section 10 says so rather than borrowing confidence it does not have. This is a study aid for examination preparation, not a clinical reference - check any value against current literature before it weights a decision about a patient.

How to read a value. Unmarked means traced to a source you can read for free, with the citation beside it. Convention nobody has tested it. Contested tested, and the studies disagree - quote the range.

The competency checklist

check-off saves in this browser

Two questions come before any special test: is this a knee problem at all, and does it need a radiograph before it needs a hand on it.

#ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
1 Screen above and below Clear the hip and lumbar spine before examining the knee. Do this first. Knee pain refers from both, and a knee examined for referred pain reads normal and reassuring. Convention
2 Decide about imaging early Apply the Ottawa Knee Rule before any special test. It is a rule-out. Pooled sensitivity 0.98 and −LR 0.12 across 18 studies and 6,702 patients. Kazemi 2023
3 Red flags History and observation for septic arthritis, fracture, extensor mechanism rupture, DVT, malignancy. An acutely hot, exquisitely painful knee with fever is not an examination problem. Convention
4 Classify from the history Traumatic or atraumatic, mechanical or inflammatory - and how long ago. Time since injury changes what the tests are worth. See section 07; this is not a formality at the knee. Rinonapoli 2025
5 Resting state Standing alignment, effusion, quadriceps bulk. The shortest section in the guide, because almost nothing here has been measured in a way a reader can check. Convention
6 What they can do alone Gait, active range, extension lag versus fixed flexion, squat, step-down, hop if appropriate. The lag-versus-contracture distinction is the most useful thing in that list. Convention
7 What you do to them Passive against active, end feel, patellar mobility, accessory glide. A springy block to extension is the classic displaced-meniscus end feel. Convention
8 What is short Quadriceps by Ely or prone knee bend, hamstrings, gastrocnemius. Gastrocnemius crosses the knee, so it belongs in a knee examination rather than only an ankle one. Convention
9 What is weak Quadriceps first, then hamstrings, then hip abductors. Expect inhibition rather than weakness at the knee, and retest after reducing pain and effusion before you believe a grade. Convention
10 Where is it Joint line, patellar borders and tendon, tibial tubercle, collateral origins. Palpate after the movement examination, not before - it tells you whether the tenderness explains anything. Convention

Meniscal and cartilage lesions, osteoarthritis, ACL injury and arthroplasty - and almost all of it grades treatment rather than examination. AAOS 2021

02

Numbers worth knowing cold

The knee has fewer trustworthy numbers than the shoulder and fewer reachable ones than the hip. What it does have is one excellent screening rule, decent ligament figures from an inflated population, and a meniscal finding almost nobody is taught.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Ottawa Knee Rule Five criteria; any one positive means image. Pooled sensitivity 0.98 (95% CI 0.96-0.99), specificity 0.43 (0.42-0.45), +LR 1.56 (1.39-1.75), −LR 0.12 (0.05-0.26), from 18 studies and 6,702 patients. Kazemi 2023
What that actually means Read the ratios, not the summary sentence. A rule-out and nothing else. A +LR of 1.56 barely moves probability and the HSROC AUC is 0.54, close to chance. The authors' own summary calls it "high diagnostic performance" - take that at face value and you will over-read a positive rule. Kazemi 2023
Lachman Knee at 20-30°, stabilise femur, translate tibia forward. Grade endpoint and excursion. Sn 87.6% · Sp 88.9% - the balanced ligament test. Nguyen 2025
Anterior drawer Knee at 90°, foot stabilised, draw the tibia forward. Sn 77.5% · Sp 86.1% - less sensitive than Lachman for the same structure. Nguyen 2025
Pivot shift Valgus and internal rotation from extension into flexion; feel for the reduction clunk. Sn 65.9% · Sp 94.4% - the specific one and the least sensitive. A positive is meaningful; a negative is not. Nguyen 2025
The caveat on all three Ask who was in the study. Those figures come from 165 patients already indicated for arthroscopy. High pretest probability and a narrow spectrum inflate accuracy; expect worse in a clinic. Nguyen 2025
Lever sign Fist under the proximal calf, press the distal thigh, watch the heel lift. Contested One study reports Sn 93.8% · Sp 94.4% and calls it useful Nguyen 2025; another, in a general traumatic-knee population against MRI, found only moderate interrater agreement (κ 0.529, 0.368-0.691) and at best +LR 3.02, −LR 0.45 - "limited clinical utility". Norris 2025
Why they disagree Compare the two populations. Norris says it outright: reviews naming the lever sign the best ACL rule-out rest on studies whose limitations "could bias the test outcome, potentially overestimating its clinical utility". One cohort was pre-selected for surgery; the other was not. Norris 2025
The lever sign's stranger finding Standardise your setup before you trust the result. Its outcome depends on the surface the leg rests on and where the examiner puts their fist - both significantly associated with the result. Effusion grade, force applied and pain reported were not. Norris 2025
McMurray in the first week Flexion with rotation, extend while palpating the joint line. Tested at 5-7 days. Sn 91% · Sp 55%. Sensitive and non-specific. Rinonapoli 2025
McMurray at a month The same test, repeated at 4-5 weeks. Sn 80% · Sp 79%, with a positive predictive value of 92%. Specific and less sensitive. Rinonapoli 2025
The point of those two rows Ask when the injury happened before you weight the test. A test's operating characteristics move with time since injury. A positive McMurray in week one means much less than the same positive a month later, and almost nobody is taught this. Rinonapoli 2025
But McMurray is also contested Ask who performed the test. Contested In primary care hands against MRI, in 292 patients plus 75 healthy controls, McMurray was Sn 0.58 (0.49-0.67) · Sp 0.56 (0.45-0.66) - far below the orthopaedic figures above. Logerstedt 2018
Which is right? Both, for their own setting. Rinonapoli used orthopaedic surgeons in a trauma cohort with roughly 74% prevalence; Blyth used primary care clinicians in a mixed population including healthy controls. Same manoeuvre, different examiners, different people - and the gap between them is wider than the gap between any two tests. Rinonapoli 2025 Logerstedt 2018
What to take from that Weight the examiner and the population, not only the test. Who performs a test, and on whom, moves its accuracy more than which test you chose. That is the single most useful thing in this guide.
Best meniscal combination Run McMurray and Apley together. McMurray with Apley gave the best accuracy and minimised false positives, in 255 patients with 188 arthroscopically confirmed tears. Rinonapoli 2025
Thessaly Weight-bearing rotation at 20° of flexion. Sn 0.66 (0.57-0.74) · Sp 0.39 (0.29-0.50), OR 1.24 (0.71-2.18) against MRI in primary care hands. Reliability is fair, κ 0.54, from one moderate-quality study. Logerstedt 2018
Apley Prone, knee at 90°, compression with rotation. Sn 0.53 (0.44-0.62) · Sp 0.53 (0.42-0.63), OR 1.24 (0.73-2.12). Barely better than a coin in either direction. Logerstedt 2018
Joint line tenderness Palpate the tibiofemoral line with the knee flexed. Sn 0.77 (0.68-0.84) · Sp 0.26 (0.18-0.36), OR 1.16 (0.63-2.13) - sensitive, almost non-specific. Reliability poor, κ ≤ 0.38. Logerstedt 2018
Joint line tenderness differs by sex Read the figure for the patient in front of you. In men Sn 0.50-0.58, Sp 0.74-1.00, accuracy 0.63-0.86. In women Sn 0.40-0.49, Sp 0.71-0.98. Logerstedt 2018
The odds ratios are the tell Check whether the interval includes 1. Three of those four include 1 - Thessaly, Apley and joint line tenderness. Only McMurray's excludes it, and only just, at 1.04 to 3.09. Logerstedt 2018
Ege and hyper-flexion Named but not quantified. No individual figures. no traceable source Noted only as more influenced by anterior knee pain. Rinonapoli 2025
The honest frame for the section Say it to the patient too. The authors' conclusion: no single clinical test was sufficiently reliable for independent diagnosis, reinforcing the need for imaging confirmation. Rinonapoli 2025
Normal knee ROM Goniometry, compared to the other knee. Convention. Convention No free normative source. no traceable source The tables everyone quotes are paywalled at the table.
What is traceable about that Compare a textbook figure to a measured population. Measured against a national probability sample, normative hip and knee values differed from textbook estimates by as much as 18°, and differences between the youngest and oldest age groups were only 3 to 5°. Roach 1991
Q angle ASIS to patellar centre to tibial tubercle. Convention Published values are population-specific - separate figures for different national cohorts, for sedentary adults versus athletes - with no universal norm and no reliability study reachable. no traceable source The familiar 15° is not quoted here.
Knee OA bedside criteria Ask whether a criteria set exists. It does not, reachably. no traceable source The knee has no equivalent to the hip's Grade A criteria set.
Patellofemoral test accuracy Ask before you rely on one. Not established. no traceable source Searched twice; nothing gives diagnostic accuracy for a bedside patellofemoral test.
Effusion test reliability Sweep and ballottement. Not established. no traceable source The reachable literature is MRI volumetry, not the hand tests students are examined on.
Quadriceps limb symmetry index Used as a return-to-sport gate. Convention Cohorts apply a symmetry cutoff; none reachable here establishes one. no traceable source A threshold whose origin you cannot reach is a convention, not a measurement.

Posture and resting alignment

The shortest section in the guide, because almost nothing here has been measured in a way a reader can check. Saying that is more useful than filling it with thresholds nobody established.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Standing alignment Observe varus, valgus and recurvatum, weight-bearing, from front and side. Convention No threshold that makes an alignment abnormal is traceable here. no traceable source
Effusion - how to test Sweep or stroke test for small effusions; ballottement for large ones. Grade and compare sides. Technique is uncontroversial. The grading is yours to repeat rather than to cite. Convention
Effusion - how much to trust it Ask what the reliability is. No reliability figure no traceable source - searched twice. But the modified stroke test is named as an essential physical impairment measure to document at baseline and follow-up, so use that one and record it. Logerstedt 2018
Quadriceps bulk Compare girth and vastus medialis definition side to side. Atrophy is a chronicity marker, not a diagnosis. Convention
04

Active range and what they can do alone

One distinction in this section does more work than the rest of it combined: whether a knee that will not straighten is a quadriceps problem or a joint problem.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Active flexion and extension Compare sides before comparing to a book. The norms are convention Convention and the other knee is the better reference.
Extension lag versus fixed flexion Take it passively, then actively, and compare. Passive exceeds active = an extension lag, a quadriceps problem. Both equally limited = a contracture, a joint problem. Convention Two different treatments follow.
Gait Observe stance-phase control, quadriceps avoidance and thrust. Convention No free accuracy figures for gait observation at the knee. no traceable source
Squat and step-down Depth, symmetry, frontal-plane control, and whether the symptom reproduces. Convention Useful as a reassessment marker because it repeats within a session.
Hop testing and limb symmetry Used as a return-to-sport gate. ContestedHop testing "may be considered as one factor to determine return to sport" - Strength of Option Limited, quality of evidence low AAOS 2022. No symmetry cutoff is established, and the guideline says why rather than leaving it silent: "the optimal timing of functional testing and return to sport is unclear based on the literature". The studies behind it tested at 6 months, at a mean of 8.1 months, at 9 months and at 12 months, and disagree about whether waiting longer lowers graft failure. Use it as one input among several, never as the gate.
05

Passive range, end feel and length

Passive testing at the knee is mostly about separating the joint from the muscle. The end feels carry the information, and none of them have reachable reliability figures.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Passive against active Take each direction passively and compare with the active range. Passive exceeding active points to inhibition or weakness; both equally limited points to the joint. Convention
End feel Note what stops the movement: capsular, bony, springy, empty, guarding. A springy block to extension is the classic displaced-meniscus finding. Convention Taught universally; no reachable reliability at the knee. no traceable source
Patellar mobility and tilt Glide medially and laterally in slight flexion; assess tilt. Convention No traceable threshold for what counts as hyper- or hypomobile. no traceable source
Accessory motion Tibiofemoral anterior and posterior glide, patellofemoral glide. Grade resistance, compare sides. Convention Graded resistance is universally taught; no reachable reliability figure at the knee.
Quadriceps length Prone knee bend, or Ely's test. Watch for a compensatory pelvic rise. The pelvic rise is the finding students miss; it means you have run out of quadriceps. Convention
Hamstring length Passive straight-leg raise, or 90/90 knee extension. Convention No threshold or reliability figure reachable at the knee. no traceable source
Gastrocnemius Ankle dorsiflexion with the knee straight, then with it bent. The difference between the two is the gastrocnemius contribution, because it crosses the knee. Convention
06

Strength

At the knee the important question is usually not how weak the quadriceps is, but whether it is weak at all as opposed to inhibited.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Quadriceps Test through range and against resistance; prefer a dynamometer so the number repeats. Record a number you can compare next visit rather than a grade you cannot. Convention
Weakness or inhibition? Retest after reducing pain and effusion. A grade that improves at once was inhibition. Convention The rest of the knee plan depends on which one it is.
Why it matters here specifically Look at what the guideline recommends for it. The one graded guideline available recommends daily NMES to the quadriceps at the highest tolerable intensity after arthroplasty, at moderate evidence quality - an intervention aimed squarely at inhibition rather than at load. Bove 2026
Hamstrings Prone knee flexion against resistance. Note the hamstring-to-quadriceps relationship rather than either in isolation. Convention
Hip abductors Test them, every time. The knee follows the hip in the frontal plane, and a valgus collapse on step-down is often a hip finding. Convention
Strength norms Ask what you are comparing against. None reachable. no traceable source Use side-to-side difference and serial change.
07

Special tests

Two things make this section different from the shoulder's. The ligament figures come from a population pre-selected for surgery, and the meniscal figures move depending on when you examine. Both change how the numbers should be used, not merely how confidently.

08

Syndromes and what you call it

The knee's syndromes are easier to name than to confirm. Two of the five below have no reachable bedside criteria at all, and saying that is more useful than a confident label.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
ACL rupture Mechanism, immediate effusion, instability on the history. Screened with Lachman, confirmed by neither the examination nor the lever sign alone. Nguyen 2025 Norris 2025
Meniscal tear Mechanical symptoms, joint line pain, effusion. McMurray with Apley is the best available combination, read against time since injury. Rinonapoli 2025
Patellofemoral pain Anterior knee pain with loading, stairs, prolonged sitting. A diagnosis of pattern and exclusion here, because no bedside test has reachable accuracy. no traceable source
Knee osteoarthritis Age, load-related pain, stiffness, crepitus. No reachable bedside criteria set no traceable source - unlike the hip, there is no Grade A rule to apply. Checked against the OA guideline rather than assumed: its stated purpose is "to evaluate the current best evidence associated with treatment", so it grades what to do about knee OA and says nothing about how to recognise it. Naming it stays clinical; managing it is graded in detail - see section 10. AAOS 2021
After arthroplasty Post-surgical timeline and precautions. The one syndrome here with graded evidence behind its management - see section 10. Bove 2026
Movement diagnosis Name the movement fault and its direction under load. State what the knee does under load that reproduces the symptom, not only the tissue you suspect. Convention
Relative stiffness Look above and below before treating the knee. A knee that will not flex borrows from the hip and the ankle. Convention
09

Palpation

Palpation comes late deliberately. Palpating first tells you where a patient is tender; palpating after the movement examination tells you whether that tenderness explains anything.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Joint line Knee flexed, palpate the tibiofemoral line medially and laterally. Tenderness raises meniscal suspicion, but its accuracy is not reported. no traceable source Rinonapoli 2025
Patella Borders, facets, tendon, tibial tubercle. Convention Localises rather than grades.
Collaterals Origins and insertions of the medial and lateral collateral ligaments. Convention Tenderness here narrows the differential but confirms nothing on its own.
What palpation cannot do Know its ceiling before relying on it. It cannot distinguish a tear from degeneration, or confirm any of the syndromes in section 08. Use it to localise. Convention
10

Mobilisation, exercise and dose

Four graded guidelines reach this section, not one. The knee's problem is not a shortage of graded management evidence - it is that the grades cover treatment while the examination that gets you there is mostly convention. Read the grade and the harm statement together: a grade says how much evidence exists, not how much the intervention is worth.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Where grades apply, and where they do not Ask which guideline covers what you are treating. Four are reachable and graded - meniscal and articular cartilage lesions Logerstedt 2018, non-arthroplasty knee osteoarthritis AAOS 2021, anterior cruciate ligament injuries AAOS 2022 and total knee arthroplasty Bove 2026. The one that is not reachable is the JOSPT ligament revision, whose free abstract is a purpose statement carrying no recommendation, grade, dose or threshold Logerstedt 2017 - but the AAOS ligament guideline covers that ground and is free in full.
Meniscal and cartilage - B Supervised and progressed, not handed over as a sheet. Progressive range-of-motion exercise, strength training of the knee and hip muscles, and neuromuscular training, for meniscus tears, cartilage lesions, and after surgery for either. Logerstedt 2018
Meniscal - B Electrical stimulation after meniscus procedures. Neuromuscular stimulation and re-education to increase quadriceps strength, functional performance and knee function. Logerstedt 2018
After meniscectomy - B In-clinic plus home, both supervised. Supervised in-clinic exercise and a progressed home programme with education for independent performance (2018). The strength and functional exercise for quadriceps and hamstring strength and endurance is a 2010 recommendation retained in 2018. Logerstedt 2018
Motion after surgery - B Early, progressive, both kinds. Early progressive active and passive knee motion after meniscal or articular cartilage surgery. Logerstedt 2018
Weight bearing - B After cartilage implantation. Stepwise progression to full weight bearing by 6 to 8 weeks after matrix-supported autologous chondrocyte implantation. Logerstedt 2018
Weight bearing after meniscal repair - C Note the weaker grade. Early progressive weight bearing may be considered - a C against the B above, so the confidence is not the same. Logerstedt 2018
Return to activity - C and E Two different strengths of advice. Early progressive return may be used after meniscal repair (C); return may need delaying depending on the type of articular cartilage surgery (E, expert opinion). Logerstedt 2018
Risk factors - C Ask about age and how long ago. Age and greater time from injury are predisposing factors for meniscal injury. A 2010 recommendation retained in the 2018 revision - still current guidance, but the evidence behind it is the older set. Logerstedt 2018
Outcome measures - B Pick from the named set. A validated patient-reported outcome measure, a general health questionnaire and a validated activity scale. IKDC 2000 and KOOS are the named self-report measures. Also a 2010 recommendation retained in 2018. Logerstedt 2018
Essential physical measures Document these at baseline and follow-up. Modified stroke test for effusion, knee active ROM, maximum voluntary isometric or isokinetic quadriceps strength, and joint line tenderness. Logerstedt 2018
Read the harms, not only the grades Look up the harm statement beside each recommendation. The guideline states benefits, risks, harms and costs for every recommendation, and that is where the clinical judgement lives. A grade tells you how much evidence exists; the harm statement tells you what it costs to be wrong. Bove 2026
Strength training - high Progressive strength training and exercise, taught to the patient. Begin in the early postacute period, to improve function, strength and ROM. Bove 2026
Cryotherapy and positioning - high Prescribe cryotherapy and teach limb positioning. Elevate the surgical limb with 30 to 90° of knee flexion in the early postoperative period. Bove 2026
Digital health tools - high Offer them alongside or instead of in-clinic care. Recommended either in addition to in-clinic care or as an alternative to it. Bove 2026
Continuous passive motion - high, against Do not use it after primary uncomplicated arthroplasty. The reasoning is stated: no consistent long-term improvement in function, ROM or quality of life, and it may increase patient discomfort. Bove 2026
NMES - moderate Electrodes to quadriceps, from the early postoperative period. Apply at least daily, at the highest tolerable intensity, for quadriceps strength, gait and performance outcomes. Bove 2026
Range of motion exercise - moderate Passive, active-assistive and active. Include all three to optimise recovery and functional outcomes of the affected knee. Bove 2026
Manual therapy - moderate May augment active-assistive exercise for range, early. With the guideline's own caveat: effectiveness may vary depending on therapist skill and the techniques used. Bove 2026
Pain - moderate Two separate recommendations. Should teach and encourage cryotherapy for early postoperative pain. May use TENS (acute and subacute), Kinesio taping (acute), manual therapy and psychologically informed techniques - the last two with no time frame specified. Bove 2026
Activity - moderate Encourage early activity and progress it. Progress against safety, functional tolerance, physiological response and collaborative goal setting. Bove 2026
Delivery - moderate Group or individual sessions. Both are acceptable after arthroplasty. Bove 2026
Bracing and splinting - moderate, against Do not use routinely to gain early range. It may prolong bedrest and limit natural movement patterns, restricting early mobility and gait training. But the guideline also records that bracing into extension may have short-term effects on pain and function - so this is against routine use, not a prohibition. Bove 2026
Swelling - moderate, hedged Kinesio taping may be considered. To reduce swelling after uncomplicated arthroplasty - "however, evidence is mixed regarding its benefit", the guideline's own words, and the reason this is a may rather than a should. Bove 2026
Swelling - low, against Manual lymphatic drainage, compression dressings, CPM. Do not use these routinely to reduce postoperative swelling; they have not been proven effective. Bove 2026
What is striking about that list Count the negative recommendations. The strongest evidence in it is negative. The high-quality recommendation is what to stop doing, and the moderate and low grades add bracing, splinting, lymphatic drainage and compression to the same list. Bove 2026
Knee osteoarthritis - the graded set this guide previously said did not exist
Exercise - Strong Any of the three. The comparison that carries the grade is against no exercise, not between kinds. "Supervised exercise, unsupervised exercise, and/or aquatic exercise are recommended over no exercise to improve pain and function for treatment of knee osteoarthritis." AAOS 2021 Note what is not graded: which of the three, or at what dose.
Self-management and education - Strong Two separate recommendations, both Strong, both cheap. Self-management programmes are recommended "to improve pain and function"; patient education programmes "to improve pain" AAOS 2021. They carry the same grade as the exercise recommendation and are the two most often left out of a plan.
Neuromuscular training - Moderate Balance, agility and coordination, added to traditional exercise rather than instead of it. "could be used to improve performance-based function and walking speed" - Moderate, downgraded AAOS 2021. The outcome it is graded for is function and speed, not pain.
Weight loss - Moderate Sustained, in overweight and obese patients. Recommended to improve pain and function - Moderate, downgraded AAOS 2021. "Sustained" is doing the work in that sentence.
Manual therapy - Limited In addition to an exercise programme, never as the programme. "may be used to improve pain and function" - Limited, downgraded AAOS 2021. Two grades below the exercise it is added to, which is the ranking to keep in mind when a session runs short.
What is recommended against - Strong Read the negatives; they are where a grade changes practice fastest. Lateral wedge insoles are "not recommended" (Strong). Oral narcotics including tramadol "result in a significant increase of adverse events and are not effective" (Strong). Arthroscopy with lavage and/or debridement for a primary diagnosis of knee OA is "not recommended" (Moderate). AAOS 2021
Anterior cruciate ligament - graded, and mostly surgical
History and physical - Strong The examination itself is the graded recommendation. "A relevant history should be obtained, and a focused musculoskeletal exam of the lower extremities should be performed when assessing for an ACL injury" - Strong, on high-quality evidence AAOS 2022. The whole lower extremity, not the knee alone.
Injury prevention - Moderate Programmes for athletes in high-risk sports. "Training programs designed to prevent injury can be used to reduce the risk of primary ACL injuries in athletes participating in high-risk sports" - Moderate, on high-quality evidence AAOS 2022. This is the one place at the knee where prevention carries a grade.
Functional bracing - Limited, on High evidence Read the grade and the evidence quality separately. "Functional knee braces are not recommended for routine use in patients who have received isolated primary ACL reconstruction, as they confer no clinical benefit" - quality of evidence High, strength Limited (downgraded) AAOS 2022. High evidence with a downgraded strength is not a contradiction: the evidence is solid and something in the decision framework - values, cost, applicability - pulled the recommendation down. Worth knowing before you quote a grade as if it were a measurement.
The reassessment marker Name it before you treat. Extension lag, effusion grade, or a timed functional measure - whichever the impairment actually was. Convention
11

Rapid drill

Two ways to rehearse. The cases work the whole chain from presentation to reassessment marker; the single facts test one value at a time, and most of them test whether you know what a value is worth as well as what it is.

What to practice

Built from the cells you marked missed while running recall. Nothing here is pre-filled - if this is empty, go and quiz yourself first. The practice method is chosen by what the item is, not by what kind of learner you are: a number, a pair of hands and a decision are three different jobs and they need three different kinds of rehearsal.

Why this is not a learning-styles quiz. Sorting students into visual, auditory or kinesthetic learners and matching teaching to the label is one of the most durable ideas in education that the evidence does not support - the studies that test it properly find no benefit from the match Pashler 2008. What does hold up is the method fitting the material: retrieving a fact from memory beats re-reading it, and spacing the attempts beats massing them Dunlosky 2013. So the plan below sorts your misses by the kind of thing they are.

Cases - work the whole chain

Open a case, then work down the prompts - answer each out loud and click to check. Answers are hidden every time a case opens, so nothing is spoiled by looking.

Footballer, 22. Twisting injury four days ago, swelled within an hour, felt a pop. Lachman feels soft with a vague endpoint. McMurray reproduces medial joint line pain.
Ottawa first
Apply it before anything else. Sensitivity 0.98 with a −LR of 0.12 means a negative rule genuinely lowers fracture probability; a positive one barely raises it, since +LR is 1.56 and the AUC is 0.54 Kazemi 2023.
What Lachman buys you
Sn 87.6%, Sp 88.9% - the balanced ligament test Nguyen 2025. Discount it slightly: those figures come from patients already indicated for arthroscopy.
The McMurray trap
At four days you are in the T1 window, where McMurray is Sn 91% but Sp only 55% Rinonapoli 2025. A positive here is weak evidence. The same positive at four to five weeks, with specificity up to 79% and PPV 92%, would mean considerably more.
Would the lever sign settle it?
No. Contested One study gives Sn 93.8%/Sp 94.4% Nguyen 2025, another only moderate agreement and +LR 3.02 in a general population Norris 2025 - and its result shifts with the surface and your fist position.
What to do
Do not chase a diagnosis from tests this early. Settle irritability, protect, restore extension, and re-examine at three to four weeks when the meniscal tests are worth more.
Teacher, 61. Six weeks after an uncomplicated total knee replacement. Active extension lacks 12°; passive extension is full. Quadriceps looks wasted. She has been given a brace to wear at night and a CPM machine to hire.
Lag or contracture?
Passive exceeds active, so this is an extension lag - a quadriceps problem, not a joint one. Stretching the joint will not fix it.
The CPM
Recommended against, at high evidence quality: no consistent long-term improvement in function, ROM or quality of life, and it may increase discomfort Bove 2026.
The brace
Also against for routine early use, at moderate quality - it may prolong bedrest and restrict early mobility and gait training. But note the guideline records a possible short-term benefit of bracing into extension, so this is against routine use rather than a prohibition Bove 2026.
What to add instead
NMES to quadriceps at least daily at the highest tolerable intensity, and progressive strength training from the early postacute period, at high quality Bove 2026. The lag is an inhibition problem and NMES targets exactly that.
The teaching point
The strongest evidence in this guideline is negative - the high-quality recommendation is what to stop doing.
Runner, 29. Anterior knee pain on stairs and after sitting. No trauma, no effusion, no giving way. She asks which test will confirm patellofemoral pain.
The honest answer
None of them. No bedside patellofemoral test has reachable diagnostic accuracy no traceable source, searched twice. It is a diagnosis of pattern and exclusion.
So what are the tests for?
Reproducing and localising the symptom, and giving you something to re-measure - not confirming a label.
Where to look instead
Above and below. The knee follows the hip in the frontal plane, so test hip abductors and watch a step-down for valgus collapse.
Do not quote a Q angle
Convention Published values are population-specific, with no universal norm and no reliability study reachable no traceable source. The familiar 15° is not a measurement you can defend.
The marker
Pick one before treating - step-down repetitions to symptom, or stair descent - and re-measure it rather than re-asking about pain.

Single facts

The Ottawa Knee Rule has a sensitivity of 0.98. What is its specificity, and what does that make it good for?
Specificity 0.43. It is a rule-out and nothing else: +LR 1.56 barely moves probability, the HSROC AUC is 0.54, and the whole value sits in the −LR of 0.12 Kazemi 2023.
That paper's conclusion calls it "high diagnostic performance". Is it?
In one direction only. An AUC of 0.54 is close to chance discrimination. Take the phrase at face value and you will over-read a positive rule - read the ratios instead Kazemi 2023.
Give sensitivity and specificity for Lachman, anterior drawer and pivot shift. Which is the rule-in test?
Lachman 87.6/88.9, anterior drawer 77.5/86.1, pivot shift 65.9/94.4 Nguyen 2025. Pivot shift is the rule-in test - most specific, least sensitive.
Why should you discount all three slightly?
Because all 165 patients were already indicated for arthroscopy. High pretest probability and a narrow spectrum inflate accuracy; expect worse in clinic Nguyen 2025.
Two studies disagree about the lever sign. Give both results and the reason for the gap.
Contested Sn 93.8%/Sp 94.4%, "useful" Nguyen 2025, against moderate interrater agreement (κ 0.529) and at best +LR 3.02/−LR 0.45, "limited clinical utility" Norris 2025. The second says why: reviews favouring the test rest on studies whose limitations could overestimate its utility. One cohort was pre-selected for surgery, the other was not.
Two things change the lever sign's result that have nothing to do with the knee. What are they?
The surface the leg rests on and where the examiner puts their fist - both significantly associated with the outcome. Effusion grade, force applied and pain reported were not Norris 2025.
McMurray at one week versus one month - give both sensitivity and specificity.
5-7 days: Sn 91%, Sp 55%. 4-5 weeks: Sn 80%, Sp 79%, with PPV 92% Rinonapoli 2025.
Why does that matter for an acute knee?
Because a test's operating characteristics move with time since injury. A positive McMurray in week one is weak evidence; the same positive a month later is much stronger. Re-examining later is a diagnostic act, not a delay Rinonapoli 2025.
Which two meniscal tests combine best, and what does the combination buy you?
McMurray with Apley - best accuracy, minimising false positives, across 255 patients with 188 confirmed tears Rinonapoli 2025.
What is Thessaly's sensitivity?
Unknown here. no traceable source It was tested and compared but no accuracy figure appears in the free text; it was noted as more influenced by anterior knee pain Rinonapoli 2025. Any Thessaly figure you have seen quoted did not come from this study.
Extension lag or fixed flexion contracture - how do you tell, and what does each implicate?
Compare passive with active. Passive exceeds active = lag, a quadriceps problem. Both equally limited = contracture, a joint problem. Two different treatments follow, and it is the most useful distinction in the active section.
What is the normal knee range of motion?
There is no figure here you can defend. Convention no traceable source What is traceable: measured against a national probability sample, normative hip and knee values differed from textbook estimates by as much as 18° Roach 1991. Compare to the other knee instead.
What is a normal Q angle?
Not a defensible number. Convention Published values are population-specific, with no universal norm and no reliability study reachable no traceable source.
Which intervention after knee arthroplasty has high-quality evidence against it, and why?
Continuous passive motion. No consistent long-term improvement in function, ROM or quality of life, and it may increase patient discomfort Bove 2026.
What is the NMES recommendation after arthroplasty, and at what intensity?
Apply to the quadriceps at least daily, from the early postoperative period, at the highest tolerable intensity, for strength, gait and performance outcomes. Moderate evidence quality Bove 2026.
"Do not brace after knee replacement." Is that what the guideline says?
Not quite. It advises against routine early bracing to gain ROM, because it may prolong bedrest and restrict early mobility and gait training - but it also records that bracing into extension may have short-term effects on pain and function Bove 2026. Against routine use is not a prohibition.
Where does the limb symmetry threshold come from?
Nowhere reachable. Convention no traceable source Cohorts apply a cutoff; none found here establishes one. A threshold whose origin you cannot reach is a convention, not a measurement.
McMurray is reported at Sn 91% in one study and Sn 0.58 in another. Which is right?
Both, for their own setting. Rinonapoli used orthopaedic surgeons in a trauma cohort with roughly 74% prevalence Rinonapoli 2025; Blyth used primary care clinicians in a mixed population including healthy controls Logerstedt 2018. Same manoeuvre, different examiners, different people - and the gap between them is wider than the gap between any two tests.
Give Thessaly's sensitivity and specificity, and say what its odds ratio tells you.
Sn 0.66, Sp 0.39, OR 1.24 with a confidence interval of 0.71 to 2.18 - it includes 1, so the test barely discriminates. Its reliability is nonetheless the best of the three at κ 0.54, against κ ≤ 0.38 for McMurray and joint line tenderness Logerstedt 2018.
Joint line tenderness has a sensitivity of 0.77. Why is that less useful than it sounds?
Because its specificity is 0.26 and its odds ratio interval includes 1 Logerstedt 2018. It is sensitive and almost non-specific: a negative is somewhat informative, a positive is close to noise. It also performs differently by sex - in men Sp 0.74 to 1.00, in women Sn only 0.40 to 0.49.
Which knee problem has no graded guideline at all?
Ligament management. Meniscal and cartilage lesions have one Logerstedt 2018 and arthroplasty has one Bove 2026, but the knee ligament sprain guideline's free abstract is a purpose statement carrying no recommendation, grade, dose or threshold Logerstedt 2017.

References

Eight sources, each cited at least once above, each read before it was quoted - and seven of the eight are free full text. Where a value is marked convention, or flagged as having no traceable source, it appears in no entry below, because nothing supports it. Each note records what the paper cannot support as well as what it can, which for this guide is where most of the work is: three of these papers are quoted mainly for their limits.

Fracture screening

  1. Kazemi SM, Khorram R, Fayyazishishavan E, Amani-Beni R, Haririan Y, Hosseini Khameneh SM, et al Diagnostic accuracy of Ottawa Knee Rule for diagnosis of fracture in patients with knee trauma: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Arch Acad Emerg Med 2023;11(1):e3018 studies, 6,702 patients, QUADAS-2 assessed. Pooled sensitivity 0.98 and specificity 0.43, +LR 1.56, −LR 0.12, HSROC AUC 0.54. Note the gap between those numbers and the abstract's own summary phrase, "high diagnostic performance": the rule discriminates barely better than chance in the positive direction and earns its place purely as a rule-out.

Ligament tests

  1. Nguyen LT, Truong SV, Nguyen UTP Value of clinical tests in diagnosing anterior cruciate ligament tears: what is new?. Rev Bras Ortop 2025;60(5):s00451813005165 knee-injury patients compared against arthroscopy. Source for anterior drawer, Lachman and pivot shift sensitivity and specificity. Every patient was already indicated for arthroscopy, so pretest probability is high and the spectrum narrow - expect lower accuracy in a general clinic. Its lever sign figures should not be quoted without Norris 2025 beside them.
  2. Norris R, Price A, Byrne J, Pulford S, van Melick N, Maddox TW, Boswell W, Kerin C, Oldershaw RA The lever sign test demonstrates limited clinical utility for diagnosing full-thickness anterior cruciate ligament tears after a traumatic knee injury. Orthop J Sports Med 2025;13(5):23259671251334775101 patients after traumatic knee injury, MRI reference, tested beyond 3 weeks. Only moderate interrater agreement (κ 0.529) and at best +LR 3.02 / −LR 0.45. Names the reason earlier reviews overstate the test: their included studies could bias the outcome. Also the source for the finding that the result shifts with the surface used and the examiner's fist position, while effusion, force and pain do not affect it.

Meniscal tests

  1. Rinonapoli G, Lucchetta L, Ancillai G, Manfreda F, Ceccarini P, Caraffa A Clinical reliability of 6 meniscal tests: a diagnostic accuracy study of 255 patients. Acta Orthop 2025;96:452-458255 patients aged 20-45, two blinded surgeons, tested at 5-7 days and again at 4-5 weeks, 188 arthroscopically confirmed tears. Source for McMurray's sensitivity falling from 91% to 80% while specificity rises from 55% to 79%, and for McMurray with Apley as the best combination. Carries no individual figures for Apley, Thessaly, Ege, joint line tenderness or hyper-flexion - those tests are compared but not quantified in the free text.

Range of motion

  1. Roach KE, Miles TP Normal hip and knee active range of motion: the relationship to age. Phys Ther 1991;71(9):656-665NHANES I national probability sample, 1,892 subjects. Covers hip and knee, so it is a knee source in its own right. Carries the finding that measured values differ from textbook estimates by as much as 18° and that age-group differences are only 3 to 5°. Does not carry per-motion normative figures; those tables are paywalled, which is why every ROM number here is marked convention.
