Physical therapy · the lateral ankle screen, in order Version 1.0 · September 2026

Ankle Examination Playbook

A full lateral ankle screen in the order you would perform it, with every value marked for what it is worth. This is the best-evidenced joint in the series - two freely readable graded guidelines carry 34 current recommendations between them - and it is also the one where the honest prognosis is worst: 40% of people who seek care for a first sprain develop chronic instability, and full recovery "may take months or years to obtain, and cannot be expected in all patients". The best evidence here is for prevention, which is why prevention is the argument rather than an afterthought. And the special tests are recommended without any published accuracy at all, which the guide says plainly rather than filling in. This is a study aid for examination preparation, not a clinical reference - check any value against current literature before it weights a decision about a patient.

How to read a value. Unmarked means traced to a source you can read for free, with the citation beside it. Convention nobody has tested it. Contested tested, and the studies disagree - quote the range.

The competency checklist

One decision comes before everything else - whether this ankle needs a radiograph. Get it early, and apply the rule entire.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Apply the Ottawa Ankle Rules first Malleolar zones, navicular, base of the fifth metatarsal, and ability to bear weight for four steps. And apply them in their entirety. Omitting malleolar tenderness and relying on weight bearing alone dropped sensitivity to 88%. Martin 2021
What the rules are for Deciding about imaging, not finding fractures. Ruling out. Pooled sensitivity 0.92, specificity 0.35, −LR 0.13; a negative leaves roughly a 1% post-test probability of fracture. Sharifi Razavi 2026

Resting state Swelling and where it sits, ecchymosis, point tenderness, standing posture. Swelling is a Grade A documented measure, at baseline and two or more times. Martin 2021
What they can do alone Gait and weight bearing, active range, lunge, balance, hopping. The guideline names the instruments: weight-bearing lunge, eyes-closed single-limb balance, Star Excursion Balance Test, timed single-limb hopping. Martin 2021
What you do to them Passive range, talar translation, talar inversion, accessory glide. Talar translation and talar inversion are Grade A documented measures, not optional extras. Martin 2021

02

Numbers worth knowing cold

The ankle has the strongest evidence in this series and still no published accuracy for its special tests. That contrast is the section - and so is the prognosis, which is worse than most people assume.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Ottawa Ankle Rules, pooled Meta-analysis across studies. Sn 0.92 (95% CI 0.91-0.93), Sp 0.35 (0.34-0.36), +LR 1.76 (1.46-2.13), −LR 0.13 (0.09-0.19), DOR 16.21 (10.15-25.89). Sharifi Razavi 2026
What that buys you Read the two directions separately. A negative drops fracture probability to about 1%, which is why it safely reduces radiography. A positive barely moves it. Rule-out, not rule-in. Sharifi Razavi 2026
The specificity is not one number Ask across how many studies. Reported across studies as 7.8% to 68% against a sensitivity of 92%-100% - a fourteen-fold spread. A single pooled specificity hides how much it varies by setting. Martin 2021
Apply them entire Do not shortcut to the weight-bearing question. Dropping malleolar tenderness and using only inability to bear weight and take four steps gave sensitivity 88% - worse than the whole rule. Martin 2021
In children Compare the two rules. The low-risk ankle rules' sensitivity of 85.7% is inferior to the Ottawa rules' 100% in that population. Martin 2021
What implementing them achieves Beyond the diagnosis. Reduced cost, wait time, length of stay (median 20 minutes) and imaging, without sacrificing outcomes. Martin 2021
How often a first sprain becomes chronic instability Ask the prognosis question out loud. 40%. In the only prospective study to date, chronic ankle instability was present in 40% of people who sought care for a first-time lateral ankle sprain. Martin 2021
And that is probably an underestimate elsewhere Compare study designs. The guideline attributes much lower retrospective estimates to care-seeking behaviour, and says the true burden is likely underrepresented in retrospective designs. Martin 2021
Return to participation Set the expectation early. 1 day to a little more than 3 weeks, depending on the demands of the activity or sport. Martin 2021
Full recovery is a different question Do not let the first number stand for the second. The guideline's own words: full recovery with no symptoms or limitations may take months or years to obtain, and cannot be expected in all patients. Return to sport is not recovery. Martin 2021
Does severity predict the course? Resist the grading instinct. The evidence conflicts. The guideline states there is conflicting evidence for the role of injury severity in the clinical course - a mild sprain is not a promise. Martin 2021
Recurrence rates Ask about previous sprains every time. Nearly 12% of reported sprains in collegiate athletes were recurrent; 14.2% of professional football and basketball players report a history of recurrence; 13.7% of elite soccer players sustain one. Martin 2021
Modifiable risk for a repeat sprain Two things you can change. Not using prophylactic bracing and not doing a balance-training programme are both risk factors for a subsequent sprain. Martin 2021
What predicts chronic instability at 6 months Test it at two weeks. Inability to complete jumping and landing tasks within 2 weeks of injury, poorer dynamic postural control, and lower self-reported function. Martin 2021
An early MRI finding that predicts a slow course If imaging was done, read it for prognosis. A medial joint bone marrow contusion on tibia and/or talus within two weeks meant 25 versus 16 days to normal walking (P = .0002) and 92 versus 56 days to sport (P = .0001). Martin 2021
Laxity and function come apart Do not read one from the other. Over six weeks, self-reported function improved significantly while anterior drawer laxity did not change at all (P > .05). Martin 2021
Anterior drawer accuracy Ask for the figure before you rely on one. There is none published here. no traceable source The guideline recommends the test at Grade B and does not report its accuracy.
Talar tilt accuracy Same question. None. no traceable source Its only appearance in the guideline is inside the literature search-strategy appendix - a list of query terms, not a finding.
Normal ankle ROM Goniometry against the other side. Convention No free normative source, as at the hip and knee. no traceable source
What is traceable about that Compare textbook to measured. Against a national probability sample, textbook values for hip and knee were off by as much as 18°. That paper does not cover the ankle, so it supports the argument here and is not an ankle figure. Roach 1991
03

Posture and resting alignment

Short, because little here is measured - but two of its items are Grade A documented measures rather than impressions.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Swelling Figure-of-eight or circumference, same landmarks each time. Grade A: assess and document, at baseline and two or more times over an episode of care. Martin 2021
Where the swelling sits Lateral, medial or diffuse; whether it tracks distally. Diffuse or medial swelling after an inversion injury should raise the question of a deltoid or syndesmotic injury. Convention
Ecchymosis and point tenderness Inspect and palpate. Both feed the Ottawa decision and the Grade B severity classification. Martin 2021
Standing foot posture Weight-bearing alignment and hindfoot position. Convention No threshold making a posture abnormal is traceable here. no traceable source
04

Active range and what they can do alone

The guideline names the specific instruments rather than the domains, which is unusual and useful: use the ones it names and your measurements compare to something.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Active range, four directions Dorsiflexion, plantarflexion, inversion, eversion. Compare sides; the norms are convention Convention and the other ankle is the better reference.
Dorsiflexion, specifically Weight-bearing lunge: knee to wall, heel down, measure the distance. The weight-bearing lunge test is the instrument the guideline names for dorsiflexion. Martin 2021
Single-limb balance Static, firm surface, eyes closed. Time it. Named specifically - eyes closed on a firm surface, not simply "balance". Martin 2021
Dynamic balance Star Excursion Balance Test. In the anterior, anteromedial, posteromedial and posterolateral reach directions. Martin 2021
Hopping Single-limb hopping under timed conditions. Grade B, where appropriate. Martin 2021
Why hopping matters beyond function Use it as a prognostic test at two weeks. Inability to complete jumping and landing tasks within two weeks predicted chronic instability at six months. It is prognostic, not only a discharge criterion. Martin 2021
Gait and weight bearing Can they bear weight and take four steps? That question is part of the Ottawa rule, not separate from it. Sharifi Razavi 2026
05

Passive range, end feel and length

Two of the passive measures here are Grade A documented findings. The rest is convention, and the section says which is which.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Passive against active Compare each direction. Passive exceeding active points to weakness, guarding or pain inhibition. Convention
Talar translation Anterior glide of the talus on the tibia. Grade A: assess and document at baseline and two or more times. Martin 2021
Talar inversion Inversion stress at the talocrural joint. Grade A: assess and document at baseline and two or more times. Martin 2021
Accessory glide Talocrural anterior-posterior, subtalar, distal tibiofibular. Convention Graded by comparison with the other side rather than by a cited figure.
Gastrocnemius against soleus Dorsiflexion with the knee straight, then bent. The difference between the two is the gastrocnemius contribution, because it crosses the knee. Convention
Plantar fascia Windlass mechanism on great toe extension. Convention Note this guide does not cover plantar heel pain - see section 08 for why.

Strength

The interesting recommendation in this section is not about the ankle at all.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
The four directions Against resistance, compared side to side. Convention No reachable strength norms. no traceable source
Fibularis specifically Resisted eversion in slight plantarflexion. The evertors are the lateral ligament's active partner. The guideline supports dry needling of the fibularis group with proprioceptive training in chronic instability, at Grade C. Martin 2021
Hip strength Abduction, extension and external rotation. Grade C in chronic ankle instability: assess and document, two or more times over an episode of care. Martin 2021

07

Special tests

The best-evidenced joint in this series recommends its special tests without publishing their accuracy. That is an unusual and honest position for a guideline to take, and this section says so rather than filling the gap.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
The recommended set - Grade B Anterior drawer, plus reverse anterolateral drawer and anterolateral talar palpation. Use them in addition to a thorough history and physical examination to aid diagnosis of a lateral ankle sprain. Martin 2021
Note what that recommendation does Read the phrase "in addition to". The traditional drawer is not enough on its own. The other two are added to it, not offered instead of it. Martin 2021
Accuracy of any of the three Ask for a figure. None published. no traceable source Recommended, not quantified.
Talar tilt Ask the same question. None, and its only appearance in the guideline is in a search-strategy appendix. no traceable source
What the drawer does not tell you Do not read stability from laxity. Laxity and function move independently - function improved significantly over six weeks while drawer laxity did not change. A stable-feeling ankle can have a lax drawer, and the reverse. Martin 2021
Classifying an acute sprain - Grade B Seven clinical findings, together. Level of function, ligamentous laxity, haemorrhaging, point tenderness, total ankle motion, swelling and pain. Martin 2021
Identifying chronic instability - Grade B A questionnaire and a test battery. Use the Cumberland Ankle Instability Tool or the Identification of Functional Ankle Instability, plus functional performance tests with established validity to separate people with instability from healthy controls. Martin 2021
The point of that row Notice what is absent from it. Chronic ankle instability is identified by a questionnaire and performance tests, not by a laxity test. That surprises most students.
Interpreting a likelihood ratio Convert to a probability shift. Convention for the familiar bands. What is sourced: a rule with −LR 0.13 and +LR 1.76 is a rule-out. Sharifi Razavi 2026
08

Syndromes and what you call it

The naming problem at the ankle is not a misnamed test - it is that an acute sprain is treated as a self-limiting nuisance when two in five care-seekers do not fully recover.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Acute lateral ankle sprain Classify with the seven findings from section 07. Grade B. Martin 2021
Chronic ankle instability Identified by CAIT or IdFAI plus functional performance tests. Grade B - a questionnaire-and-performance diagnosis rather than a laxity one. Martin 2021
How common it is after a first sprain Ask the prognosis question. 40% of people who sought care for a first-time sprain, in the only prospective study. Not a rare complication - the modal bad outcome. Martin 2021
Why that reframes the acute sprain Decide what the acute visit is for. If two in five care-seekers develop chronic instability, the prevention recommendations in section 10 are the main event rather than an afterthought. Martin 2021
Midportion Achilles tendinopathy Load-related midportion pain and stiffness. Managed by loading first, and explicitly not by rest - see section 10. Chimenti 2024
Plantar heel pain Deliberately not covered. The only reachable guideline for it is a confidential stakeholder-review draft, watermarked on every page as not for reproduction or circulation. This guide does not quote drafts, and says so rather than quietly omitting the region.
Movement diagnosis Name the fault and its direction under load. Convention
Relative stiffness Look at the knee and the midfoot. A stiff ankle borrows from both. Convention
09

Palpation

Palpation at the ankle is unusually consequential, because part of it is a Grade A imaging decision rather than a soft finding.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
The malleoli Posterior edge and tip of each, 6cm proximally. This is an Ottawa zone. Dropping malleolar tenderness from the rule cost 12 points of sensitivity. Martin 2021
The fibula along its length And the base of the fifth metatarsal, and the navicular. Ottawa zones, and none of them is where the patient points. Convention
Lateral ligaments ATFL, then CFL, then PTFL. Convention Tenderness localises; it does not grade.
Deltoid ligament Medial, below and anterior to the medial malleolus. Medial tenderness after an inversion injury raises the question of a deltoid or syndesmotic injury. Convention
Point tenderness Record it. One of the seven findings the guideline names for classifying severity - Grade B. Martin 2021
Achilles Midportion against insertion. The guideline covers midportion only; insertional tendinopathy is outside its scope and outside this guide's. Chimenti 2024
What palpation cannot do Know its ceiling. It cannot grade a ligament or confirm instability. Chronic instability is a questionnaire-and-performance diagnosis. Martin 2021
10

Prevention, mobilisation, exercise and dose

The best-evidenced section in any of these four guides, and the only one containing Grade A prevention - which matters more than it sounds, because 40% of people who seek care for a first sprain go on to chronic instability. Every letter below comes from a front-matter Summary of Recommendations, checked by position, so it is the current revision rather than a superseded one.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Prevention, first sprain - A Before anything has happened. Prophylactic bracing to reduce the risk of a first-time sprain, particularly in those with risk factors. Martin 2021
Prevention, never-injured - C Balance training as primary prevention. May be recommended to people who have not yet sprained. Note the weaker grade than bracing. Martin 2021
Prevention, after a first sprain - A The combination, not either alone. Prophylactic bracing and proprioceptive, balance-focused exercise, targeted at the impairments found on examination, to reduce subsequent injury. Martin 2021
Acute - A External support and weight bearing. Advise braces or taping and progressive weight bearing on the affected limb. Martin 2021
Acute - A A structured exercise programme. Protected active ROM, stretching, neuromuscular training, postural re-education and balance training, in clinic and at home, matched to severity, impairments, preferences, learning needs and social barriers. Martin 2021
Acute manual therapy - A Within pain-free movement. Lymphatic drainage, active and passive soft tissue and joint mobilisation, and anterior-to-posterior talar mobilisation, alongside exercise, to reduce swelling, improve pain-free mobility and normalise gait. Martin 2021
Return to work - B Plan it rather than wait for it. A return-to-work schedule, a brace early in rehabilitation, occupational or sport-related training, and/or a work-hardening programme. Martin 2021
Acute - A, against One thing to stop doing. Do not use ultrasound for the management of acute ankle sprains. Martin 2021
Acute - C The weaker acute options. Ice with a therapeutic exercise programme; pulsed shortwave diathermy for oedema and gait deviation; low-level laser for early pain; NSAIDs where practice acts allow. Martin 2021
Chronic instability - A The core intervention. Proprioceptive and neuromuscular exercise, to improve dynamic postural stability and patient-perceived stability during function. Martin 2021
Chronic instability - A Manual therapy, with a stated target. Graded joint mobilisations, manipulations, and weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing mobilisation with movement, to improve weight-bearing dorsiflexion and dynamic balance in the short term. Martin 2021
Chronic instability - B, against The brace is an adjunct, never the plan. Do not use bracing or taping as a stand-alone intervention to improve balance and postural stability. Grade A with exercise, Grade B against alone - the contrast is the teaching point. Martin 2021
Chronic instability - B and C Supplementing balance training. Multiple interventions may supplement balance training, guided by the patient's values and goals and the clinician's judgement (B); dry needling of the fibularis group with proprioceptive training (C). Martin 2021
Psychological - E Address the beliefs as well as the joint. Psychologically informed techniques such as motivational interviewing, to maximise self-efficacy and address psychological correlates of injury adjustment and recovery. Martin 2021
Outcome measures - A Validated, and named. PROMIS physical function and pain interference scales, the Foot and Ankle Ability Measure, and the Lower Extremity Functional Scale. Martin 2021
Coping and fear - C When recovery stalls, measure why. Pain Self-Efficacy Questionnaire acutely and post-acutely; TSK-11 and FABQ for fear of movement, reinjury and fear-avoidance in chronic instability. Martin 2021
Achilles - A Load it. Tendon loading exercise, with loads as high as tolerated, as first-line treatment, to improve function and decrease pain in midportion Achilles tendinopathy. Chimenti 2024
Achilles - B What not to advise. Complete rest is not indicated. Patients should continue their activities within their pain tolerance. Chimenti 2024
Achilles - B Education alongside the loading. With either a pain-science or a pathoanatomic focus, combined with tendon-loading exercise. Chimenti 2024
Achilles - C The adjuncts. Heel lifts to temporarily reduce dorsiflexion during activity; multimodal treatment to enhance the benefits of exercise. Chimenti 2024
Achilles - C, against Two to avoid. Do not use low-level laser therapy; do not use therapeutic ultrasound alone. Chimenti 2024
Achilles - the recommendation that reversed Check which revision you were taught. The 2018 guideline said should not use therapeutic elastic tape (F); the 2024 revision says may (E). The current position is may. Quoting the older one would contradict the guideline while citing it. Chimenti 2024
Achilles - D A guideline declining to recommend. No recommendation is possible on orthoses, because the evidence is contradictory. That is information, not a gap. Chimenti 2024
The reassessment marker Name it before you treat. Swelling, ROM, talar translation, talar inversion and single-leg balance are the Grade A set, at baseline and two or more times. Martin 2021

Rapid drill

Two ways to rehearse. The cases work the whole chain from presentation to reassessment marker; the single facts test one value at a time, and most test whether you know what a value is worth as well as what it is.

What to practice

Built from the cells you marked missed while running recall. Nothing here is pre-filled - if this is empty, go and quiz yourself first. The practice method is chosen by what the item is, not by what kind of learner you are: a number, a pair of hands and a decision are three different jobs and they need three different kinds of rehearsal.

Why this is not a learning-styles quiz. Sorting students into visual, auditory or kinesthetic learners and matching teaching to the label is one of the most durable ideas in education that the evidence does not support - the studies that test it properly find no benefit from the match Pashler 2008. What does hold up is the method fitting the material: retrieving a fact from memory beats re-reading it, and spacing the attempts beats massing them Dunlosky 2013. So the plan below sorts your misses by the kind of thing they are.

Cases - work the whole chain

Open a case, then work down the prompts - answer each out loud and click to check. Answers are hidden every time a case opens, so nothing is spoiled by looking.

Netball player, 19. Inverted her ankle two days ago landing from a jump. Swollen laterally, bruising tracking into the foot. She can weight bear but limps. Tender over the ATFL. She wants to know when she can play.
Ottawa first
Palpate the posterior edge and tip of both malleoli, the navicular and the base of the fifth metatarsal, and ask whether she could bear weight for four steps. Apply it entire - dropping malleolar tenderness costs 12 points of sensitivity Martin 2021. A negative rule leaves about a 1% probability of fracture Sharifi Razavi 2026.
Classify it
Level of function, ligamentous laxity, haemorrhaging, point tenderness, total ankle motion, swelling and pain - the seven findings, Grade B Martin 2021.
The answer to her question
Return to participation runs 1 day to a little more than 3 weeks depending on the demands of the sport. But full recovery with no symptoms "may take months or years to obtain, and cannot be expected in all patients" Martin 2021. Do not let the first number stand for the second.
The number she should hear
40% of people who seek care for a first sprain develop chronic ankle instability Martin 2021. That is what makes the next row the important one.
First three
External support and progressive weight bearing (A); a structured exercise programme with protected ROM, neuromuscular training and balance work, in clinic and at home (A); manual therapy within pain-free range including anterior-to-posterior talar mobilisation and lymphatic drainage (A) Martin 2021. No ultrasound (A, against).
Reassess
Swelling, ROM, talar translation, talar inversion and single-leg balance - the Grade A set, at baseline and at least twice more. And test jumping and landing at two weeks, because failing it predicts instability at six months Martin 2021.
Recreational footballer, 27. Fourth sprain of the same ankle in three years. "It just gives way." Anterior drawer feels lax but so does the other side. He has a brace he wears for matches and does no other rehabilitation.
Is the drawer the diagnosis?
No. Chronic ankle instability is identified with a discriminative instrument - the Cumberland Ankle Instability Tool or the Identification of Functional Ankle Instability - plus functional performance tests with established validity, Grade B Martin 2021. It is a questionnaire-and-performance diagnosis, not a laxity one.
Why the lax drawer does not settle it
Laxity and function move independently: over six weeks function improved significantly while drawer laxity did not change at all Martin 2021. And no accuracy figure for the drawer is published anyway no traceable source.
The brace
Here is the contrast worth learning. Bracing is Grade A as prophylaxis combined with proprioceptive and balance exercise - and Grade B against as a stand-alone intervention for balance and postural stability Martin 2021. He is doing the half that does not work alone.
What to add
Proprioceptive and neuromuscular exercise for dynamic postural stability (A); manual therapy including weight-bearing mobilisation with movement to improve dorsiflexion and balance in the short term (A) Martin 2021.
Look proximally
Assess and document hip abduction, extension and external rotation strength, Grade C in chronic instability Martin 2021. Also consider fear of reinjury with the TSK-11 or FABQ (C).
Runner, 44. Six months of midportion Achilles pain, worst starting a run and easing as he warms up. He has stopped running entirely for eight weeks on advice and is no better. He asks about orthotics and a night splint.
The advice he was given
Wrong, and the guideline says so directly: complete rest is not indicated, and patients should continue activities within their pain tolerance, Grade B Chimenti 2024.
First-line treatment
Tendon loading exercise with loads as high as tolerated, Grade A, to improve function and decrease pain - either eccentric or heavy-load slow-speed work Chimenti 2024.
Orthotics
The guideline makes no recommendation, Grade D, because the evidence is contradictory Chimenti 2024. A guideline declining to recommend is information, not a gap.
The night splint
Recommended against in the body of the guideline for midportion tendinopathy Chimenti 2024.
What else is worth adding
Education with a pain-science or pathoanatomic focus, combined with the loading, Grade B Chimenti 2024. Heel lifts may temporarily reduce dorsiflexion during activity (C). Not low-level laser, not ultrasound alone (C, against).

Single facts

Give the Ottawa Ankle Rules' pooled sensitivity and specificity. What is a negative worth?
Pooled Sn 0.92 (0.91-0.93), Sp 0.35 (0.34-0.36), −LR 0.13. A negative leaves roughly a 1% post-test probability of fracture, which is why the rules safely reduce radiography. A positive barely moves it - +LR 1.76 Sharifi Razavi 2026.
Why must the rules be applied in their entirety?
Because a partial application is measurably worse. Omitting malleolar tenderness and relying on inability to bear weight and take four steps gave sensitivity 88%, against 92%-100% for the whole rule Martin 2021.
In children, which performs better - the low-risk ankle rules or the Ottawa rules?
The Ottawa rules. The low-risk ankle rules' sensitivity of 85.7% is inferior to the Ottawa rules' 100% in that population Martin 2021.
What is the anterior drawer test's sensitivity?
Not published here. no traceable source The guideline recommends it at Grade B - alongside the reverse anterolateral drawer test and anterolateral talar palpation - and reports no accuracy for any of the three Martin 2021. The same goes for talar tilt, whose only appearance in the guideline is inside a literature search-strategy appendix.
Function improved over six weeks. What happened to drawer laxity?
Nothing. Ankle laxity did not change significantly on the anterior drawer test (P > .05) despite significant improvement in self-reported function Martin 2021. A stable-feeling ankle can have a lax drawer, and the reverse.
How is chronic ankle instability actually identified?
With a discriminative questionnaire - the Cumberland Ankle Instability Tool or the Identification of Functional Ankle Instability - plus functional performance tests with established validity to separate people with instability from healthy controls. Grade B Martin 2021. Not with a laxity test.
What proportion of people who seek care for a first ankle sprain develop chronic instability?
40%, in the only prospective study performed to date. The guideline adds that retrospective designs likely underrepresent the true burden, because of care-seeking behaviour Martin 2021.
Return to participation takes 1 to 3 weeks. How long does full recovery take?
Possibly far longer, and possibly never: "full recovery with no symptoms or limitations may take months or years to obtain, and cannot be expected in all patients" Martin 2021. Return to sport is not recovery.
Does injury severity predict the clinical course?
The evidence conflicts, by the guideline's own statement Martin 2021. A mild sprain is not a promise of a quick course.
Bracing for chronic ankle instability - Grade A or Grade B against? Explain both.
Both, and the contrast is the point. Grade A as prophylaxis combined with proprioceptive and balance-focused exercise. Grade B against as a stand-alone intervention to improve balance and postural stability Martin 2021. The brace is an adjunct, never the plan.
Name the four Star Excursion Balance Test reach directions the guideline specifies.
Anterior, anteromedial, posteromedial and posterolateral Martin 2021. It also names the weight-bearing lunge test for dorsiflexion and static single-limb balance on a firm surface with eyes closed.
Which hip movements should be tested in chronic ankle instability, and at what grade?
Abduction, extension and external rotation, assessed and documented two or more times over an episode of care - Grade C Martin 2021. The proximal contribution to a distal problem is a graded recommendation here, not a theory.
What is first-line treatment for midportion Achilles tendinopathy, and at what load?
Tendon loading exercise, with loads as high as tolerated, Grade A - either eccentric or heavy-load slow-speed Chimenti 2024.
Is rest indicated for Achilles tendinopathy?
No. "Complete rest is not indicated", and patients should continue their activities within their pain tolerance - Grade B Chimenti 2024.
Elastic tape for Achilles tendinopathy - should not, or may? Which revision said which?
The 2018 guideline said should not (F). The 2024 revision says may (E). The current position is may Chimenti 2024. Quoting the older one would contradict the guideline while citing it.
What does the Achilles guideline say about orthoses?
That no recommendation can be made, Grade D, because the evidence is contradictory Chimenti 2024. A guideline declining to recommend is itself information.
What predicts chronic ankle instability at six months?
Inability to complete jumping and landing tasks within 2 weeks of the injury, poorer dynamic postural control, and lower self-reported function Martin 2021. Which makes a two-week hop test a prognostic act, not a discharge one.
What is the normal ankle range of motion?
No figure here is defensible. Convention no traceable source - the same result as at the hip and knee. What is traceable is that textbook hip and knee values were off by as much as 18° against a measured population sample Roach 1991, and that paper does not cover the ankle.
Why is plantar heel pain absent from this guide?
Because the only reachable guideline for it is a confidential stakeholder-review draft, watermarked on all 49 pages as not for reproduction or circulation. A draft's values may differ from what was finally published, and quoting it here would be exactly the circulation it asks against.

Two are graded clinical practice guidelines openly hosted by the Academy of Orthopaedic Physical Therapy. Where a value is marked convention, or flagged as having no traceable source, it appears in no entry below, because nothing supports it. Each note records what the paper cannot support as well as what it can.

Guidelines

  1. Martin RL, Davenport TE, Fraser JJ, Sawdon-Bea J, Carcia CR, Carroll LA, Kivlan BR, Carreira D Ankle Stability and Movement Coordination Impairments: Lateral Ankle Ligament Sprains Revision 2021. J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 2021;51(4):CPG1-CPG80Openly hosted by the Academy of Orthopaedic Physical Therapy, all 80 pages free and graded. The spine of this guide: 23 current recommendations taken from its front-matter Summary of Recommendations, plus the Ottawa figures, the named measurement instruments, the classification and instability criteria, and the prognosis. Its body reprints 2013 recommendations beside the 2021 ones, so every letter quoted here was confirmed by position to come from the current set. It recommends the anterior drawer test at Grade B without publishing accuracy for it, which is why no sensitivity appears beside it in this guide.
  2. Chimenti RL, Neville C, Houck J, Cuddeford T, Carreira D, Martin RL Achilles Pain, Stiffness, and Muscle Power Deficits: Midportion Achilles Tendinopathy Revision 2024. J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 2024Openly hosted, free, 32 pages. Eleven current recommendations from its front-matter summary. Source for tendon loading at loads as high as tolerated as first-line treatment, and for the statement that complete rest is not indicated. Note its therapeutic elastic tape recommendation reversed direction between revisions - 2018 said should not, 2024 says may - so the era header matters. Scope is midportion tendinopathy only; insertional tendinopathy is outside it.

Fracture screening

  1. Sharifi Razavi D, Seiri M, Farrokhi M Revisiting the diagnostic accuracy of Ottawa Ankle Rules for ankle fractures: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Arch Acad Emerg Med 2026;14(1):e24Free full text. Pooled sensitivity 0.92 and specificity 0.35, +LR 1.76, −LR 0.13, DOR 16.21. Unlike the equivalent knee meta-analysis, this paper reads its own numbers correctly - it frames the rules as a screening tool for ruling out fracture rather than claiming high diagnostic performance in both directions.

Range of motion

  1. Roach KE, Miles TP Normal hip and knee active range of motion: the relationship to age. Phys Ther 1991;71(9):656-665Cited once, for the size of the gap between textbook and measured values - as much as 18° against a national probability sample. It covers the hip and knee only, so it supports the argument that normative tables are unreliable and is explicitly not an ankle figure.

About this guide. Version 1.0 · September 2026. The examination sequence, the section order and the wording are original. Every clinical value is drawn from the four published sources listed above and attributed where it appears - principally Martin and colleagues 2021 for the lateral ankle sprain and chronic instability content and the whole of section 10, Chimenti and colleagues 2024 for midportion Achilles tendinopathy, and Sharifi Razavi and colleagues 2026 for the Ottawa Ankle Rules. Every graded recommendation quoted here was taken from its guideline's front-matter Summary of Recommendations and confirmed by position to be the current revision. Both guidelines reprint superseded recommendations in their bodies, and in one case the direction reversed between revisions, so this was checked rather than assumed. Values marked Convention are named as such because no research stands behind them, and three are flagged as having no traceable source: normative ankle range of motion, and the diagnostic accuracy of the anterior drawer and talar tilt tests - the latter two because the guideline recommends the tests without publishing figures for them. Plantar heel pain is deliberately absent. The only reachable guideline for it is a confidential stakeholder-review draft, watermarked on every page as not for reproduction or circulation, and this guide does not quote drafts. Verify against current literature before letting any of it weight a clinical decision.
