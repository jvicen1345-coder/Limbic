Physical therapy · the decision to mobilise, and the dose Version 1.0 · September 2026

Joint Mobilization Playbook

The other four guides in this series walk a body region. This one walks a decision: whether to mobilise, where, how hard, and how you would know it worked. It is shaped by an absence. Three of the conventions that decide how a mobilization is performed could not be traced to any reachable definition, derivation or reliability figure - the Maitland and Kaltenborn grading systems, open- and close-packed positions, and the concave-convex rule. Meanwhile the things that are measured are modest and specific: two clinicians agree on an accessory glide at ICC 0.64, the only poolable proprioception result is null, and across 26 neuroimaging studies none linked the cortical change it found to the clinical improvement it observed. What survives that filter is worth knowing cold - above all the one dose vocabulary that is defined and has different outcomes attached to each level. This is a study aid for examination preparation, not a clinical reference - check any value against current literature before it weights a decision about a patient.

How to read a value. Unmarked means traced to a source you can read for free, with the citation beside it. Convention nobody has tested it. Contested tested, and the studies disagree - quote the range.

01

The reasoning sequence

Mobilization is a decision before it is a technique. The sequence below is the decision - and its first two steps disqualify most of the joints you were about to put your hands on.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Screen before you touch History, red flags, and the reason this joint is stiff. The reachable harms data is reassuring about common events and silent about rare ones. It cannot tell you screening is unnecessary. Daniels 2026
Is the restriction articular? Compare active with passive, and passive with accessory. Mobilization treats a joint. If the limit is muscular, neural or pain-driven guarding, the technique is aimed at the wrong tissue. Convention
Does the evidence support it here? Look up the region before you commit. This is regional, not general. Grade A at the hip and ankle, expert opinion (F) for Achilles, weak recommendations after knee arthroplasty, and absent entirely from the knee meniscal and cartilage guideline. Koc 2025 Martin 2021 Chimenti 2024 Bove 2026 Logerstedt 2018
What do you expect to change? Name it, and name the number you will re-measure. Range and pain do not respond to the same dose. High force moved range; low force moved pain pressure threshold. Decide which you are buying. Koc 2025
Which glide, which direction? Assess accessory motion joint by joint. Name the restricted direction before choosing a technique, not after. Convention
How hard? Take up slack, then decide where to stop. Use the vocabulary that is actually defined: stretching exceeding resistance, stopping at the point of resistance, no resistance. Koc 2025
How much? Set sessions per week and a review date. The hip guideline's own conclusion is that a specific dosage "could not be made" - five of ten studies were at high risk of bias. Its stated range is 1 to 3 times per week over 6-12 weeks. Koc 2025
Re-measure immediately Same marker, same session, same examiner. The measured effects are immediate and short term. If you do not re-test in the same session you have no evidence you did anything. Hadjisavvas 2026 Martin 2021
Then load it Hand the patient the exercise before they leave. Mobilization appears in every graded recommendation alongside exercise, never instead of it. Koc 2025 Martin 2021 Bove 2026

02

Numbers worth knowing cold - and the ones that do not exist

Half of this section is deliberately empty. The empty half is the finding: the vocabulary every student memorises cannot be traced to anything, while the things that are measured turn out modest and specific.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Maitland grades I-IV The four-grade oscillation scale. no traceable source No reachable paper defines them, derives them, or reports whether two clinicians produce the same one. Also absent as a definition from all five guideline PDFs held.
Kaltenborn grades I-III The three-grade translatoric scale. no traceable source Same searches, same result. Zero mentions across all five guidelines.
Reproducibility of a nominated grade Whether your grade III is my grade III. no traceable source No force or amplitude reliability study was reachable. This is the single most important gap in the guide.
The one guideline mention Read what it is doing. The ankle guideline refers to "anterior-to-posterior Maitland grade III joint mobilizations" while summarising someone else's trial. It uses the grade as a label and never defines it. Martin 2021
Open- and close-packed positions The position you pre-position the joint in. no traceable source Four search-phrase variants returned nothing that defines, derives or tests them - and the hip guideline performs its distraction "in open packed position" without defining that either. Koc 2025
The concave-convex rule The rule that tells you which way to glide. Convention and now Contested. The one clinical paper that engages it measured 36 (3) mm of posterior femoral translation from 90° to 150° of knee flexion, and concluded the findings "invite review of the concave-convex rule as it might apply to manual therapy of the knee". Scarvell 2019
Inter-rater agreement, accessory glide Two clinicians, one ankle. ICC 0.64 (SEM 2.8°, MDC 4.7°) for the posterior talar glide test between a novice and an experienced rater - described as poor to good. Nguyen 2026
Intra-rater agreement, same test One clinician, twice. ICC 0.97 (novice) and 0.94 (experienced). One clinician is highly consistent with themselves; two clinicians are not consistent with each other. Nguyen 2026
What fixed it Add an external reference. A referential horizontal plane raised inter-rater agreement to ICC 0.78. Not skill, not a finer grading vocabulary - an external measurable datum. Nguyen 2026
The noise floor Before you call a change real. MDC 3.3°-4.7°. A difference smaller than roughly four degrees between two examiners is not a finding. Nguyen 2026
High-force long-axis distraction, hip Three sessions, 1-week follow-up, 60 patients. Flexion 10.6°, extension 8.0°, abduction 6.4°, adduction 3.3°, ER 5.6°, IR 7.6° - all significant (p < .05) against the low-force group. Koc 2025
Low and medium force, same study The comparison that makes the point. No significant change in hip ROM at all. Koc 2025
But low force won on pain The dose trade-off, measured. Largest effect on pain pressure threshold, d = 2.0. High force had the largest effect on physical function, d = 0.5-0.7. Koc 2025
Single-session hip MWM n = 40, mean age 78 ± 6. Immediate: pain d = 1.9, flexion ROM d = 3.0, IR ROM d = 1.4, TUG d = 1.0, 30-second chair stand d = 1.7, 40-m walk d = 1.5. Koc 2025
Mulligan MWM, rotator cuff disease Four RCTs, 160 participants. Pain at rest MD −1.19 (I² = 0%), pain on activity MD −2.25, function MD −14.71, ROM MD 19.92, joint position sense MD −3.31. Quality of life: no significant difference. Alqallaf 2025
Mulligan SNAGs, chronic low back pain Seventeen RCTs, 716 participants. Pain SMD −0.78, disability SMD −0.60, lumbar extension ROM SMD 0.75; flexion ROM not significant. Shager 2026
Read that row with its heterogeneity Always print the I² beside the SMD. I² = 92%, 83% and 93%. The authors rate certainty moderate for pain and low for disability and mobility. A pooled estimate at that dispersion describes no single population. Shager 2026
Proprioception, the only poolable result Knee JPSE at 60°, two trials. Hedges' g = −0.21, 95% CI −1.36 to 0.94, I² ≈ 83% - not significant. Every other outcome was too heterogeneous to pool. Hadjisavvas 2026
Adverse events, adults over 55 6 RCTs, 4 cohorts, 15 case series. 412 study-related events, 9 severe, none catastrophic - mostly soreness, stiffness, numbness, weakness, fatigue, headache, dizziness. Daniels 2026
What that count is not Read the denominator before the numerator. Not an incidence. There is no denominator, several included studies lacked adverse-event definitions, and the review excluded the database designs that detect rare events. Daniels 2026
03

Before you touch - screening and contraindications

The honest position is that common harms are well described and mild, and the rare ones are undescribed rather than absent. Those are different statements, and the difference is the whole section.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
What happens, commonly Warn the patient about this before, not after. Changes in pain quality, muscle or joint soreness or stiffness, numbness, weakness, fatigue, headache, dizziness or lightheadedness. Mild to moderate. Daniels 2026
Severe events in trial and cohort data Across 6 RCTs and 4 observational studies. 9 of 412, and none catastrophic, in adults over 55. Daniels 2026
Severe events in the case reports A different literature, a different picture. 6 severe adverse events, four of them vascular. Twenty of the case reports involved spinal manipulation. Daniels 2026
What generated no case reports Worth knowing before you escalate a technique. None were reported following soft tissue techniques, exercise, or other modalities. Every case report involved a thrust, a modality, or repetitive shoulder abduction. Daniels 2026
Why that is not reassurance The limitation the authors state themselves. The review excluded administrative database studies - and names that as a limitation precisely because those "may detect rare AEs". The design could not have found a catastrophic event. Daniels 2026
One more caveat on that source Read the funding line. It was partially funded by Clinical Compass and the NCMIC Foundation, both chiropractic organisations. A harms review funded by the profession under review is a conflict to weigh, not to ignore. Daniels 2026
A validated screening test Before cervical work especially. no traceable source Nothing reachable validates a screening procedure against the event it is meant to prevent. The nearest hit was a survey of what faculty teach about cervical artery dissection - a study of teaching, not of screening.
Harms in the hip guideline Stated inside a Grade A recommendation. "No harms of manual therapy were reported." Koc 2025
Modify for the person in front of you Grade F, but a real instruction. Clinicians may modify procedures and force amplitude according to bony hip morphology and tissue tolerance/irritability. Koc 2025
The ankle guideline's own limit Written into the recommendation itself. Acute-phase talar mobilization is specified "within pain-free movement". That is a safety boundary you can only enforce by asking during. Martin 2021
Absolute contraindications The list everyone teaches. Fracture, instability, infection, malignancy, and any undiagnosed progressive neurological deficit. Convention - universally taught; no reachable source lists or tests them.
When you are unsure The failure mode to avoid. Treat "I cannot rule this out" as a reason to examine further, not as a reason to proceed gently. Reducing force does not convert an unscreened joint into a screened one. Convention
04

What the joint does on its own

Active range is where you decide whether there is an articular problem to mobilise at all. Get this wrong and everything after it is a well-executed answer to the wrong question.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Why active comes first Watch them move before you move them. It is the patient's own report in movement - willingness, pain arc and available range in one observation. Convention
What a full active range tells you And what it saves you doing. That the joint surfaces travel. A joint with full active range rarely needs accessory mobilization for range. Convention
What a limited active range does not tell you The commonest reasoning error in this guide. Which tissue limits it. Active limitation is equally consistent with capsular restriction, muscular restriction, pain inhibition and unwillingness - and mobilization is indicated for one of those four. Convention
Where in the range the problem sits Note the arc, not just the endpoint. A mid-range catch and an end-range block are different problems and take different techniques. Convention
Weight-bearing or not Position changes both technique and measurement. The ankle guideline names weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing mobilization with movement separately, and the weight-bearing lunge as the dorsiflexion measure. Martin 2021
Normative range values Do not quote a textbook table. no traceable source for every joint in this series - as the hip, knee and ankle guides each found independently.
The measurement you can trust And the one you cannot. A within-session change on the same measure, taken by the same person. Intra-rater reliability is where the agreement lives: ICC 0.94-0.97 against 0.64 between raters. Nguyen 2026
So who re-measures? This follows directly from the row above. You do, and immediately. Handing the re-test to a colleague discards most of the reliability the measurement had. Nguyen 2026
What to record Three values, every time. The starting value, the technique and force level, and the value after. Without the first and third, the second is anecdote. Convention
05

Accessory motion and end feel

This is the assessment the whole intervention is built on, and it is the one place in the guide where a real reliability figure exists. It is not flattering - and it points straight at the fix.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
What accessory motion is Glide, spin and roll between surfaces. Movement the patient cannot perform voluntarily, which is why you have to produce it to assess it. Convention
Why assess before treating It sets the direction of the technique. The direction you find restricted is the direction the technique is aimed. Convention
Can you reproduce your own finding? Test, wait, re-test yourself. Yes, well. Posterior talar glide test intra-rater ICC 0.97 (novice) and 0.94 (experienced), SEM 2.2-2.4°. Nguyen 2026
Can two clinicians reproduce each other's? Ask a colleague to repeat it blind. Much less. Inter-rater ICC 0.64. And experience did not close the gap - the novice and the experienced rater were equally self-consistent. Nguyen 2026
What improved agreement The finding worth generalising. A referential horizontal plane raised inter-rater ICC to 0.78 and cut SEM to 2.3°. Nguyen 2026
The lesson from that Applies well beyond this one test. Reproducibility came from measuring against something outside the hands - not from more skill, and not from a finer grading vocabulary. Nguyen 2026
The test's own standing Quoted as the authors wrote it. The posterior talar glide test is "recommended for ankle sprain assessment, but it has limited scientific support". They do not name which guideline recommends it. Nguyen 2026
What counts as a real difference Before you disagree with a colleague. MDC 3.3°-4.7°. Below that, two examiners disagreeing means nothing at all. Nguyen 2026
End feel Capsular, bony, springy, empty, muscular. Convention. Universally taught, and no reachable source defines the categories or reports agreement between examiners on them. no traceable source
Capsular pattern Cyriax's proportional restriction. It does not hold. In 200 patients the pattern was "not present as a distinct pattern", and it "cannot be regarded as a valid test for the diagnosis of OA of the hip or knee". Bijl 1998
Which glide direction to choose Two ways to decide, and one is better. The concave-convex rule is the conventional answer and it is Contested. The restricted direction you assessed is a better guide than the rule that predicts it. Scarvell 2019
06

Force, amplitude, and where you stop

The section this guide is built around. The vocabulary students memorise cannot be traced; the vocabulary the guidelines use is defined, reproducible from its own description, and has a different measured outcome at each level.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
The grading systems Maitland I-IV, Kaltenborn I-III. no traceable source for origin, definition, or reliability. Not a gap in this guide's searching - a gap in the literature.
Say that precisely Do not overstate the absence either. They are taught universally and used as vocabulary - the ankle guideline names a "Maitland grade III" mobilization while summarising a trial. Nothing reachable defines them or shows two clinicians produce the same one. Martin 2021
What the guidelines use instead Learn these three phrases verbatim. Force relative to tissue resistance. High = stretching exceeding resistance. Medium = stopping at the point of resistance. Low = no resistance. Koc 2025
Why that vocabulary is better The test any dose scale should pass. It is defined by where the force stops relative to the tissue, so it is reproducible from the description alone - and each level has a different measured outcome. Koc 2025
High force buys range Three sessions, 1-week follow-up, 60 patients. Significant ROM gains in all six hip directions against the low-force group. Koc 2025
Low force buys pain relief And by a wider margin than you would guess. Largest effect on pain pressure threshold, d = 2.0 - larger than either heavier level achieved. Koc 2025
So the dose is a choice This is the practical takeaway of the guide. You cannot maximise both. Pick range or pain pressure threshold, and set the force accordingly. Koc 2025
Medium force The level that lost twice. Won neither outcome in that comparison - no significant ROM change, and not the largest pain effect. Koc 2025
Do not translate between scales A tempting error. These three force levels are not Maitland or Kaltenborn grades and must never be presented as equivalents. Koc 2025
Name which scale a grade belongs to The near-miss this build hit. "Grade III" in the hip literature is as likely to mean Kellgren-Lawrence radiographic severity as anything about mobilization. Those 60 patients had Grade III hip OA - a radiographic grade, not a treatment grade. Koc 2025
Dose, per the guideline that studied it For mild-to-moderate hip OA. 1 to 3 times per week over 6-12 weeks. Koc 2025
The dose caveat, verbatim Read this before quoting any dose. Across 10 studies and 768 participants the common dosages were 10-30 min per session, 2-3 times weekly for 2-6 weeks - but five of the ten studies were at high risk of bias, and "recommending a specific manual therapy dosage for those with hip OA could not be made". Koc 2025
Amplitude and oscillation How big, how fast. no traceable source No reachable study specifies or compares oscillation rate or amplitude.
Where in range to work The one defensible answer. The force scale above is a statement about where in range you stop. Use it instead of a grade number. Koc 2025
Position for the technique Named by the guideline, defined by nobody. The hip studies performed long-axis distraction in open packed position - a position the guideline names and does not define. Koc 2025
07

The techniques, by category

Five families. The honest ranking is not by sophistication - it is by how much of each one has actually been measured.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Long-axis distraction Grip distal to the joint, pull along the shaft. The only technique here with a dose-response study: three defined force levels, six directions of measured ROM change, and separate pain and function effects. Koc 2025
Sustained glides Hold at the barrier. Recommended within the guidelines' broader "joint mobilization" wording; no reachable source isolates sustained from oscillatory glides by outcome. Koc 2025 Martin 2021
Oscillatory mobilization Rhythmic, repeated, at a chosen depth. Same position. Named in practice, not separated by evidence. no traceable source for any comparison against sustained technique.
Mobilization with movement Sustain the glide while they move actively. Grade A at the hip within the manual therapy recommendation, and Grade A at the ankle in chronic instability, weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing. Koc 2025 Martin 2021
MWM, single session at the hip What one session did. Immediate pain d = 1.9, flexion ROM d = 3.0, IR ROM d = 1.4, TUG d = 1.0, chair stand d = 1.7, 40-m walk d = 1.5. Large, and immediate. Koc 2025
MWM at the shoulder Four RCTs, 160 participants. Pain, function, ROM and joint position sense all improved; quality of life did not. Alqallaf 2025
The design limit on that result Read the comparator, not the conclusion. Add-on, no sham. Mulligan plus conventional therapy against conventional therapy alone cannot separate the technique from the extra contact time. Alqallaf 2025
Mulligan's proposed mechanism Stated in the paper's own background. "Correcting positional faults" is the model's claim. No included trial measured a positional fault. Alqallaf 2025
SNAGs at the lumbar spine Seventeen RCTs, 716 participants. Significant pooled effects on pain, disability and extension range - with I² of 83-93% and certainty rated low for two of the three. Shager 2026
Manipulation High-velocity, low-amplitude thrust. Distinct in execution, and no traceable source separates it from mobilization by outcome - the reviews covering both pool them together as "articular manual therapy". Hadjisavvas 2026 Jahromi 2026
Cavitation - what the pop is The tribonucleation account. Consistent with tribonucleation, inferred from joint-space change rather than imaged directly. Sillevis 2025
Cavitation - what it is evidence of The row to remember from this section. Not of a successful technique. "All subjects regardless of audible sound increased in joint space" after MCP and MTP manipulation. The joint separated whether or not it made a noise. Sillevis 2025
Is the pop simply being heard? A neat control the study built in. No - after a T7 thrust the audible group showed significant EEG change in frontal, parietal and occipital lobes but not the temporal lobes, where the auditory centres are. Sillevis 2024
How much weight those two carry Check the author list before counting studies. They share a first author and method. Treat them as one research line, not as independent replication. Sillevis 2024 Sillevis 2025
The authors' own verdict on the pop Their sentence, not this guide's. "The clinical usefulness of audible sounds remains questionable." Sillevis 2025

Matching technique to presentation

The match is made on irritability and on what you are trying to change - not on the joint's name.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Highly irritable, pain-dominant Low force - no resistance. And this is not a compromise: low force produced the largest pain pressure threshold effect, d = 2.0. Koc 2025
Stiff, low irritability, range-dominant High force - stretching exceeding resistance. It is the only level that moved range. Koc 2025
Acute lateral ankle sprain With therapeutic exercise, from the start. Grade A: lymphatic drainage, active and passive soft tissue and joint mobilization, and anterior-to-posterior talar mobilization within pain-free movement. Martin 2021
Chronic ankle instability Weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing. Grade A: graded joint mobilizations, manipulations and MWM, to improve weight-bearing ankle dorsiflexion and dynamic balance in the short term. Martin 2021
Mild-to-moderate hip osteoarthritis The strongest recommendation in the guide. Grade A: soft tissue and/or joint mobilization including high- and low-force long-axis hip distraction and hip MWM, to increase ROM, decrease pain and improve function. Koc 2025
Early after total knee arthroplasty Read both of its ratings. Evidence quality moderate, but recommendation strength weak - the guideline downgraded it "due to limited evidence". Manual therapy may augment active-assistive exercise for ROM. Bove 2026
Midportion Achilles tendinopathy And note the population. Grade F - expert opinion. May manipulate or mobilise muscles, joints and connective tissues in those with midportion tendinopathy and mobility deficits. Chimenti 2024
Meniscal and articular cartilage lesions The absence worth knowing. No manual therapy recommendation at all. Not a negative recommendation - an absence. Logerstedt 2018
Bony morphology that limits technique Grade F, and practical. Modify procedures and force amplitude to the patient's bony hip morphology and tissue tolerance. Koc 2025
Suspected proprioceptive deficit The claim to avoid making. Immediate joint position sense gains exist in individual trials, but the only poolable result was null and no trial linked the change to pain or function. Do not promise this one. Hadjisavvas 2026
What never changes with presentation True in every graded recommendation here. Mobilization appears with exercise. Not one of these guidelines recommends it alone. Koc 2025 Martin 2021 Bove 2026
09

Position, stabilisation and hand placement

Almost everything in this section is convention. It is written down anyway, because a convention you can name is far safer than one you have mistaken for evidence.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Resting / open-packed position Maximum capsular laxity, by definition. no traceable source Tabulated identically in every textbook; nothing reachable derives or tests the values.
Yet the guidelines use it Which is exactly the problem. The hip distraction studies were performed "in open packed position" - named, used, undefined. Koc 2025
Close-packed position Maximum congruence, ligaments taut. no traceable source Same four search-phrase variants, same absence.
Why the convention still earns its place Not every unsourced thing is useless. It gives two clinicians a shared starting position. That is worth having even unsourced - provided nobody claims it is evidence-based. Convention
Stabilise the proximal segment Belt, table, or your other hand. Otherwise force is shared between the joint and everything proximal to it, and the dose you intended is not the dose delivered. Convention
Hand placement As close to the joint line as the technique allows. A longer lever multiplies force at the joint unpredictably. Convention
Force direction Along the assessed restricted glide. Assessed, not deduced. Convention
The conventional direction rule Convex on concave, concave on convex. Concave-convex, and it is Contested - see the next two rows. Scarvell 2019
What contests it Fluoroscopy plus CT, six degrees of freedom. In deep knee flexion the femur translated posteriorly by 36 (3) mm from 90° to 150°, finishing 23 (3) mm posterior to the tibial centre, with external rotation from 8° to 16°. Flexion and translation were coupled at r = 0.96. Scarvell 2019
How far that finding goes Do not overstate it - the authors did not. It does not refute the rule. The authors say the findings "invite review" of it, and they studied kneeling in 25 healthy adults over 45 - no mobilization, and no patients. Scarvell 2019
What to do with it The practical resolution. Assess the glide rather than deducing it from surface geometry. The rule predicts; the assessment measures. Convention
Communicating during the technique Ask while you are doing it. Ask about pain quality and location during, not only after - "within pain-free movement" is a limit you can only enforce by asking. Martin 2021
Why the mechanism literature will not settle this A commentary, and labelled as one. Named authors argue that mechanisms of force-based manipulation interact with unmeasured variables and are "influenced by time, clinician, environments and a litany of other mediators". A commentary, not data. Cook 2026

What the evidence supports, by region

One table, five guidelines, two grading scales. It is the most useful page in the guide and the least comfortable.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Hip osteoarthritis The strongest of the five. Grade A - soft tissue and/or joint mobilization, including high- and low-force long-axis hip distraction and hip MWM, to increase ROM, decrease pain and improve function in mild-to-moderate hip OA. Koc 2025
Hip osteoarthritis, second recommendation The individualisation clause. Grade F - may modify procedures and force amplitude to bony hip morphology and tissue tolerance/irritability. Koc 2025
Lateral ankle sprain, acute Alongside therapeutic exercise. Grade A - lymphatic drainage, active and passive soft tissue and joint mobilization, anterior-to-posterior talar mobilization within pain-free movement. Martin 2021
Lateral ankle sprain, chronic instability Note both qualifiers. Grade A - graded joint mobilizations, manipulations, weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing MWM, for weight-bearing ankle dorsiflexion and dynamic balance in the short term. Martin 2021
Lateral ankle sprain, adjunct The softer one. Grade B - multiple interventions may supplement balance training, guided by patient values and clinician judgement. Martin 2021
Total knee arthroplasty, range Two ratings, and the second is the honest one. Evidence quality moderate, strength weak, "downgraded due to limited evidence" - may augment active-assistive exercise for ROM early postoperatively. Manual therapy itself rests on one high-quality study. Bove 2026
Total knee arthroplasty, pain Same pattern again. Evidence quality moderate, strength weak, "downgraded due to low certainty of evidence" - may use manual therapy among several options; time frame not specified. Bove 2026
Midportion Achilles tendinopathy The weakest that still exists. Grade F - expert opinion; may use manual therapy where mobility deficits exist. Chimenti 2024
Knee meniscal and cartilage lesions The one that says nothing. None. No manual therapy recommendation appears in the guideline at all. Logerstedt 2018
The headline finding Say this to a student before anything else. The evidence is regional, not general. "Manual therapy is evidence-based" is true in two of these five documents, weak in two, and absent from the fifth - absent, not rejected. Koc 2025 Martin 2021 Chimenti 2024 Bove 2026 Logerstedt 2018
Two scales, not one And a trap inside the second. Four guidelines grade A-F; the arthroplasty guideline reports Evidence Quality and Recommendation Strength separately. A moderate is not a B - and quoting its evidence quality alone hides that both its manual therapy recommendations are rated weak. Bove 2026
Where dose is specified Look at the form the dose takes. Always functionally - force amplitude relative to tissue tolerance, within pain-free movement, in the short term, early postoperative, where mobility deficits exist. Not one guideline says "grade III". Koc 2025 Martin 2021 Bove 2026 Chimenti 2024
Duration of effect The qualifier attached to almost everything here. The ankle guideline's own words are "in the short term", and the proprioception evidence is entirely immediate post-intervention with no follow-up. Martin 2021 Hadjisavvas 2026
What happens centrally Twenty-six neuroimaging studies. Most found post-intervention change in motor cortex excitability (TMS), functional connectivity (fMRI) and sensorimotor integration (SEP). Jahromi 2026
The sentence to remember The gap between mechanism and outcome. "Although many studies reported reductions in pain intensity, none of them linked cortical changes directly to clinical improvements." Jahromi 2026
Reassessment Close the loop yourself. Re-measure the marker you chose, in the same session, yourself. Immediate change is what the evidence describes - and it is all you can honestly claim. Nguyen 2026 Hadjisavvas 2026
11

Rapid drill

Two ways to rehearse. The cases work a whole decision from presentation to reassessment marker; the single facts test one value at a time, and several of them test whether you know that a value does not exist.

What to practice

Built from the cells you marked missed while running recall. Nothing here is pre-filled - if this is empty, go and quiz yourself first. The practice method is chosen by what the item is, not by what kind of learner you are: a number, a pair of hands and a decision are three different jobs and they need three different kinds of rehearsal.

Why this is not a learning-styles quiz. Sorting students into visual, auditory or kinesthetic learners and matching teaching to the label is one of the most durable ideas in education that the evidence does not support - the studies that test it properly find no benefit from the match Pashler 2008. What does hold up is the method fitting the material: retrieving a fact from memory beats re-reading it, and spacing the attempts beats massing them Dunlosky 2013. So the plan below sorts your misses by the kind of thing they are.

Cases - work the whole decision

Open a case, then work down the prompts - answer each out loud and click to check. Answers are hidden every time a case opens, so nothing is spoiled by looking.

Retired teacher, 68. Two years of right groin pain, radiographic hip osteoarthritis. Stiff into flexion and internal rotation, but not especially painful to move - she rates it 3/10 and wants to get back to walking her dog up a hill. Your student asks which Maitland grade to use.
Answer the student first
You cannot source one. The Maitland and Kaltenborn grading systems have no reachable definition, origin or reliability figure no traceable source, and none of the five guidelines held here defines a grade. The ankle guideline mentions "Maitland grade III" once, as a label on someone else's trial Martin 2021.
Use the vocabulary that is defined
Force relative to tissue resistance: high = stretching exceeding resistance, medium = stopping at the point of resistance, low = no resistance Koc 2025.
Which level, for her?
High force. She is stiffness-dominant and low-irritability, and high force is the only level that moved range - flexion 10.6°, extension 8.0°, abduction 6.4°, adduction 3.3°, ER 5.6°, IR 7.6°, significant against low force. Low and medium force produced no significant ROM change at all Koc 2025.
What you are giving up
Pain relief. Low force had the largest effect on pain pressure threshold (d = 2.0) Koc 2025. You cannot maximise both, so choose deliberately and tell her which you chose.
The recommendation this sits under
Grade A: soft tissue and/or joint mobilization, including high- and low-force long-axis hip distraction and hip mobilization with movement, to increase ROM, decrease pain and improve function in mild-to-moderate hip OA Koc 2025.
Dose
1 to 3 times per week over 6-12 weeks - and say the caveat out loud: the guideline concluded that recommending a specific dosage "could not be made", because five of ten studies were at high risk of bias Koc 2025.
Before she leaves
Exercise. Mobilization appears with exercise in every graded recommendation in this guide, never instead of it Koc 2025.
Warehouse worker, 31. Third sprain of the left ankle, now six months on. Stiff into dorsiflexion. A colleague assessed the posterior talar glide last week and recorded "mildly restricted"; you assess it today and think it is normal. He asks which of you is right.
The honest answer
Possibly neither, and the disagreement may be meaningless. Inter-rater reliability for that test is ICC 0.64 - poor to good - with MDC 4.7° Nguyen 2026. A difference under about four degrees between two examiners is not a finding.
Why it is not a skill problem
Because intra-rater reliability is excellent for both a novice (ICC 0.97) and an experienced rater (ICC 0.94) Nguyen 2026. Each of you is consistent with yourself. Experience did not close the gap between you.
What would close it
An external datum. Adding a referential horizontal plane raised inter-rater agreement to ICC 0.78 Nguyen 2026. Reproducibility came from measuring against something outside the hands - not from a finer grading vocabulary.
The practical rule
Re-measure your own marker, yourself, in the same session. Handing the re-test to a colleague discards most of the reliability the measurement had Nguyen 2026.
What the evidence supports here
Grade A: graded joint mobilizations, manipulations, and weight-bearing and non-weight-bearing mobilization with movement, to improve weight-bearing ankle dorsiflexion and dynamic balance in the short term in chronic instability Martin 2021. Both qualifiers matter.
Office manager, 55. Knee pain after a meniscal tear managed conservatively. She has read that manual therapy is evidence-based and asks you to mobilise the knee. Later in the session your thumb-and-thenar technique produces a loud pop and she is delighted - "that's it, it's gone back in."
Her first question
The knee meniscal and articular cartilage guideline carries no manual therapy recommendation at all Logerstedt 2018. That is an absence, not a rejection - but it is not the Grade A she has read about. The evidence is regional, not general: Grade A at hip and ankle, Grade F for Achilles, weak after knee arthroplasty, absent here.
What the pop tells you
Nothing about whether the technique worked. In the study that measured it, all subjects increased in joint space after manipulation regardless of whether an audible sound occurred Sillevis 2025. The joint gapped whether or not it made a noise.
Was she simply hearing it?
No - after a thoracic thrust, the audible group showed significant EEG change in frontal, parietal and occipital lobes but not the temporal lobes, where the auditory centres sit Sillevis 2024. Note those two studies share a first author and method: one research line, not two.
Nothing "went back in"
Correct the model gently. The authors' own verdict is that "the clinical usefulness of audible sounds remains questionable" Sillevis 2025.
What you can offer instead
Say what is true: the effects that are measured are on range, pain and immediate proprioception, they are short term, and they come alongside exercise. Then set a marker and re-measure it in this session Hadjisavvas 2026.

Single facts

Name the three force levels the hip guideline defines, in terms of tissue resistance.
High = stretching exceeding resistance. Medium = stopping at the point of resistance. Low = no resistance Koc 2025. This is the one mobilization dose vocabulary in the guide that is defined, reproducible from its own description, and has outcomes attached.
Which force level produced the largest effect on pain, and which on range?
Low force on pain pressure threshold (d = 2.0); high force on range - and on physical function (d = 0.5-0.7). Medium force won neither Koc 2025. You cannot maximise both, so the dose is a choice.
What is the origin of the Maitland grading system, and what is its published inter-rater reliability?
Neither is traceable to any reachable source no traceable source. That is the answer, and it is the guide's central finding. Zero of the five guidelines held here define a grade; the only mention is the ankle guideline using "Maitland grade III" as a label while summarising someone else's trial Martin 2021.
What is the inter-rater ICC for the posterior talar glide test, and the smallest difference that means anything?
ICC 0.64, poor to good, with MDC 4.7° Nguyen 2026. Intra-rater is excellent by contrast - 0.97 novice, 0.94 experienced. One clinician is consistent with themselves; two are not consistent with each other.
What raised that inter-rater agreement, and to what?
A referential horizontal plane - an external measurable datum - raised it to ICC 0.78 Nguyen 2026. Not more skill, and not a finer grading vocabulary.
In how many of the five guidelines examined here does manual therapy reach Grade A? In how many does it appear at all?
Grade A in two - hip osteoarthritis and lateral ankle sprain. It appears in four of five; the knee meniscal and articular cartilage guideline carries no manual therapy recommendation at all Koc 2025 Martin 2021 Chimenti 2024 Bove 2026 Logerstedt 2018.
The knee arthroplasty guideline rates manual therapy "moderate". Moderate what?
Evidence Quality. Its Recommendation Strength for both manual therapy recommendations is weak - downgraded in the guideline's own words "due to limited evidence" (range) and "due to low certainty of evidence" (pain) Bove 2026. Quoting the quality alone makes it look far stronger than its authors rate it.
Your patient's joint popped loudly. What did that tell you about whether the joint gapped?
Nothing. All subjects increased in joint space after manipulation regardless of audible sound Sillevis 2025. And the pop is not simply being heard - the audible group showed EEG change everywhere except the temporal lobes Sillevis 2024.
A meta-analysis reports SMD −0.78 for pain with I² = 92%. What does the I² change about how you read it?
At that dispersion the pooled estimate describes no single population. The authors themselves rate certainty moderate for pain and low for disability and mobility, with I² of 92%, 83% and 93% across the three outcomes Shager 2026. Always print the I² beside the SMD.
Why is "no catastrophic adverse events were reported" not the same as "catastrophic adverse events are rare"?
Because the review excluded administrative database studies, and its authors name that as a limitation precisely because those designs "may detect rare AEs" Daniels 2026. The design could not have found one. Note also its funding: partially from Clinical Compass and the NCMIC Foundation, both chiropractic organisations.
What are the common adverse events, and how many were severe?
412 study-related events in adults over 55, of which 9 severe and none catastrophic - mostly changes in pain quality, soreness, stiffness, numbness, weakness, fatigue, headache and dizziness. In the case-report literature, 6 severe events, four of them vascular Daniels 2026. It is a count, not an incidence: there is no denominator.
In which position were the hip long-axis distraction studies performed, and what is the evidence base for that position?
Open packed position - which the guideline names and does not define Koc 2025, and which no reachable source derives or tests no traceable source. It is the second load-bearing convention in this guide that could not be traced.
What did Scarvell measure in deep knee flexion, and what did the authors conclude about the concave-convex rule - precisely?
36 (3) mm of posterior femoral translation from 90° to 150° flexion, finishing 23 (3) mm posterior to the tibial centre, with external rotation from 8° to 16° and flexion coupled to translation at r = 0.96. Their conclusion was that the findings "invite review" of the rule Scarvell 2019 - not that it is wrong. Twenty-five healthy adults over 45, kneeling; no mobilization and no patients.
Across 26 neuroimaging studies of spinal manipulation and mobilization, what was never demonstrated?
That the cortical changes were linked to the clinical improvements. Most studies found post-intervention change in motor cortex excitability, functional connectivity and sensorimotor integration, and many reported pain reduction - but "none of them linked cortical changes directly to clinical improvements" Jahromi 2026.
Mulligan MWM improved pain, function and ROM in rotator cuff disease. What design feature limits what you can conclude?
It is an add-on design with no sham: Mulligan plus conventional therapy against conventional therapy alone, so the extra contact time and attention are uncontrolled Alqallaf 2025. Note too that the paper's stated mechanism - "correcting positional faults" - was never measured in any included trial.
What is the only proprioception result that could be pooled, and what did it show?
Knee joint position sense error at 60° in patellofemoral pain, two trials: Hedges' g = −0.21, 95% CI −1.36 to 0.94, I² ≈ 83% - not significant Hadjisavvas 2026. Every other outcome was too heterogeneous to pool, and all effects were immediate post-intervention with no follow-up.
Name the two "grade III"s a hip paper might mean.
Kellgren-Lawrence radiographic severity Grade III, and Maitland mobilization grade III. Entirely unrelated scales that share a word Koc 2025. The 60 patients in the force-level study had Grade III osteoarthritis. Every grade in this guide names its scale for that reason.
Is the capsular pattern a valid diagnostic?
No. In 200 patients the pattern Cyriax describes was "not present as a distinct pattern", and it "cannot be regarded as a valid test for the diagnosis of OA of the hip or knee" Bijl 1998.
Does the evidence distinguish manipulation from mobilization by outcome?
No reachable source separates them no traceable source. The reviews that cover both pool them together as "articular manual therapy" Hadjisavvas 2026 or report their responses jointly Jahromi 2026. State that as the finding it is, rather than implying a difference the literature has not shown.
Why does this guide cite a commentary, and what may it be cited for?
For the position it takes and nothing else. Cook and colleagues argue that mechanisms of force-based manipulation interact with unmeasured variables and are "influenced by time, clinician, environments and a litany of other mediators" Cook 2026. It contains no data, no participants and no effect size, and is labelled a commentary wherever it appears.

About this guide. Version 1.0 · September 2026. The reasoning sequence, the section order and the wording are original. Every clinical value is drawn from the sixteen published sources listed above and attributed where it appears - principally Koc and colleagues 2025 for the force vocabulary, the hip recommendations and every dose figure, Martin and colleagues 2021 for the ankle recommendations, and Nguyen and colleagues 2026 for the reliability of accessory glide assessment. Every graded recommendation quoted here was taken from its guideline's front-matter Summary of Recommendations and confirmed by position to be the current revision, because these documents reprint superseded recommendations in their bodies. This guide flags more untraceable material than any other in the series, and does so deliberately. The Maitland and Kaltenborn grading systems, open- and close-packed positions, the reproducibility of a nominated grade, amplitude and oscillation parameters, end-feel categories, normative range values, a validated pre-treatment screening test, and any source separating manipulation from mobilization by outcome are each marked no traceable source - not because they were not searched for, but because the searches returned nothing that defines, derives or tests them. The concave-convex rule is marked Contested on the strength of a single paper whose authors ask only that it be reviewed. Three constraints were carried deliberately into the writing: the two cavitation studies share a first author and are presented as one research line rather than two findings; the Cook commentary is cited only for the position it takes and never for an effect; and every "grade" in this guide names the scale it belongs to, because Kellgren-Lawrence radiographic severity and Maitland mobilization grades share a numeral and nothing else. Verify against current literature before letting any of it weight a clinical decision.
