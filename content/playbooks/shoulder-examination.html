What is the resting state?
4Standing postureObserve from front, side and back. Note thoracic curvature, head position, and whether the resting position changes when the patient is cued to stand tall.Record what you see, but hold it loosely: the link between static posture and shoulder pain is far weaker than it is usually taught. What can they do on their own?
7Active range of motion, all planesFlexion, abduction, extension, and rotation at 0° and at 90°. Watch where the range comes from, not only how much there is.Flexion 180°, abduction 180°, extension 60°, IR 70°, ER 90° AAOS 1965. A community cohort of 2,404 people averaged flexion 158-162°, abduction 148-152° and ER 55-61°, and lost roughly 40° of flexion and abduction across the adult age range Gill 2020. The textbook figures are a ceiling, not an expectation. Compare sides, and age-match, before comparing to a table.
8Scapulohumeral rhythmWatch elevation from behind through the full arc and name where the scapular contribution changes.2:1 - two degrees of glenohumeral motion for every one of scapular Inman 1944. That holds only as an average across the whole arc. It is nearer 4:1 through the first 25-30° and closer to 5:4 after Poppen & Walker 1976, quoted inconsistently by later sources, and it varies by plane - 2.1:1 in abduction, 2.4:1 in flexion, 2.2:1 in the scapular plane Ludewig 2009.
9Painful arcAbduct actively through the full range and mark where pain starts and stops.ContestedClassically 60-120° Convention. Cyriax named the sign but defined it without any degrees - the range is later convention. Accuracy varies widely between studies: sensitivity 0.33 to 0.76 and specificity 0.61 to 0.81 Caliş 2000; Park 2005. Useful inside a cluster, weak alone.
10Functional compositesHand behind head, then hand behind back climbing from the lumbar spine. Measure against a spinal landmark on both sides, then add overpressure at end range.Hand behind head is abduction and external rotation. Hand behind back is IR, extension and adduction. Positive is a clear side-to-side difference in reach, or symptoms reproduced by the overpressure.
What can I do to them?
11Passive range, compared to activeRepeat each restricted motion passively and record the difference.Passive exceeding active points to weakness or pain inhibition rather than a mechanical block. The size of that gap is one of the criteria that sets irritability McClure & Michener 2015.
12End feelTake each passive motion to its limit and classify what stops it.Firm and leathery suggests capsule, springy suggests muscle, hard suggests bone, and empty means pain stopped you before tissue did. An empty end feel is a red flag worth acting on. But do not treat end feel as the more solid finding: inter-tester agreement for shoulder end feel runs kappa 0.26-0.47 while passive range measurement reaches ICC 0.95 May & Littlewood 2010.
13Capsular patternCompare the proportional loss across ER, abduction and IR.ConventionThe glenohumeral capsular pattern is ER limited most, then abduction, then IR Cyriax. Worth recognizing, but in 25 shoulders with idiopathic stiffness the pattern did not hold: internal rotation was the most restricted motion in 92% when tested in abduction Rundquist & Ludewig 2004. Treat a matching pattern as a hint, not a diagnosis.
14Accessory motion at all four jointsGlide the glenohumeral joint in the resting position, then assess acromioclavicular, sternoclavicular and scapulothoracic mobility.Open-packed at about 55° abduction with 30° horizontal adduction. Convention no traceable source That exact pairing has no primary source. Measured, the resting position depends on what you are testing - about 50° for physiological movement but 24° for accessory glides Lin 2007. The defensible range is 30-60°.
What is short?
15Pectoralis minor lengthSupine, arms at the sides, no downward pressure. Measure acromion to table. Or measure the muscle directly, from the caudal edge of the 4th rib at the sternum to the inferomedial coracoid, and divide by height.Acromion-to-table over 2.6 cm means a short pectoralis minor the cutoff tested by Lewis & Valentine 2007. The measure is reliable, ICC 0.90-0.93, but that cutoff had specificity 0% and a likelihood ratio of 1 - every subject tested positive, symptomatic or not Lewis & Valentine 2007. Use the index instead: PMI = (length ÷ height) × 100 Borstad 2008, intrarater ICC 0.95-0.97 Rosa 2016. Note the two papers land the rib end differently - Borstad measures a finger-width lateral to the sternum, Rosa at the sternum - so pick one and stay with it. No validated cutoff for "short" exists, so compare sides.
16Latissimus and posterior shoulder lengthLatissimus: supine with the pelvis strapped, raise the arm and stop at the first substitution. Posterior shoulder: supine at 90° elevation, stabilize the scapula, take into horizontal adduction.Horizontal adduction is the more trustworthy of the two - ICC 0.88, minimal detectable change 10.2° Hall 2020, so a difference under about ten degrees is measurement noise. The latissimus test has inter-rater ICC of 0.33 Dawood 2018; use it for your own repeated measures, not to hand off to a colleague.
What is weak?
17Rotator cuff manual muscle testingSupraspinatus in the full can position, 90° scaption with 45° external rotation. Infraspinatus with resisted ER at 0° elevation and the humerus pre-positioned in 45° of internal rotation - that pre-position is part of the test. Subscapularis with the Gerber push-off.Weakness here means the cuff, not one named tendon. The positions maximize cuff EMG while limiting synergists Kelly 1996, but neither can test is supraspinatus-specific - both load eight or nine other muscles equally Boettcher 2009 - and the same fine-wire EMG work found that no test position selectively isolates supraspinatus at all Malanga 1996. For teres minor, use hornblower's sign: arm to 90° in the scapular plane, elbow at 90°, resist active external rotation. Inability to externally rotate against resistance was 100% sensitive and 93% specific for irreparable teres minor degeneration Walch 1998 - though in 54 surgical patients against advanced fatty degeneration, so those figures belong to that population, not to routine shoulder pain.
18Scapular muscle testingProne for the trapezius pair and the position that isolates serratus anterior. Stabilize below the scapula so the trunk cannot supply the motion.Prone positions isolate lower from middle trapezius Convention. What actually differs between the two tests is the angle of arm elevation, not the position - and a prone overhead raise produces near-maximal activity in both regions Ekstrom 2003. They do not - the modified Kendall trapezius tests are not region-specific: the lower trapezius test produced the greatest activation of all three trapezius regions Henderson 2021. Grade what you feel, but do not claim you isolated a region.
19Grade on the 0-5 scale0 none, 1 trace, 2 full range with gravity eliminated, 3 full range against gravity, 4 holds against some resistance, 5 holds against full resistance.The whole grades are sound; the plus and minus refinements are not. The scale descends from Lovett and Martin's gravity-based testing Lovett 1916 and the MRC's Aids to the Investigation of Peripheral Nerve Injuries, War Memorandum No. 7 (1942, revised 1943) Compston 2010. Rasch analysis found the categories unequal, with disordered thresholds in 74-79% of the muscles examined - grades 1 to 3 too narrow, grade 4 far too broad. That was 1,065 patients with neuromuscular disease graded by neurologists, not shoulder testing Vanhoutte 2012. The MRC proposed 4−, 4 and 4+ for slight, moderate and strong resistance but never defined the boundaries, and adding a 4+ grade did not clearly improve agreement: 64% in the standard group against 48% in the modified one, though those were two separate groups of raters scoring written vignettes rather than examining anyone O'Neill 2017. Use them if your program requires them, but do not mistake the extra symbol for extra precision.
20Distinguish weakness from inhibitionRetest the weak motion after reducing the provocation - a different position, less range, or with the scapula supported.Strength that returns when the provocation is removed was inhibited, not weak, and it needs a different treatment than loading.
Where is it?
21Bony landmark palpationRun one repeatable sequence rather than hunting. Rotate the humerus under your fingers instead of moving your hand around the bone.You should be able to find every landmark without looking, and name what attaches to it. Tenderness means little until it agrees with something else you found.
22Soft tissue palpationPalpate each cuff tendon in the position that brings it out from under the acromion, then the biceps tendon and the AC joint line.Connect every tender structure back to the test that implicated it. Isolated tenderness with no corroborating test is a finding you should be willing to discard.
What is it?
23Perform the special tests by categorySubacromial, instability, labral, acromioclavicular and biceps. Know the set-up and the positive for each.Performing them is the low bar. Knowing which ones are worth performing is the competency.
24Interpret likelihood ratiosRead a test result as a shift in probability rather than a yes or no.LR+ above 10 or LR− below 0.1 is a large shift; 5-10 moderate; 2-5 small but sometimes important; 1-2 rarely matters. The bands are usually credited to Jaeschke 1994, though they are also attributed to Sackett's textbooks and are reproduced widely without citation - and they are a convention either way, not a law.
25Build a cluster from your hypothesisChoose three tests that together answer the question the history raised, and run those.Hawkins-Kennedy, painful arc and resisted ER all positive gives LR+ 10.56 for impingement; painful arc, drop arm and resisted ER all positive gives LR+ 15.57 for a full-thickness tear Park 2005. No single shoulder test can be recommended as diagnostic on its own, and clusters improve accuracy only modestly Hegedus 2012.
What do I call it?
26State a movement diagnosisName the direction of susceptibility and the impairments producing it, rather than only the structure that hurts.A structural label tells you what is irritated. A movement diagnosis tells you what to change.
27Recognize the common syndromesMatch the pattern of findings to the recognized shoulder movement syndromes and name the impairments driving each.The syndrome is a shorthand for a cluster of impairments. If you cannot list the impairments, you have not made the diagnosis.
28Identify relative stiffnessAsk which segment moves first, and which one should have.Motion goes where it is easiest. The painful tissue is often the flexible one compensating for a stiff neighbor, which is why treating the painful site alone tends to fail.
What do I do about it?
29Set the dose from irritabilityGrade irritability before choosing intervention intensity.High: pain ≥7/10, consistent night pain, AROM less than PROM, pain before end range - minimize stress. Moderate: pain 4-6/10, intermittent night pain, AROM close to PROM, pain at end range. Low: pain ≤3/10, no night pain, AROM equals PROM, pain only with overpressure - moderate to high stress McClure & Michener 2015, a proposed classification the authors describe as conceptual and not yet tested for reliability or validity.
30Match intervention to impairmentFor each intervention chosen, state which finding it addresses.If you cannot name the impairment an intervention targets, it is in the plan for the wrong reason.
31Mobilize with a stated rationaleChoose joint or soft tissue technique, direction, grade and dosage - and say why each.Direction follows the restricted glide. Grade follows irritability. Dosage follows the response you get on reassessment.
32Define the reassessment markerBefore treating, name the one finding you will recheck, and what result would change your mind.A marker chosen after the fact is a justification. A marker chosen in advance is a test.

Before you use this section, know what posture does not predict. A systematic review of ten studies found no significant difference in thoracic kyphosis between people with and without shoulder pain, and concluded kyphosis "may not be an important contributor to the development of shoulder pain" Barrett 2016. A second review found scapular resting orientation in impingement so inconsistent between studies that "rehabilitation of the scapula to an idealised normal posture is currently not supported by the available literature" Ratcliffe 2014. I could find no prospective study showing that resting posture predicts who develops shoulder pain. What the first review Barrett 2016 did find strong evidence for is short-term and positional: an erect posture yields more shoulder range than a slouched one, in painful and pain-free shoulders alike. So use posture as a description of how someone is organized right now and as a thing to change during a test - not as the cause you are treating.

Other normals during AROM: minimal scapular abduction, no winging, no excessive elevation, no excessive medial rotation during flexion, and the humeral axis of rotation stays relatively constant.
Scapular assist test - if symptoms appear with AROM, manually supply upward rotation, adduction, and posterior tilt. You are looking for a change in quantity, quality, or symptoms. Inter-rater agreement for the modified version of this test is kappa 0.53 in the scapular plane and 0.62 in the sagittal plane Rabin 2006 - moderate, which is better than rating dyskinesis by eye. A positive test is also your treatment direction.
Scapular dyskinesis is far weaker as a finding than it is taught to be. It is present in about 48% of people with no symptoms at all Salamh 2023, and when 21 therapists assessed 135 shoulders it was not significantly more prevalent in the painful ones, and among those painful shoulders the unblinded examiner called it more often than the blinded one Plummer 2017. Contested Two meta-analyses reach different conclusions about future risk: one found a 43% raised risk of shoulder pain, the larger and more recent found no significant raised risk of shoulder injury Hickey 2018; Hogan 2021. Different endpoints, and the estimates overlap - what separates them is significance, not direction. Rating it visually is also unreliable: Fleiss' kappa 0.12 across 104 physical therapists classifying videos of healthy shoulders, with agreement between 54% and 77% Vila-Dieguez 2026. Watch the scapula, describe what you see, and let a symptom change rather than the appearance drive the plan.

Quadruped movement assessment

Loading the shoulder through the hand changes what you can see. Scapular faults that hide in standing show up here, which is why the movement syndrome material uses quadruped specifically to look for scapular internal rotation.

What you assessLook for
Static alignmentHead, neck, thorax, shoulders, scapula, humerus. The scapula should lie flat with the medial border against the thorax, not tented away from it.
Scapular internal rotationMedial border lifting off the ribs under load - the finding that used to be called winging. Palpate the triangle below the scapular spine to confirm.
Rocking backwardThe scapula should stay controlled as the trunk moves over a fixed hand. Watch for the scapula sliding into elevation, downward rotation or anterior tilt as you rock back.
CorrectionCue the scapula flat and reassess symptoms and quality - the same logic as the scapular assist test, in a loaded position.

Special tests, grouped by the question they answer

SnSensitivity. How often the test comes up positive in people who do have the condition. A very sensitive test that comes back negative helps rule the condition out.
SpSpecificity. How often it comes back negative in people who do not have it. A very specific test that comes back positive helps rule the condition in.
+LRPositive likelihood ratio. How far a positive result should shift your suspicion. Over 10 is a large shift, 5-10 moderate, 2-5 small, around 1 changes nothing.

Movement system diagnoses

What kind of thing this section is. The movement system syndromes are a clinical classification proposed by named clinicians, not a set of validated diagnoses Convention Sahrmann. That matters, because the assumption underneath them - that a visible movement fault causes the pathology - is not well supported. Scapular orientation in impingement is inconsistent across studies Ratcliffe 2014, dyskinesis appears in about half of pain-free shoulders Salamh 2023, and in a sham-controlled trial thoracic manipulation changed neither scapular nor thoracic kinematics, while symptoms improved just as much after the sham Kardouni 2015. The syndromes are still a useful way to organize findings into a plan. They are not a mechanism you have demonstrated.

Kinesiopathology: suboptimal alignment and movement induce pathology. The syndrome is named for the movement fault, the body takes the path of least resistance, and relative stiffness - not true shortness - is usually the culprit.

The relative-stiffness cascade

Rotator cuff overuse → posterior shoulder extensibility loss → loss of posterior humeral glide → excessive anterior tissue stress → anterior microinstability → SLAP tear. This is why posterior capsule tightness is treated as an upstream problem, not a side finding.

Force couples worth reciting

CoupleFunction
Deltoid ↔ rotator cuffThe vertical pull needs a horizontal partner. The deltoid pulls up and out; the cuff pulls in and down on the other side of the center of rotation. The taught consequence is that without the cuff the head migrates superiorly until the greater tuberosity meets the acromion - measured in vivo, single- and two-tendon tears show no such migration Millett 2016, and migration tracks with tear size instead Keener 2009. A tight capsule compounds it by preventing inferior glide.
Upper trap ↔ serratus anterior (+ lower trap)Produces scapular upward rotation: positions the glenoid, maintains the deltoid's length-tension, prevents subacromial impingement, gives a stable scapular base. Lower trap and serratus dominate near and above 90°. Serratus anterior has the most efficient lever arm.
Subscapularis ↔ infraspinatus + teres minorConcavity-compression: anterior and posterior cuff compress the head into the fossa and give inferior dynamic stability.
Impingement EMG pattern: decreased serratus anterior activity, delayed middle and lower trap firing, and dominance of upper trap and levator scapulae → faulty scapulohumeral rhythm.

Irritability drives the dose

End feel during PROM tells you which column you are in. Name the irritability level before you pick a grade; the grade follows from it, not from the diagnosis.

High irritability Gr I–II

End feel: empty, before you reach end range
History: pain at rest or at night, easily provoked, slow to settle

  • Soft tissue mobilization emphasized
  • Grade I or II joint mobilizations
  • Pain-free PROM only
  • Patient-controlled AAROM, high reps, minimal discomfort
  • Bolster into the open-packed position

Moderate irritability Gr II–III

End feel: pain at the onset of the end feel
History: pain with activity, settles within a reasonable time

  • Soft tissue mobilization emphasized
  • Grade II to III joint mobilizations
  • PROM with 5–15 second holds at end range
  • Remove bolstering as able; progress STM to end range
  • Tack and stretch posterior cuff and teres major

Low irritability Gr III–IV

End feel: minimal pain even with overpressure
History: symptoms only at end range or under load, settles quickly

  • Grade III–IV joint mobilizations
  • Sustained-hold PROM
  • Maximize total end range time (TERT)
The irritability framework has been tested, and it holds up moderately. The three levels come from the STAR classification McClure & Michener 2015, whose authors said plainly it "needs to be tested for reliability and validity." It since was: across 101 patients, 24 therapists and 11 sites, agreement on irritability level reached 0.69 Kareha 2021, and in a 692-record review, patients whose care matched their irritability level improved more on pain and disability than those whose care did not 1.2 points and 12.7 points Podschun 2024. Contested Read that second study carefully though - it is a chart review, not a trial, and only 46% of records counted as matched, so clinician skill is tangled up with the classification.

The subjective exam that sets the level

End feel confirms the level; the history usually tells you before you lay a hand on them. Ask these first, then see whether the end feel agrees.

Joint mobilizations

Two things worth knowing before you pick a direction. First, adding glenohumeral mobilization to an exercise program produced a statistically significant but clinically small disability difference - 4.0 points on the SPADI, against an MCID of roughly 8 to 13 - and no difference in pain at all. Across 15 pooled trials of 24 eligible Gutiérrez-Espinoza 2023. The guideline gives manual therapy Grade B for short-term pain, alone or combined with exercise - a weaker recommendation than exercise, not a restriction to adjunct use Desmeules 2025, JOSPT CPG. Second, Contested the concave-convex rule may not tell you which way to push. In adhesive capsulitis, posterior glide improved external rotation by 31° while anterior glide - the direction the rule predicts - improved it by 3° Johnson 2007, and humeral head translation during external rotation runs posteriorly, opposite to the rule's prediction Baeyens 2000. That trial had 20 patients and has not been replicated, so treat this as a reason to test the glide that works rather than a new rule.

The Maitland grade I-V system comes from Maitland's textbooks rather than any primary study Convention, and this guide does not tell you which grade to use, because we could not find evidence that settles it. Exercise by direction and irritability

Exercise is the part of this section with the strongest backing, and the weakest specificity. It is the Grade A first-line recommendation Desmeules 2025, JOSPT CPG, and it matches surgery: arthroscopic decompression beat placebo surgery in neither trial, and beat exercise therapy in neither at two years nor at ten Paavola 2018; Kanto 2025. A separate trial found it no better than no treatment at all at six months Beard 2018, though that one had no exercise arm. What the evidence does not support is the idea that a particular exercise is the active ingredient. Contested High load and low load produced no difference Ingwersen 2017; eccentric and scapula-focused protocols sit at low or very low certainty against general exercise; and the one comparison that held up - motor-control or tailored-progressive exercise, disability SMD -0.29 short-term and -0.33 at medium term, moderate certainty, with pain not significant - may reflect the progression and tailoring rather than the label Lafrance 2024. Load something, progress it, and match the dose to irritability. Do not oversell the choice of exercise.

Single facts

Patient has 175° of total flexion but only 100° of pure GH flexion with a muscular end feel. What are you testing next?
Teres major length - watch whether the lateral scapular border gets dragged laterally during passive flexion. Convention no traceable source The one-inch threshold you may have been taught traces to nothing published, so read the pattern rather than measuring it. A capsular end feel at the same range would send you to the inferior capsule instead. Also check whether the extra total range is coming from excessive scapular upward rotation.
Empty end feel with PROM. Grade of mobilization, and what else changes?
High irritability: grade I–II only, emphasis on soft tissue mobilization, pain-free PROM, patient-controlled AAROM with high reps and minimal discomfort. Bolster into the open-packed position.
Which ligament is the main GH stabilizer, and where does it work?
The inferior glenohumeral ligament complex, restraining anterior translation at 60–90° of abduction. Middle GHL covers 30–60°; the superior GHL/coracohumeral/subscap pulley covers 0–30°.
Three tests, all positive. Which cluster, and what does that buy you?
Hawkins-Kennedy + painful arc + painful or weak resisted ER: +LR 10.56 for subacromial pain syndrome. All three negative gives −LR 0.17. The broader rule is any 3 of the 5 SPS tests. Hold it against the finding that no single shoulder test is diagnostic on its own and clusters improve accuracy only modestly Hegedus 2012.
Scapular upward rotation is limited during AROM but full on PROM. Do you mobilize?
That is the taught inference Convention, and it is the reasonable first move - train serratus anterior and lower trapezius, mobilize only when PROM itself is restricted. But it is not validated: in ten healthy people under 5% of the variance in scapular rotation came from active versus passive movement, and only between 10° and 50° of elevation Johnson 2000.
Where does the goniometer go for shoulder IR?
Axis on the olecranon process, stationary arm vertical, moving arm to the ulnar styloid, shoulder at 90° abduction (45° if 90 isn't available). Stabilize the scapula against anterior tilt and watch for scapular compensation.
Swimmer with general laxity, anterior shoulder pain, humeral head sitting forward. Diagnosis and first three treatments?
Humeral anterior glide syndrome - more than a third of the head anterior to the acromion, lax anterior capsule with a stiff posterior capsule and weak subscapularis. Treatment: supine IR with the correct movement pattern to lengthen the external rotators; horizontal adduction stretch for the posterior capsule; subscapularis strengthening. Correct resting and sleeping alignment first.
Why does losing the rotator cuff cause impingement?
The deltoid–cuff force couple: the deltoid's upward-outward pull needs the cuff's opposing horizontal force across the center of rotation. Without it the head migrates superiorly and the greater tuberosity contacts the acromial undersurface - the cadaveric mechanism is solid, but in vivo single- and two-tendon tears show no such migration Millett 2016 - it tracks with tear size Keener 2009. A tight capsule compounds it by preventing inferior glide.
Which direction do you push for a GH posterior glide, and what is it for?
Posterolateral, with the hand lateral to the coracoid, in about 50° abduction with slight horizontal adduction and ER. It is for limited IR and horizontal adduction with a capsular end feel and limited AP joint play.
Which muscle has the most efficient lever arm for scapular upward rotation?
Serratus anterior - followed by upper and lower trapezius. Lower trap and serratus dominate near and above 90° of elevation.
