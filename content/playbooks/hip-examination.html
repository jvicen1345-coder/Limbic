Hip Examination Playbook

A full hip screen in the order you would perform it, with every value marked for what it is worth. Most of what this guide has to say is that the hip examination is weaker than it is taught to be - the Ober test does not test the iliotibial band, the capsular pattern is not a pattern, and a standardised examination for impingement is right about half the time - and each of those is quoted from a paper rather than asserted. Where nothing supports a taught number, the guide says so in place instead of borrowing the confident voice of the values that are sourced. This is a study aid for examination preparation, not a clinical reference - check any value against current literature before it weights a decision about a patient.

How to read a value. Unmarked means traced to a source you can read for free, with the citation beside it. Convention nobody has tested it. Contested tested, and the studies disagree - quote the range.

01

The competency checklist

The order is the content. A hip screen that opens with special tests has already skipped the two questions that decide whether those tests mean anything.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Lumbar and SI screen Clear the lumbar spine and sacroiliac joint before examining the hip: quadrant, segmental mobility, provocation cluster. Do this first. Hip and lumbar pain refer into each other's territory. A hip examination performed on referred pain will be normal, reassuring and wrong. Convention
Red flag screen History and observation for fracture, slipped capital femoral epiphysis, avascular necrosis, septic arthritis and malignancy. SCFE is the one that gets missed. An adolescent with knee pain and a limp needs the hip examined. Convention
Classify from the history Decide, before you touch: intra-articular, extra-articular, or referred. This chooses your tests. Running a battery on everyone produces false positives at whatever rate the prevalence dictates. Convention
Resting state Standing posture and pelvic alignment, leg length, muscle bulk. The baseline every later finding is compared against. Also the part with least measurement behind it. Convention
What they can do alone Gait, single-leg stance, squat, active range in all planes, then a functional composite. Prefer the measures the guideline names. 6-minute walk, 30-second chair stand, timed up-and-go, timed stair performance, all Grade A. Koc 2025
What you do to them Passive range against active, end feel, capsular pattern, accessory motion. Passive exceeding active points to weakness or inhibition; passive matching a limited active points to joint or true shortening. Convention
What is short Thomas, Ober, hamstring length, piriformis length. Two of these do not do what their names say. See sections 05. Willett 2016
What is weak Abductors, extensors, flexors, adductors, graded 0-5 - and weakness separated from inhibition. Test all six directions. IR, ER, flexion, extension, abduction, adduction is the Grade A impairment set. Koc 2025
Where is it Bony then soft-tissue palpation: greater trochanter, adductor origin, ischial tuberosity. Palpate last, not first. Palpating first tells you where a patient is tender; palpating after movement tells you whether that tenderness explains anything. Convention
What is it Special tests by category, interpreted as likelihood ratios, clustered from your hypothesis. Expect them to screen, not confirm. Mean diagnostic accuracy of a standardised hip examination for impingement was 45.5%. Owusu-Akyaw 2019
What you call it A movement diagnosis, the syndrome it matches, and where the relative stiffness sits. Name the movement fault and its direction, not only the tissue. Convention
What you do Dose from irritability, match intervention to impairment, mobilise with a stated rationale, name the reassessment marker before treating. The guideline's irritability measure at the hip is the FABER test. Koc 2025

02

Numbers worth knowing cold

This is the section that separates a guide from a revision card. Most hip numbers a student memorises have no traceable primary source, and the two that do are diagnostic thresholds rather than norms. Both facts are more useful than the numbers.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Normal hip ROM by plane Goniometry, each plane, compared to the unaffected side. Every circulating figure is convention. Convention Flexion 120°, abduction 45°, IR 45° - taught everywhere, traceable nowhere free. The classic tables are paywalled at the table. no traceable source
Why that matters more than the number Compare any textbook figure against a population sample before trusting it. Measured in a national probability sample (NHANES I; 1,892 subjects, analysis of 1,313 white and 370 black subjects), normative values differ from textbook estimates by as much as 18°. Roach 1991
Does hip range fall with age? Compare age groups rather than assuming decline. Barely. Differences between youngest and oldest age groups are "generally small, ranging from 3 to 5 degrees". Only hip extension declines by more than 20% of its arc. To age 74, substantial loss is abnormal, not ageing. Roach 1991
The one ROM figure you can state as sourced Passive hip internal rotation, adults over 50. Hip IR less than 24°, or IR and flexion 15° less than the painless side, is part of the Grade A diagnostic criteria for hip OA. Koc 2025
The caveat that makes it usable Read it as a criterion, not a norm. That is a diagnostic threshold in adults over 50, not a normal value. A 25-year-old with 20° of IR has not met a criterion - they have a hip that needs explaining.
Full Grade A criteria for hip OA History plus passive IR. Adults over 50 with moderate anterior or lateral hip pain on weight-bearing; morning stiffness under 1 hour; IR < 24° or IR and flexion 15° less than the painless side; and/or increased pain with passive IR. Koc 2025
FADIR - how good is it? Flexion, adduction, internal rotation. Pooled sensitivity 0.94 (95% CI 0.90-0.97) to 0.99 (0.98-1.00); DOR 5.71 (0.84-38.86) to 7.82 (1.06-57.84). The authors' verdict: it possesses "only screening accuracy". Reiman 2015
What that means at the bedside Interpret direction, not presence. A negative FADIR argues meaningfully against impingement. A positive one settles nothing - the DOR intervals include or nearly include 1. Reiman 2015
FADIR specificity Not reported. There is none to quote. no traceable source The meta-analysis reports sensitivity and DOR only. Any specificity written beside those sensitivities has been invented. Reiman 2015
Flexion-internal rotation test Hip flexed, passive internal rotation. Pooled sensitivity 0.96 (95% CI 0.81-0.99); DOR 8.36 (0.41-171.3). Same conclusion: screening only. Reiman 2015
How much evidence is behind all that Read the quality, not just the number. 21 candidate articles, one of high quality; nine entered the meta-analysis. Few hip tests "have been investigated in enough studies of substantial quality to direct clinical decision-making". Reiman 2015
FABER accuracy Flexion, abduction, external rotation - Patrick's test. Not established. no traceable source Absent from the FAI meta-analysis and from the guideline's evidence - while the guideline still recommends documenting it at Grade A, filed under joint irritability, not diagnosis. Koc 2025
How well does the hip exam perform overall? Compare the whole examination against a reference diagnosis. In 75 patients examined for FAIS, mean diagnostic accuracy was 53.6% for a patient self-examination and 45.5% for a standardised clinician examination (p=0.02) - both called low by the authors. Owusu-Akyaw 2019
Hip OA clinical prediction rule Five examination variables; count how many are present. With at least 4 of 5 present, +LR 24.3 (95% CI 4.4-142.1), raising probability to 91%. From 72 patients with unilateral hip pain, 21 (29%) with radiographic OA. Preliminary and unvalidated by the authors' own statement. Sutlive 2008
The five variables themselves Not obtainable. They are not in the free abstract. no traceable source The 2025 guideline does not cite this rule and no free validation restates them. Quote the rule's performance; do not list variables you cannot read.
Trendelenburg sign accuracy Single-leg stance; watch the unsupported hemipelvis. Contested Against MRI in 24 symptomatic patients: sensitivity 72.7%, specificity 76.9%, best of three signs. Bird 2001 But under experimental paralysis of gluteus medius and minimus, only 1 of 10 healthy adults produced the sign. Schmid 2026
Trendelenburg reliability Same observer, repeated. Intraobserver kappa 0.676, 95% CI 0.270-1.08 - an interval running from poor to uninformative. Measured in a 12-patient subset of the 24, retested at 2 months. Bird 2001
Thomas test reliability Supine at the table edge, contralateral hip held to flatten the lumbar spine. Pooled across five hip-flexor tests: intrarater ICC 0.90 (0.85-0.95), interrater 0.80 (0.71-0.88). But the classic Thomas test has the lowest interrater reliability of any of them: ICC 0.62 (0.46-0.78). González-de-la-Flor 2026
The trap in that number Check what the pooled figure pooled. The headline 0.90 is across five different tests. Quoting it as "the Thomas test's reliability" inverts the paper's own finding about that test. González-de-la-Flor 2026
Imaging, if you are wondering Referral decision, not a bedside test. CT arthrography is most accurate for labral tear: SN 0.91, SP 0.89, +LR 6.28, -LR 0.11. But pretest probability was 81% for labral tear and 74% for FAI, which the authors say limits generalisability sharply. Reiman 2017
Leg-length measurement reliability ASIS to medial malleolus, both sides. None available. no traceable source No free clinical tape-measure reliability study was found. Measure it, record it, and attach no confidence figure you cannot support.
03

Posture and resting alignment

The resting state is the baseline every later finding is compared against. It is also where the least has been measured, and saying so is the point.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Standing pelvic alignment Observe iliac crest heights, ASIS and PSIS levels, and frontal-plane pelvic tilt. Convention The thresholds that make an asymmetry significant are not established in any free source. no traceable source
Leg length - how to measure Supine, ASIS to medial malleolus, same landmarks both sides. Record the difference. Technique is uncontroversial; its reliability is unrecorded. no traceable source Treat an apparent discrepancy as a finding to corroborate, not a diagnosis.
Muscle bulk Compare gluteal and quadriceps bulk side to side. Atrophy is a chronicity marker, not a diagnosis. Convention
What the resting state is actually for Collect the risk factors the history did not surface. Hip OA risk factors include reduced hip ROM, especially IR, plus age, previous hip injury, developmental hip disorders, osteophytes, higher BMI, higher bone mass and lower socioeconomic status. Koc 2025
04

Active range and what they can do alone

Active movement is where you learn what the hip is willing to do, and single-leg stance is the most informative ten seconds of the examination - as long as you do not over-read the sign it produces.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Active range, all planes Flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, IR, ER, standing or supine as tolerated. Compare sides before comparing to a book. The norms are convention Convention and the unaffected side is the better reference.
Which motion to weight most Passive and active internal rotation. IR. It is in the Grade A diagnostic criteria for hip OA and is a listed risk factor for developing it. Koc 2025
Single-leg stance - what to watch Time a ten-second hold. Watch for pelvic drop on the unsupported side and trunk lean over the stance leg. Ten seconds is the tested interval. Pelvic drop is the Trendelenburg sign; lateral trunk lean is the Duchenne sign. Schmid 2026
What a positive Trendelenburg tells you Interpret it as a chain failure, not a muscle. That frontal-plane pelvic control has failed. It does not localise to gluteus medius. Under paralysis of gluteus medius and minimus, 5 of 10 could not hold ten seconds but only 1 showed the sign; after gluteus maximus paralysis 5 of 10 also failed, 2 with a Duchenne sign. Schmid 2026
So is the sign useful? Use it as a screen, then look for why. Contested In symptomatic patients it was the most accurate and reliable of three signs for a gluteus medius tear (Sn 72.7%, Sp 76.9%). Bird 2001 In an experimental model it barely responded to paralysis of the muscle it names. Schmid 2026
Gait Observe for antalgia, trunk lean, pelvic drop and reduced stance time. Convention No free source gives accuracy figures for gait observation at the hip. no traceable source
Squat Depth, symmetry, and whether pain reproduces. Convention Useful as a reassessment marker precisely because it is reproducible within a session.
Functional composites Step-down and sit-to-stand, then a timed measure. Prefer the Grade A measures: 6-minute walk, 30-second chair stand, timed up-and-go, timed stair performance. Koc 2025
Balance and falls Screen anyone with reduced function or a fall history. Grade A. Berg Balance Scale, four-square step test, timed single-leg stance. Fall-risk management per APTA Geriatrics is Grade F. Koc 2025
05

Passive range, end feel and length

Two of the best-known tests in this section do not do what their names say. The Ober test does not test the iliotibial band, and the hip capsular pattern is not a pattern. Both are quotable findings, and both are usually taught the other way round.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Passive against active Take each plane passively and compare to the active range. Passive exceeding active points to weakness or inhibition; passive matching a limited active points to the joint or true shortening. Convention
End feel Note what stops the movement: capsular, bony, springy, empty, or guarding. Convention The taxonomy is Cyriax's and is taught universally; no free source establishes its reliability at the hip. no traceable source
The capsular pattern of the hip Compare limitation across medial rotation, flexion and abduction. It does not hold. In 200 patients the pattern Cyriax describes "was not present as a distinct pattern" in hip OA, and "cannot be regarded as a valid test for the diagnosis of OA of the hip or knee". Bijl 1998
What that does and does not mean Separate the diagnostic claim from the descriptive one. It was tested as a diagnostic for OA and failed as one. That is not evidence about non-arthritic stiffness, and it does not stop the pattern being a useful description of what you found.
Knee, for contrast Note that the same study tested both joints. The same study found an indication of a knee pattern (flexion and extension) in subgroups - so the finding is specific to the hip, not a blanket rejection. Bijl 1998
Accessory motion Long-axis distraction, lateral distraction, anterior and posterior glide. Grade resistance and compare sides. Convention Graded resistance is universally taught; no free reliability figure at the hip is recorded here. no traceable source
Thomas test - what it is for Supine at the table edge, contralateral hip held. Read thigh position for iliopsoas, knee angle for rectus femoris, abduction drift for TFL. The technique is not the problem; the agreement is. Convention for the interpretation thresholds.
Thomas test - how far to trust it Use one version consistently. Pooled across five hip-flexor tests intrarater ICC is 0.90 and interrater 0.80, but the classic Thomas test's interrater ICC is 0.62 (0.46-0.78), the lowest of any test in the review. Overall heterogeneity was I² 99% intrarater and 98.3% interrater. González-de-la-Flor 2026
Which version to use The modified Thomas test, and the same version every time. The review's own conclusion is that the variability is methodological and calls for standardised protocols. González-de-la-Flor 2026
Is any of it valid? Ask what the test was validated against. Unknown. no traceable source The review measures agreement, not whether these tests measure hip flexor length at all.
Ober test - the received version Side-lying, hip abducted and extended, knee flexed or straight, thigh lowered into adduction. Taught as a test of iliotibial band tightness. Convention
Ober test - what a cadaveric study found Sequential transection: ITB, then gluteus medius and minimus, then hip capsule. Transecting the ITB produced no significant change in hip adduction in either version. Significant change came only from cutting gluteus medius/minimus and the hip capsule (both P < .0001). Willett 2016
The numbers Inclinometer at the thigh, each condition. Modified Ober mean adduction: intact 4.28° → 5.00° after ITB transection → 11.20° after gluteus medius/minimus → 13.20° after capsule. Ober: intact -2.90° → -2.20° → 6.50° → 9.53°. The ITB step is the one that does nothing. Willett 2016
The authors' conclusion Read it verbatim before repeating the textbook. The findings "refute the hypothesis that the ITB plays a role in limiting hip adduction during either version of the Ober test" and the test "assesses tightness of structures proximal to the hip joint … rather than the ITB". Willett 2016
What to say to a patient, then Report the structure, not the band. That the test showed restriction proximal to the hip - gluteus medius and minimus, and capsule - not a tight ITB.
Limits of that evidence Weigh the design before generalising. Cadaveric, sequential transection (Ober n=28, modified Ober n=34, 18 limbs through all conditions). It says nothing about living, symptomatic hips or about reliability. Willett 2016
Hamstring length Passive straight-leg raise, or 90/90 knee extension. Convention No free source gives a threshold or a reliability figure at the hip. no traceable source
Piriformis length Hip flexed, then adducted and internally rotated. Convention Note that this position is also FADIR - a positive may be impingement rather than muscle length.
06

Strength

The guideline tells you which strength to measure and at what grade of evidence. It does not give you norms, and no free source does either - so the comparison is the other side, and the change over time.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Which motions to test All six, each side, same position each time. IR, ER, flexion, extension, abduction and adduction - with passive ROM and the FABER test, this is the Grade A physical impairment set. Koc 2025
Abductors Side-lying abduction against resistance with the pelvis stabilised, or hand-held dynamometry. Compare sides and record the value. Convention for the manual grade; dynamometry gives you a number you can repeat.
Abductor strength norms There is no table to check against. None available. no traceable source No free normative dataset was found. Use side-to-side difference and serial change.
Extensors Prone hip extension with the knee flexed to bias gluteus maximus. Convention Knee flexion reduces the hamstring contribution; the threshold at which that matters is not established.
Grading 0-5 manual muscle test. Convention Universal, and no free reliability figure at the hip is recorded in this guide. no traceable source
Weakness or inhibition? Retest immediately after reducing pain. A grade that improves at once was inhibition, not weakness. Convention - a clinically important distinction the literature does not quantify.
Why the distinction matters here Connect it back to stance. Because a Trendelenburg sign from pain inhibition and one from a gluteus medius tear look identical in stance, and the sign itself does not separate them. Schmid 2026
07

Special tests

The honest summary is that the hip's special tests screen better than they confirm, and that one high-quality study underpins the whole literature. Interpreting them as likelihood ratios is not a nicety - it is the only way to use tests this weak without misleading yourself.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Run tests from a hypothesis Classify from the history, then choose the category: impingement, labral, intra-articular, extra-articular. A battery run on everyone produces false positives at the rate the prevalence dictates. Convention
FADIR / anterior impingement Hip flexed to 90°, then adducted and internally rotated to symptom reproduction. Pooled SN 0.94-0.99; DOR 5.71-7.82; "only screening accuracy". Reiman 2015
How to use FADIR Treat a negative as informative and a positive as a prompt. As a rule-out. Negative meaningfully lowers the probability of impingement; positive does not raise it much, and the DOR intervals include or nearly include 1. Reiman 2015
Flexion-internal rotation Hip flexed, passive internal rotation to end range. Pooled SN 0.96 (0.81-0.99), DOR 8.36 (0.41-171.3). Same use, same caveat. Reiman 2015
FABER / Patrick's Figure-four position; measure the distance from lateral knee to table, or note symptom reproduction. Document it - Grade A. But the guideline lists it under joint irritability, not diagnosis, and no accuracy figure appears in either the guideline or the FAI meta-analysis. Koc 2025 no traceable source
The quality behind these numbers Read the denominator of the evidence. 21 candidate studies, one rated high quality; nine pooled. Reiman 2015
What the whole examination is worth Compare against a reference standard. Mean diagnostic accuracy for FAIS: 53.6% self-examination, 45.5% standardised clinical examination. Owusu-Akyaw 2019
Hip OA - the strongest cluster Count the variables present. ≥4 of 5 variables: +LR 24.3 (4.4-142.1), post-test probability 91%, in a sample with 29% prevalence. Preliminary and unvalidated by the authors. Sutlive 2008 The five variables are not obtainable free. no traceable source
Interpreting a likelihood ratio Convert the result into a probability shift. Convention The familiar bands - +LR over 10 or -LR under 0.1 as decisive, 2-5 as a small shift - are a widely taught heuristic, not a value traced to any source here. What is sourced: a high sensitivity with a DOR interval crossing 1 is a screening test and nothing more. Reiman 2015
Building a cluster Combine tests that fail in different ways. Two tests sharing a mechanism give no independent information - FADIR and a piriformis length test share a position. Convention
08

Syndromes and what you call it

Naming matters here more than at most joints, because the most common name in this section is wrong. Trochanteric bursitis describes a bursa that is usually not the problem.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Greater trochanteric pain syndrome Lateral hip pain, tender over the trochanter, worse in side-lying and on stairs. In 24 patients with clinical GTPS: 11 (45.8%) had a gluteus medius tear, 15 (62.5%) had tendinitis (pure in 9, with tear in 6), and only 2 had bursal distension - which never occurred without gluteus medius pathology. Bird 2001
So what is GTPS? Name it for the tendon. A gluteal tendinopathy, not a bursitis. The authors conclude gluteus medius tendon pathology is what defines the syndrome. Bird 2001
How to examine for it Single-leg stance, resisted abduction, resisted internal rotation. Trendelenburg was the most accurate of three signs for a tear (Sn 72.7%, Sp 76.9%). Bird 2001 Read that against section 04 - the sign does not localise.
Limits Weigh the sample before quoting the figures. n=24, all women, median age 58 (range 36-75), single radiologist - and the reliability kappa comes from a 12-patient subset. Do not present those figures as settled. Bird 2001
Hip osteoarthritis Apply the Grade A criteria. Adults over 50 meeting the criteria in section 02. Koc 2025
Radiographic grading Kellgren-Lawrence, from the standing AP pelvis. 0 none; 1 doubtful narrowing, possible osteophyte; 2 possible narrowing and osteophyte; 3 definite narrowing, moderate osteophyte, some sclerosis, possible bone-end deformity; 4 large osteophyte, severe narrowing, marked sclerosis, definite deformity. Koc 2025
Femoroacetabular impingement Screened by FADIR and flexion-IR; confirmed by neither. Expect the examination to perform at chance-adjacent levels. Owusu-Akyaw 2019 Reiman 2015
Movement diagnosis State the movement fault and its direction under load. Name what the hip does under load that reproduces the symptom, not only the tissue you suspect. Convention
Relative stiffness Find where the motion is coming from instead. A hip that will not extend produces lumbar extension; a hip that will not rotate produces it at the pelvis or the knee. Convention
When to change your mind Re-examine when the picture does not fit. Grade F: revise the diagnosis, change the plan, or refer when the history, activity limitations or impairments do not fit the classification, or when symptoms are not improving with treatment aimed at the impairments. Koc 2025
09

Palpation

Palpation comes late deliberately. Palpating first tells you where a patient is tender; palpating after the movement examination tells you whether that tenderness explains anything.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
Bony landmarks Iliac crest, ASIS, PSIS, greater trochanter, ischial tuberosity, pubic tubercle. Orientation before interpretation. Convention
Greater trochanter Side-lying, palpate the facets over the trochanter. Tenderness here is the entry point to GTPS - a gluteal tendinopathy far more often than a bursitis. Bird 2001
Adductor origin Palpate the pubic tubercle and the adductor tendons. Convention Tenderness localises but does not grade.
Ischial tuberosity Palpate the proximal hamstring origin; ask about sitting. Tender in proximal hamstring tendinopathy, and reproduced by sitting. Convention
What palpation cannot do Know its ceiling before you rely on it. It cannot separate a gluteus medius tear from tendinitis - MRI was needed for that distinction, and the two coexisted in 6 of 24. Bird 2001
Accuracy of hip palpation Ask what the figures are. There are none here. no traceable source No free source in this guide reports sensitivity, specificity or reliability for palpation at the hip.
10

Mobilisation, exercise and dose

This is the best-evidenced section in the guide, because a 2025 clinical practice guideline carries graded recommendations with doses attached. Every letter below is defined - and a grade measures how much consistent evidence exists, not how large the effect is, how long it lasts, or how confident you should be in a dose.

ItemHow it is performedFinding / norm
What the grades mean Read the letter with its definition, not on its own. A strong (preponderance of level I/II, including ≥1 level I; "must or should"). B moderate (a single high-quality RCT or preponderance of level II; "should"). C weak (a single level II, or level III-IV including expert consensus; "may"). D conflicting. E theoretical. F expert opinion. Koc 2025
Set the dose from irritability Use the guideline's own irritability measure. The hip's irritability measure is the FABER test, listed among the essential data elements under joint irritability. Koc 2025
Manual therapy - the recommendation Soft tissue and joint mobilisation, including long-axis distraction and mobilisation with movement. Grade A to increase ROM, decrease pain and improve function in mild-to-moderate hip OA. Koc 2025
Manual therapy - the dose 1-3 times per week, 6-12 weeks. As motion improves, add stretching and strengthening to sustain the gains. Koc 2025
The caveat the grade hides Check what the dose rests on. The guideline's own dosage review (10 studies, 768 participants) concluded that "recommending a specific manual therapy dosage for those with hip OA could not be made" - five of the ten were at high risk of bias. The 1-3×/week figure summarises the range studies used, not a dose a trial established. Koc 2025
Force amplitude - which for what Choose the force for the target, not by habit. In 60 patients with Grade III hip OA, high-force long-axis distraction increased ROM (flexion 10.6°, extension 8.0°, abduction 6.4°, adduction 3.3°, ER 5.6°, IR 7.6°) where low and medium force did not - while the low-force group showed the largest effect on pain pressure threshold (d = 2.0). Higher force for range, lower force for pain. Koc 2025
Mobilising with a stated rationale Match technique and force to the individual hip. Grade F: modify procedure and force amplitude to the patient's bony hip morphology and tissue tolerance/irritability. The rationale is the morphology and the irritability, not the grade number. Koc 2025
Harms Record what was reported. None reported for manual therapy in the evidence reviewed. Koc 2025
How long does it last? Ask about durability, not just effect. Unknown. The guideline names the benefits as "the recognized short-term benefits" and lists long-term follow-up as a gap, along with whether manual therapy enhances exercise. Koc 2025
Exercise - the recommendation An individualised programme, potentially including aquatic therapy. Grade A for motion, strength, function and pain. Koc 2025
Exercise - the dose 1-5 times per week, 30-120 minutes, over 5-16 weeks. That is the span of what studies used, not a titration. Which dose suits which patient - by K-L grade, land versus aquatic, frequency - is listed as a gap in knowledge. Koc 2025
Dry needling Myofascial trigger points, iliopsoas, rectus femoris, TFL, gluteus medius and minimus. Grade A for short-term (3-week) gains in extensibility, pain, ROM, function and force production, in K-L Grade II and III hip OA. Koc 2025
Dry needling - what is not known Do not read the ceiling as a course length. The three weeks is the ceiling of the evidence, not a treatment duration - long-term effects are an explicit gap. So is whether it adds anything alongside exercise; it has mostly been tested standalone. Koc 2025
Patient education Activity modification, exercise, weight reduction, joint unloading, internet-based pain coping skills training. Grade B, combined with exercise and/or manual therapy. Koc 2025
Why education is only a B Read the grade as a statement about the literature. Not because it failed a trial - because "research on the use of patient education is limited since the 2017 revision". A B here reflects a thin literature, not a weak intervention. Koc 2025
Weight loss Collaborate with physicians, nutritionists or dietitians alongside exercise. Grade B, upgraded from Grade C in 2017. Koc 2025
Functional, gait and balance training Impairment-based, including proper use of assistive devices. Grade C, where limitations are observed and documented. Individualise to the patient's values and participation needs, also C. Koc 2025
Therapeutic ultrasound Only under shared decision making. Grade D - conflicting. The patient must be told the evidence conflicts, and about any cost. Koc 2025
Bracing Not first line. Grade F. May be used after exercise and manual therapy have failed, for turning and pivoting activities, in mild-to-moderate and especially bilateral hip OA. Koc 2025
The reassessment marker Name it before you treat. Use at least one self-report and one performance measure, plus the impairment set, at baseline and at least one follow-up. Koc 2025
Which measures Pick from the guideline's essential data elements. Self-report: WOMAC Physical Function, HOOS, PROMIS. Performance: 6MWT, 30-second chair stand, TUG, timed stair performance. Impairment: hip ROM and strength in all six directions, NPRS for pain, FABER for irritability. Koc 2025
Monitoring over time Track the same things at each review. ROM and strength, baseline pain, K-L grade, joint space width and outcome score. Koc 2025

Rapid drill

Two ways to rehearse. The cases work the whole chain from presentation to reassessment marker; the single facts test one value at a time, and most of them test whether you know what a value is worth as well as what it is.

What to practice

Built from the cells you marked missed while running recall. Nothing here is pre-filled - if this is empty, go and quiz yourself first. The practice method is chosen by what the item is, not by what kind of learner you are: a number, a pair of hands and a decision are three different jobs and they need three different kinds of rehearsal.

Why this is not a learning-styles quiz. Sorting students into visual, auditory or kinesthetic learners and matching teaching to the label is one of the most durable ideas in education that the evidence does not support - the studies that test it properly find no benefit from the match Pashler 2008. What does hold up is the method fitting the material: retrieving a fact from memory beats re-reading it, and spacing the attempts beats massing them Dunlosky 2013. So the plan below sorts your misses by the kind of thing they are.

Cases - work the whole chain

Open a case, then work down the prompts - answer each out loud and click to check. Answers are hidden every time a case opens, so nothing is spoiled by looking.

Retired postal worker, 63. Groin pain on walking, eighteen months, stiff for about forty minutes each morning. Passive hip IR measures 18° on the painful side and 34° on the other. Passive IR reproduces the groin pain.
Does she meet the criteria?
Yes, on every count. Over 50; weight-bearing pain; morning stiffness under an hour; IR under 24° and 16° less than the painless side; pain on passive IR. That is the Grade A diagnostic set for hip OA Koc 2025.
What the 18° is and is not
It is a diagnostic threshold met, not a distance from normal. There is no traceable free source for normal hip IR no traceable source, so do not tell her she has lost 27°.
Irritability
Use FABER - the guideline's irritability measure, listed under joint irritability rather than diagnosis Koc 2025.
First three
Manual therapy including long-axis distraction, Grade A, 1-3 times per week over 6-12 weeks; an individualised exercise programme, Grade A, 1-5 times per week; education and weight support if relevant, Grade B Koc 2025.
Which force, for what
Higher force if the goal is range, lower force if the goal is pain: high-force distraction moved ROM where low and medium force did not, while low force had the largest effect on pain pressure threshold Koc 2025.
Reassess
Name the marker before treating. At least one self-report and one performance measure - IR range, a 30-second chair stand, and the NPRS Koc 2025.
Hockey player, 24. Deep anterior hip pain on sitting and cutting. FADIR reproduces it sharply. He has read that this confirms impingement and wants a surgical opinion.
What a positive FADIR buys you
Very little on its own. Pooled sensitivity is 0.94-0.99, but the diagnostic odds ratios run 5.71 to 7.82 with intervals that include or nearly include 1, and the authors call it screening accuracy only Reiman 2015.
So what would a negative have meant?
More. High sensitivity makes it a rule-out: a negative meaningfully lowers the probability. The positive is the weak direction, which is the opposite of how it is usually presented.
The specificity he will ask for
There is none to give. The meta-analysis reports sensitivity and DOR only no traceable source. A specificity quoted beside those sensitivities has been invented.
The confounder in the position
FADIR is also the piriformis length position. A positive does not distinguish the two, and two tests sharing a mechanism give no independent information.
What to tell him
That the whole examination for this diagnosis has a mean accuracy around 45.5% in a standardised clinical exam Owusu-Akyaw 2019, so the test has raised a question, not answered one.
Teacher, 57. Lateral hip pain, cannot lie on that side, worse on stairs. Tender over the greater trochanter. Trendelenburg positive on the painful side. A previous clinician called it trochanteric bursitis.
Is it a bursitis?
Almost certainly not. In 24 patients with clinical GTPS, 11 (45.8%) had a gluteus medius tear and 15 (62.5%) had tendinitis, while only 2 had bursal distension - which never occurred without gluteus medius pathology Bird 2001. It is a gluteal tendinopathy.
What the positive Trendelenburg tells you
That frontal-plane pelvic control has failed. It does not localise to gluteus medius. Under experimental paralysis of gluteus medius and minimus only 1 of 10 adults showed the sign, and gluteus maximus paralysis produced similar failures Schmid 2026.
How much is the sign worth here?
Contested Sn 72.7%, Sp 76.9% against MRI in this population Bird 2001, from 24 women with a single radiologist, and a reliability kappa drawn from only 12 of them.
What palpation cannot settle
Tear versus tendinitis. MRI was needed for that, and the two coexisted in 6 of 24 Bird 2001.
The trap
Treating the bursa. The name points at a structure that was uninvolved in 22 of 24 patients.
Runner, 31. Lateral thigh pain. Ober test positive. The referral asks for iliotibial band release work.
What did the test actually show?
Restriction proximal to the hip. Transecting the ITB produced no significant change in adduction in either version of the test; only cutting gluteus medius/minimus and the hip capsule did (both P < .0001) Willett 2016.
The numbers
Modified Ober: intact 4.28°, after ITB transection 5.00°, after gluteus medius/minimus 11.20°, after capsule 13.20°. The ITB step moves it by less than a degree Willett 2016.
What the authors concluded
That the findings "refute the hypothesis that the ITB plays a role in limiting hip adduction during either version of the Ober test", and that the test assesses structures proximal to the hip joint rather than the ITB Willett 2016.
The limit on all that
Cadaveric, sequential transection. It says nothing about living, symptomatic hips or about reliability - so it changes what the test means, not whether her thigh hurts.
What to write back
That the positive Ober points at gluteus medius, minimus and capsule, and that the plan targets those.

Single facts

Normal hip flexion is 120°. Where does that number come from?
Nowhere you can read for free. Convention no traceable source The classic tables are paywalled at the table. What is traceable is the size of the error: measured against a national probability sample, textbook values differed by as much as 18° Roach 1991.
Hip IR less than 24° - normal value or diagnostic threshold?
Diagnostic threshold, and only in adults over 50, as part of the Grade A criteria for hip OA Koc 2025. A 25-year-old with 20° of IR has not met a criterion; they have a hip that needs explaining.
FADIR has a pooled sensitivity up to 0.99. Why can you not use it to confirm impingement?
Because sensitivity is the rule-out direction. The diagnostic odds ratios run 5.71-7.82 with intervals including or nearly including 1, and the authors describe the test as possessing "only screening accuracy" Reiman 2015.
What is FADIR's pooled specificity?
There isn't one. no traceable source The meta-analysis reports sensitivity and DOR only Reiman 2015. If you have seen a specificity quoted next to those sensitivities, it was invented.
What does the Ober test actually assess?
Structures proximal to the hip joint - gluteus medius and minimus, and the hip capsule - not the iliotibial band. Transecting the ITB changed adduction by less than a degree; cutting the gluteals and capsule changed it significantly Willett 2016.
Is the hip capsular pattern a valid diagnostic for hip OA, and in how many patients was that tested?
No, and 200. The pattern "was not present as a distinct pattern" in hip OA and "cannot be regarded as a valid test for the diagnosis of OA of the hip or knee" Bijl 1998. The same study did find an indication of a knee pattern in subgroups, so this is specific to the hip.
Give the Trendelenburg sign's sensitivity and specificity - then give the reason to distrust them.
Contested Sn 72.7%, Sp 76.9% against MRI Bird 2001 - from 24 women, single radiologist, with reliability drawn from a 12-patient subset. And under experimental paralysis of gluteus medius and minimus, only 1 of 10 adults produced the sign at all Schmid 2026.
What is GTPS, in one word that is not "bursitis"? What proportion had bursal distension?
A tendinopathy. Only 2 of 24 had trochanteric bursal distension, and it never occurred without gluteus medius pathology, while 11 had a tear and 15 had tendinitis Bird 2001.
The pooled ICC for hip flexor length tests is 0.90. Why should you not quote that for the Thomas test?
Because it pools five different tests, and the classic Thomas test has the lowest interrater reliability of any of them, ICC 0.62 (0.46-0.78). Quoting the headline inverts the paper's own finding about that test González-de-la-Flor 2026.
State the manual therapy dose for hip OA, with its grade - then state the catch.
Grade A, 1-3 times per week over 6-12 weeks Koc 2025. The catch: the guideline's own dosage review (10 studies, 768 participants, five at high risk of bias) concluded that a specific dosage could not be recommended. The figure summarises the range studies used, not a dose a trial established.
Higher or lower force for range? For pain?
Higher force for range - high-force long-axis distraction increased ROM where low and medium force did not. Lower force for pain - the low-force group showed the largest effect on pain pressure threshold (d = 2.0) Koc 2025.
Which intervention is Grade D, and what must you tell the patient?
Therapeutic ultrasound. It may be added under shared decision making, but the patient must be told the evidence conflicts and about any cost Koc 2025.
Which is Grade F and explicitly not first line?
Bracing. It may be used after exercise and manual therapy have failed, for turning and pivoting activities, especially in bilateral hip OA Koc 2025.
Which test does the guideline use as its irritability measure?
FABER - listed among the essential data elements under joint irritability, not under diagnosis. Its diagnostic accuracy is not established Koc 2025 no traceable source.
Patient education is Grade B. Does that mean it works less well than a Grade A?
No. It means the literature is thinner - "research on the use of patient education is limited since the 2017 revision" Koc 2025. A grade measures how much consistent evidence exists, not effect size, durability, or how confident you should be in a dose.

Range of motion

  1. Roach KE, Miles TP Normal hip and knee active range of motion: the relationship to age. Phys Ther 1991;71(9):656-665NHANES I national probability sample; goniometric AROM from 1,892 subjects, analysed in 1,313 white and 370 black subjects. Carries the finding that textbook values differ from measured norms by as much as 18°, and that differences between the youngest and oldest age groups are 3 to 5°. It does not carry per-motion normative figures - those tables are paywalled, which is why every ROM number in this guide is marked convention.
