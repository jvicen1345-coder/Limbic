01

The examination, in the order it is performed

check-off saves in this browser

Thirty-nine competencies, ordered by dependency rather than by anatomy: each phase decides whether the next one can be believed. Cognition is screened before sensation because an inattentive patient cannot give a reliable sensory exam; sensation precedes coordination because a proprioceptive loss fails every coordination test for reasons that have nothing to do with the cerebellum. Work down it, and the findings arrive already interpretable.

#CompetencyHow it is performedFinding
Interview — before you touch anything
1 Onset tempo Ask when the deficit began and how fast it reached its worst: seconds, hours, days, weeks or years. Tempo names the mechanism before the exam starts. Seconds-to-minutes to maximum deficit is vascular; hours-to-days is inflammatory or demyelinating; weeks-to-months is compressive or neoplastic; years is degenerative. Convention A deficit that was maximal at onset and is improving behaves differently from one still progressing, and that difference decides how much of today's exam is a baseline.
2 Course since onset Ask whether it is better, worse, unchanged, or relapsing-remitting since it began. Relapsing-remitting with recovery between events points at demyelination; stepwise worsening points at repeated vascular events; steady decline points at a degenerative process. Convention Ask for the best day since onset — patients report their current state, and the best day is what tells you the ceiling.
3 Deficits the patient names Ask what they cannot do now that they could do before, in their words, before offering any of your own. The patient's own list is the goal set the outcome measures have to answer to. The core-measures CPG conditions every recommendation on the patient having goals and the capacity to change in that area Moore 2018, so a measure chosen before the goals are known is a measure that may not apply.
4 Handedness and premorbid function Ask which hand they write with, and what their function was the week before onset. Handedness predicts which hemisphere is language-dominant, and premorbid function is the ceiling for every goal you set. Convention A 10 metre walk of 0.6 m/s means something different in someone who was housebound before onset than in someone who was running.
5 Red-flag and safety screen Screen for new or worsening headache, fever, neck stiffness, bowel or bladder change, saddle anaesthesia, and any deficit still progressing right now. Any of these stops the examination and routes to medical review rather than continuing. Convention A still-progressing deficit is the one that most often gets examined instead of escalated — the examination will still be there in an hour, and the window for treatment may not be.
Vitals — before anything upright or exertional
6 Resting vital signs Take heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate and oxygen saturation in the position the patient is already in, before you move them. The resting set is the comparator for everything that follows; a post-activity value without a pre-activity value is uninterpretable. Convention Record the position it was taken in — supine, sitting or standing changes the number.
7 Orthostatic challenge Measure supine after 5 minutes, then again at 1 and 3 minutes after sitting and after standing, keeping the cuff at heart level. A sustained fall in systolic or diastolic pressure on standing, with symptoms, means upright testing is unsafe today. The 20/10 mmHg consensus threshold is defined in a statement whose text is not freely readable Freeman 2011, so the exact cut point is no traceable source here. The symptom — greying vision, light-headedness — decides the session either way.
8 Autonomic dysreflexia screen In a spinal cord injury at or above T6, ask about pounding headache, flushing and sweating above the level, and check blood pressure against that patient's own baseline. A rising blood pressure with headache above a T6 lesion is a medical emergency, not a finding to document. Convention Sit the patient up, loosen constrictions, and look for the noxious stimulus below the level — most often a blocked catheter or a full bowel. Baseline systolic in tetraplegia is often low, so a 'normal' reading can already be a large rise for that person.
Cognition and communication — the gate on everything downstream
9 Arousal and level of consciousness Grade the least stimulus that produces a response: voice, touch, then noxious stimulus. Arousal is graded before content, because an unarousable patient has no interpretable exam. The Glasgow Coma Scale is the standard vocabulary Teasdale 1974, but its component scores and 3–15 total are no traceable source in freely readable form. Record what the patient did and to what stimulus, which survives any scale.
10 Orientation and sustained attention Ask person, place, time and situation, then test attention with a task that has to be held — months backwards, or serial subtraction. Orientation can be intact while attention is not, and it is attention that invalidates the rest of the exam. Convention A patient who cannot hold months-of-the-year backwards cannot give a reliable sensory exam, and any sensory finding recorded after that point should be marked provisional.
11 Standardized cognitive screen Administer the screen your service uses, in a quiet room, with hearing aids and glasses on. Record the score with its version and the education correction applied. The MoCA's cutoff of 26 and its +1 point education adjustment are no traceable source in freely readable form — the original is not indexed, and only translated validations are free (see sources.md). Score the instrument, then say which domains failed; the profile is worth more than the total.
12 Communication screen Separate comprehension, expression and articulation: follow a one-step command without gesture, name three objects, and repeat a phrase. A patient who fails a command may not have a motor deficit — they may not have understood it. Convention Aphasia is a language failure and dysarthria is an articulation failure; a dysarthric patient writes the correct answer, an aphasic one does not. Every 'unable to follow instruction' in the notes below this line has to say which of the two it was.
Cranial nerves — tested in order, because the order is a map of the brainstem
13 CN II — acuity, fields, pupils Test acuity one eye at a time with correction on, confront the fields in four quadrants each eye, then swing a light between the pupils. Where the field loss stops tells you where the lesion is. Monocular loss is prechiasmal; bitemporal loss is chiasmal; a homonymous loss respecting the vertical midline is retrochiasmal and contralateral. Convention A relative afferent pupillary defect — the pupil dilating as the light swings to it — is the one pupil finding that localizes to the optic nerve rather than the iris.
14 CN III, IV, VI — extraocular movement Track an H pattern, then hold the end positions and watch for nystagmus and for one eye lagging. A single-nerve palsy moves the eye in a nerve pattern; a gaze palsy moves both eyes together. Convention CN IV is the one you have to look for deliberately: the eye is highest in adduction, so the patient tilts the head away from the affected side. An adduction lag with nystagmus in the abducting eye is an internuclear ophthalmoplegia and is a brainstem finding, not a nerve finding.
15 CN V — sensation and mastication Test light touch in all three divisions on both sides, then palpate masseter and temporalis on clench. Loss that follows V1, V2 or V3 is trigeminal; loss that stops at the jawline is not. Convention Facial sensation is also the reference standard for the whole sensory exam: the ISNCSCI definition of normal sensation is that it feels the same as on the cheek Rupp 2021, so an abnormal face invalidates the control you are about to compare every dermatome against.
16 CN VII — facial movement Ask for raised brows, tight eye closure, and a wide smile, and compare the two halves at each. Forehead spared means upper motor neuron; forehead involved means lower motor neuron. Convention The upper face has bilateral cortical supply, so a hemispheric lesion weakens the lower face only. This is the single most useful discriminator on the cranial nerve exam and the one most often skipped by testing only the smile.
17 CN VIII — hearing and vestibular Rub fingers beside each ear, then look for spontaneous nystagmus with fixation and again with fixation removed. Nystagmus that suppresses with fixation is peripheral; nystagmus that does not suppress, or that changes direction with gaze, is central. Convention In an unconscious patient the vestibular system is tested instead by the oculocephalic reflex — passive head-on-body rotation in the horizontal or vertical plane Tarnutzer 2026.
18 CN IX, X — palate, voice, swallow Watch the palate rise on 'ah', listen to voice quality, and ask about coughing or wet voice after drinking. The uvula deviates away from the weak side, and a wet voice after swallowing is an aspiration flag that stops oral intake until it is cleared. Convention This is the one cranial nerve finding with an immediate safety consequence, which is why it is tested before anything is given by mouth rather than at the end.
19 CN XI, XII — SCM, trapezius, tongue Resist head rotation and shoulder shrug, then have the tongue protrude and push into each cheek. The tongue deviates toward the weak side on protrusion, because the intact genioglossus pushes it across. Convention Sternocleidomastoid turns the head to the opposite side, so a weak right SCM shows as weak head rotation to the left — the pairing students most often invert.
Sensation — before motor, because sensory loss explains motor findings
20 Light touch With vision blocked, stroke cotton once across an area not exceeding 1 cm of skin at each key sensory point, comparing every point against the cheek. Score each point 0 = absent, 1 = altered including hyperaesthesia, 2 = normal, and NT where it cannot be tested Rupp 2021. Twenty-eight dermatomes from C2 to S4–5 per side, 56 points maximum per side for light touch. The single stroke matters: a repeated or dragged stroke recruits pressure and moving-touch afferents and turns an absent point into an altered one.
21 Sharp/dull discrimination Use a safety pin stretched apart — the pointed end for sharp, the rounded end for dull — and ask the patient to distinguish the two, not to rate intensity. The same 0/1/2 scoring, and 56 points maximum per side, giving 112 points per side across both modalities Rupp 2021. The question is 'sharp or dull', never 'is this sharp': without the dull comparator a patient who feels only pressure scores as intact. Inability to distinguish sharp from dull is scored 0 even when touch is felt.
22 Vibration Strike a tuning fork and place it on a bony prominence distally first, then move proximally until it is felt, comparing side to side. The finding is the most distal bony landmark at which vibration is felt, and the level at which it changes. The 128 Hz frequency is no traceable source — universally taught, no source found that measures it. Vibration and proprioception travel together in the dorsal columns, so a vibration level that disagrees with the pin-prick level is the finding that separates a dorsal-column from a spinothalamic lesion.
23 Proprioception / joint position sense Hold the digit at its sides, demonstrate up and down with vision, then with vision blocked move it a few degrees and ask which way. Grip the sides, never the top and bottom. Convention Gripping dorsally and volarly lets the patient answer from the pressure change alone, which is the commonest way this test is faked into a normal result. ISNCSCI lists joint movement appreciation and position sense as optional rather than required, and grades them on the same absent / impaired / normal scale — 0 meaning the patient cannot correctly report joint movement on large movements of the joint Rupp 2021.
24 Cortical sensation Only when light touch and pin prick are intact: test stereognosis, graphaesthesia and double simultaneous stimulation. These are only interpretable with intact primary sensation — a patient who cannot feel the object cannot be expected to name it. Convention Extinction on double simultaneous stimulation, with both sides felt individually, is a parietal finding and overlaps with the neglect examination further down.
Tone and reflexes — the upper-versus-lower motor neuron decision
25 Tone at rest With the limb fully supported and the patient not assisting, move each joint slowly through range and feel the resistance. Slow movement is the point: it removes the velocity that provokes spasticity, so what is left is the resting state. Convention Hypotonia with a hyporeflexic limb is a lower motor neuron or cerebellar picture; normal resting tone does not exclude spasticity, because spasticity is only visible at speed.
26 Velocity-dependent resistance Repeat the same movement fast, and grade the resistance and the point in range at which it appears. Resistance that appears only at speed is spasticity; resistance that is the same at every speed is rigidity or contracture. The Modified Ashworth Scale is the usual grading, with interrater agreement of 86.7% and Kendall's tau 0.847 — but in elbow flexors only, in 30 patients, which is all its origin paper tested Bohannon 1987. A later study in 23 children with cerebral palsy found intrarater ICC 0.91–0.99 and interrater ICC 0.80–0.89, and warned against interpreting it at the individual level Pušnik 2026.
27 Deep tendon reflexes Test biceps, brachioradialis, triceps, patellar and Achilles with the limb relaxed and the tendon slightly stretched, comparing side to side. Grade on the NINDS scale: 0 absent; 1+ small, less than normal, including a trace or a response only with reinforcement; 2+ brisk, within the median normal range; 3+ enhanced, high normal or hyperreflexia; 4+ enhanced with intermittent clonus Morimoto 2024. Asymmetry is worth more than the absolute grade — a symmetric 3+ can be normal for that person, and a 2+ on one side against 1+ on the other cannot.
28 Clonus and pathological reflexes Dorsiflex the ankle briskly and hold, then stroke the lateral sole from heel to forefoot and across. An upgoing great toe has specificity 99% (95% CI 97.7–100) but sensitivity only 50.8% (95% CI 41.5–60.1) for pyramidal tract dysfunction Morimoto 2024. So a present Babinski settles it, and an absent one settles nothing — that follows from the sensitivity figure, not from the paper's own sentence on the point, which is worded the other way round and cannot mean what its numbers do. Intra-observer reliability is 0.467–0.571, so record what the toe did, not your conclusion.
Motor control — what escapes the synergy
29 Patterned versus selective movement Ask for one joint to move while the others stay still, then watch whether the neighbouring joints move with it. The finding is whether the movement can be isolated, and which joints are dragged along when it cannot. Convention Obligatory coupling of shoulder abduction with elbow flexion, or hip flexion with knee flexion and ankle dorsiflexion, is patterned movement. The named stages of recovery through which this is classically described are no traceable source in freely readable form.
30 Selective control, graded per joint Grade each joint separately through its available range, holding the proximal segment so it cannot substitute. Record a grade per joint, not an impression per limb Convention — selective control is not uniform down a limb, and a patient with no selective ankle control may have full selective hip control. Published ordinal instruments for this exist but are validated in children with cerebral palsy, not adults (see sources.md), so the adult grading here is no traceable source.
31 Upper motor control test Take the limb through range passively, then ask for the same movement actively, and note where in the range control is lost. The finding is the arc, not a yes or no Convention — control is commonly present through the first part of range and lost as the limb approaches the position that demands the most selective activation. Comparing the passive and active arcs separates a control problem from a range problem: equal arcs mean control, a shorter active arc means the deficit is neural.
32 Strength, where it is interpretable Test key muscles against gravity and then against resistance, in a muscle-specific position, with the joint supported. Grade 0 total paralysis; 1 palpable or visible contraction; 2 full ROM gravity eliminated; 3 full ROM against gravity; 4 full ROM against moderate resistance; 5 full ROM against full resistance Rupp 2021. A manual muscle test is not interpretable through significant spasticity or through patterned movement: the resistance you feel is the tone, and the movement you grade may be the synergy. Test what the patient can isolate, and say so when you could not.
Coordination and perception
33 Non-equilibrium coordination Finger-to-nose, heel-to-shin and rapid alternating movements, each performed with vision and then with vision removed. Worsening markedly when vision is removed makes it sensory, not cerebellar Convention — the cerebellar patient is roughly as inaccurate either way, because the cerebellum is not being fed by vision in the first place. Norms for speed and accuracy on all three are no traceable source; the comparators are the other side and the change over time.
34 Equilibrium coordination and Romberg Stand with feet together, arms at the sides, eyes open for 30 seconds, then eyes closed, with a guard in place. Romberg is positive only if standing is steady with eyes open and unsteady with them closed Convention — that pattern isolates proprioception, because vision was compensating for it. A patient unsteady with the eyes already open has a cerebellar or vestibular problem and has not performed a Romberg at all. Accuracy figures for the test are no traceable source.
35 Visual field versus neglect Confront the fields, then present stimuli in both hemifields at once, and watch how the patient scans a page. A field cut that the patient compensates for by turning the head is hemianopia; failure to attend to a side the eyes can see is neglect. Unilateral spatial neglect is found in 38% of individuals with right-hemisphere damage and 18% with left-hemisphere damage, with reported figures varying by lesion location, time since onset and assessment method Salti 2026. The two can coexist and worsen each other Salti 2026.
36 Visual-perceptual and praxis screen Ask for a cancellation or line-bisection task on paper, then watch a familiar action performed to command and then with the real object. Paper tasks and daily-life tasks disagree often enough that both are needed: the Behavioural Inattention Test pairs six conventional paper-and-pencil tasks with nine behavioural ones, and the Catherine Bergego Scale rates neglect in everyday activity rather than on paper Salti 2026. A patient who performs an action correctly with the object but not to command has a praxis problem, not a motor one Convention.
Function and synthesis
37 Functional task analysis Watch the whole task once at the patient's own pace, then name the phase in which it breaks down and the impairment that explains it. The finding is the phase, not the score Convention — 'loss of control in mid-stance on the left' is actionable and 'poor gait' is not. Run the task before the impairment measures where you can: what fails in the task tells you which impairment measures are worth taking.
38 Core outcome measures Administer the core set for the areas the patient has goals in, at admission and discharge and between when feasible. Berg Balance Scale for sitting and standing balance (strong, level I); Functional Gait Assessment for balance while walking; ABC Scale for balance confidence; 10 metre Walk Test for speed; 6-Minute Walk Test for distance Moore 2018. Each is conditioned on the patient having goals and the capacity to change in that area Moore 2018.
39 Localize the lesion Before writing anything, state the one level that explains every finding you recorded. One lesion should account for the whole list; where it cannot, say so explicitly rather than writing a level that fits most of it. Convention The commonest error is a level that explains the motor findings and ignores the sensory ones. A finding that does not fit is either a second lesion, a pre-existing deficit, or a testing error — and each of those is a different next step.

03

The interview, and what it has already told you

tempo before anything else

The neurologic interview is not a preamble to the examination — it is the part that narrows the differential most. Tempo, course and distribution together name the mechanism before a hand is laid on the patient, and the examination that follows is largely a test of that hypothesis. Read the last column as what the answer buys you, not as what to write down.

ItemWhat to askWhat the answer buys you
Time to maximum deficit “From the first thing you noticed, how long until it was at its worst?” Seconds to minutes = vascular; hours to days = inflammatory or demyelinating; weeks to months = compressive or neoplastic; years = degenerative. Convention This single question separates the four mechanisms better than any part of the physical examination.
Course since onset “Better, worse, the same, or comes and goes?” Relapsing with recovery between = demyelinating; stepwise = repeated vascular events; steadily progressive = degenerative or compressive; improving from a maximal onset = a completed vascular event. Convention
Distribution, in the patient's words Ask them to draw or point to the border of the numbness or weakness rather than naming it. A border that follows a dermatome is a root; one that follows a nerve territory is a peripheral nerve; a stocking-glove border is a length-dependent neuropathy; a whole-side border is central. Convention The pointed border is more reliable than the named body part, which patients use loosely.
Positive versus negative symptoms “Is it that you cannot feel it, or that it feels wrong?” Negative symptoms (loss) localize; positive symptoms (tingling, burning, jerking) tell you the pathway is irritable but not where. Convention A patient reporting only positive symptoms may have a normal examination, and that is a finding rather than a failure to examine.
Diurnal or activity pattern “Is there a time of day, or an activity, that reliably makes it worse?” Worse with sustained activity and better with rest suggests a neuromuscular junction problem; worse with heat suggests demyelination; worse with a posture suggests compression. Convention
Goals, in their words “What can you not do now that you could do before?” This list decides which outcome measures apply at all. The core-set CPG conditions every one of its recommendations on the patient having goals and the capacity to change in that area Moore 2018 — so measures chosen before the goals are known may be measures that do not apply to this patient.
Red flags New severe headache, fever with neck stiffness, bowel or bladder change, saddle anaesthesia, or a deficit still progressing now. Any one of these ends the examination and starts a referral. Convention The dangerous one is the still-progressing deficit, because it looks like an examination opportunity and is actually a time-critical one.
The interview is where the examination is designed. A neurologic examination performed without a hypothesis is a long list of normal findings with one abnormality buried in it. Tempo and distribution tell you which parts of the examination are load-bearing today — a stocking-glove history makes the dermatomal map almost irrelevant and the distal reflexes central; a sudden hemibody history inverts that. Decide before you start which findings would change your mind.
04

Vital signs, and the two that are emergencies

taken before anything upright

Two of the rows below are not measurements but stopping rules. In neurologic populations the vital signs are frequently the reason a session is modified or abandoned, and both of the emergencies here are missed the same way — by comparing the patient to a population norm instead of to their own baseline.

ItemHow it is takenThe finding, and what it decides
Resting set Heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate and oxygen saturation, in the position the patient is already in. Record the position with the number. Convention Supine, sitting and standing values are not interchangeable, and a post-activity value without a matching pre-activity value in the same position cannot be interpreted.
Orthostatic challenge Supine 5 minutes, then repeat at 1 and 3 minutes after sitting and after standing, cuff at heart level. A sustained postural fall with symptoms means upright work is unsafe today. The 20 mmHg systolic / 10 mmHg diastolic consensus definition is published in a statement that is not freely readable Freeman 2011, so the exact cut point is no traceable source here. Time the readings — a fall at 3 minutes that is absent at 1 minute is the commonest miss.
Autonomic dysreflexia In an SCI at or above T6: blood pressure against that patient's own baseline, plus headache, flushing and sweating above the level. A rise above that person's baseline with a pounding headache is an emergency — sit them up, loosen everything, and find the noxious stimulus below the level. Convention Resting systolic pressure in tetraplegia is often low, so a reading inside the population normal range can already be a large rise for that patient. This is why the baseline, not the norm, is the comparator.
Exertional response Recheck heart rate, blood pressure and saturation at the same point in the same task each session. A blunted or absent heart-rate rise is itself a finding Convention — above roughly the mid-thoracic level, sympathetic cardiac drive is interrupted, so the usual exertional tachycardia may not appear and heart rate stops being a useful measure of effort. Rate of perceived exertion and the ability to talk carry the load instead.
Temperature regulation Ask about sweating above and below the lesion, and check the room temperature during long sessions. Absent sweating below the lesion means the patient cannot dump heat normally Convention and can become hyperthermic in a warm gym without reporting discomfort in the usual way. Ask; do not wait for the complaint.
Both emergencies here are found by comparison, not by threshold. Orthostatic hypotension and autonomic dysreflexia are each defined as a change from that patient's own resting value. A patient whose usual systolic is 90 can be in dysreflexic crisis at 140, which no population threshold flags. Take a real baseline early, on a quiet day, and write it where the next clinician will find it.
05

Arousal, attention and communication

everything below this depends on it

This section is a gate. A sensory examination recorded on an inattentive patient, or a motor command failed by an aphasic one, is not a finding — it is an artefact, and it will be read by the next clinician as a deficit. The purpose here is to establish how much of what follows can be believed, and to say so in the notes when the answer is 'not much'.

ItemHow it is testedThe finding, and what it invalidates
Arousal Grade the least stimulus that produces a response: spontaneous, to voice, to touch, then to a noxious stimulus. Record the stimulus and the response, not a label. The Glasgow Coma Scale is the shared vocabulary Teasdale 1974 but its component scores and 3–15 total are no traceable source in freely readable form. “Opens eyes to voice, localizes to pressure, confused speech” survives any scale change and is what the next examiner can actually reproduce.
Sustained attention Months of the year backwards, or serial sevens; note where it breaks down rather than pass/fail. A patient who cannot hold this cannot give a reliable sensory or coordination examination. Convention Attention failure is the single commonest reason a neurologic examination is internally inconsistent, and it is testable in twenty seconds. Mark every downstream finding provisional when this fails.
Orientation Person, place, time and situation, asked in that order. Orientation can be fully intact while attention is not — so a correct answer here does not license the rest of the exam. Convention Situation (“why are you here?”) is the item most often omitted and the one most often failed after brain injury.
Standardized cognitive screen The screen your service uses, in a quiet room, glasses and hearing aids on, scored to its own manual. Report the domain profile, not only the total. The MoCA's cutoff of 26 and its education adjustment are no traceable source here — the original is not indexed in Europe PMC and only translated validations are freely readable (see sources.md). Two patients with the same total and different failed domains have different problems.
Aphasia versus dysarthria One-step command with no gesture; name three objects; repeat a phrase; then ask the patient to write the answer. The patient who writes the correct answer is dysarthric; the one who cannot is aphasic. Convention This distinction changes every instruction you give for the rest of the session, and it changes whether a failed motor command is a motor finding at all.
Insight and safety awareness Ask what they think they can do now, then compare with what you observed. The gap between the two is the finding, and it predicts falls better than the motor exam does in some patients. Convention A patient who overestimates their transfer ability needs a different discharge plan from one with the same motor findings and accurate insight.
Reliability is not validity Before adopting any scored screen, ask what its most reproducible item actually predicts. In the original acute stroke examination scale the most interrater-reliable item — pupillary response — had low validity, while less reliable items such as limb motor function were more valid Brott 1989. An instrument optimized for agreement between examiners can be optimized away from meaning.
Say in the notes what you could not test, and why. The honest record of a limited examination is more useful than a complete-looking one. “Light touch not formally tested — unable to sustain attention past two trials” tells the next clinician exactly what to repeat. “Sensation intact” on the same patient is worse than no entry at all, because it will be believed.

The cranial nerves, and the discriminator in each

the order is a map of the brainstem

Almost nothing in this section has a measured accuracy figure — the cranial nerve examination is convention, and this guide says so on every row rather than dressing it up. What the section carries instead is the discriminator: for each nerve, the one observation that separates two lesions which produce a similar-looking deficit. That is what the examination is for, and it is what gets asked.

ItemHow it is testedThe discriminator
I — olfactory One nostril at a time with a non-irritant odour, eyes closed. Usually omitted, and its omission should be recorded rather than implied. Convention Bilateral loss is far more often nasal than neural; unilateral loss without nasal disease is the finding that matters.
II — optic Acuity each eye with correction; confrontation fields in four quadrants; swinging light test. Where the field defect stops localizes it: monocular = prechiasmal, bitemporal = chiasmal, homonymous respecting the vertical midline = retrochiasmal and contralateral. Convention A relative afferent pupillary defect localizes to the optic nerve and is the only pupil sign that does.
III — oculomotor Pupil size and light response; lid position; adduction, elevation and depression. A pupil-involving third nerve palsy is a compressive lesion until proven otherwise; a pupil-sparing one is more often microvascular. Convention The pupillary fibres run superficially, so compression reaches them first — which is why pupil size is checked before eye movement in a deteriorating patient.
IV — trochlear Look for vertical diplopia worse on looking down and in; check for a compensatory head tilt. The head tilt away from the affected side is the sign, and it is present at rest before any eye movement is tested. Convention This is the nerve most often missed, because the deficit is small and the patient has already compensated for it.
V — trigeminal Light touch in V1, V2 and V3 both sides; masseter and temporalis on clench; corneal reflex if indicated. A border that follows a trigeminal division is trigeminal; one that stops at the jawline is not. Convention The face is also the reference for the whole sensory examination — normal is defined as feeling the same as on the cheek Rupp 2021 — so an abnormal face costs you your control.
VI — abducens Abduction of each eye; horizontal diplopia worse on looking to the affected side. An isolated sixth nerve palsy localizes poorly, because the nerve has the longest intracranial course Convention — it is often a false localizing sign of raised intracranial pressure rather than a pointer to the sixth nucleus.
VII — facial Raise the brows, squeeze the eyes shut, show the teeth; compare halves at each. Forehead spared = upper motor neuron; forehead weak = lower motor neuron. Convention The upper face has bilateral cortical input, so a hemispheric lesion spares it. Testing only the smile cannot make this distinction, which is the most consequential one on this table.
VIII — vestibulocochlear Finger rub beside each ear; then look for nystagmus with and without visual fixation. Nystagmus suppressed by fixation is peripheral; nystagmus unsuppressed, or direction-changing with gaze, is central. Convention In the unconscious patient the same system is probed by the oculocephalic reflex — passive head-on-body rotation in the horizontal or vertical plane Tarnutzer 2026.
IX and X — glossopharyngeal, vagus Palate elevation on “ah”; voice quality; a sip of water with a hand on the larynx if swallowing is in question. The uvula deviates away from the weak side, and a wet or gurgly voice after a sip is an aspiration flag that suspends oral intake. Convention This is the one cranial nerve row with an immediate safety action attached, which is why it is not left to the end.
XI — accessory Resisted head rotation and resisted shoulder shrug, compared side to side. Sternocleidomastoid turns the head to the opposite side, so a weak right SCM shows as weak rotation to the left. Convention Students invert this pairing more reliably than any other on the cranial nerve exam.
XII — hypoglossal Tongue protrusion, then push into each cheek against your finger. The tongue deviates toward the weak side, pushed across by the intact genioglossus. Convention Fasciculations with atrophy point to a lower motor neuron lesion; deviation without either, in the presence of hemiparesis, is upper motor neuron.
Two directions, and the mnemonic that keeps them apart. The tongue points at the lesion; the uvula points away from it. Both follow from the same mechanic — the intact muscle wins — but they look opposite because one is a pusher and the other a lifter. Reason it out once from the anatomy and you will not need to remember which is which.

The four modalities, and the two pathways they split across

the best-sourced section here

This is the one part of the neurologic examination with a standardized, scored, freely readable method behind it — the international standards for classifying spinal cord injury define exactly how light touch and sharp/dull are performed and scored Rupp 2021. Borrow the scoring discipline even when the patient does not have a cord injury: a modality, a point, a 0/1/2 and a control beats “sensation grossly intact” in every setting.

ItemHow it is performedThe finding, scored
Light touch Cotton stroked once across an area not exceeding 1 cm of skin, at the key sensory point of each dermatome, with the eyes closed or vision blocked. 0 = absent, 1 = altered (impaired or partial appreciation, including hyperaesthesia), 2 = normal or intact — normal meaning it feels as it does on the cheek Rupp 2021. 28 dermatomes C2–S4–5 per side; maximum 56 per side. Points that cannot be tested are recorded NT rather than guessed Rupp 2021.
Sharp/dull discrimination A safety pin stretched apart: the pointed end for sharp, the rounded end for dull, asking the patient to say which — not to rate how sharp. Same 0/1/2 scoring, maximum 56 per side, 112 per side across both modalities Rupp 2021. Inability to distinguish sharp from dull scores 0 even if touch is felt, because pressure alone is not spinothalamic function. Asking “is this sharp?” without the dull comparator is the standard way this test is faked normal.
Vibration A struck tuning fork on a distal bony prominence first, moving proximally until it is felt; compare sides and record the most distal level at which it is present. The finding is the level, and whether that level matches the pin-prick level. A vibration level that disagrees with the pin level separates a dorsal-column from a spinothalamic lesion — they are different tracts and they can be damaged separately. The 128 Hz frequency is no traceable source. Note that vibration is scored nowhere in ISNCSCI: its required modalities are light touch and pin prick, and the only sensory functions it lists as optional are joint movement appreciation with position sense, and deep pressure or deep pain Rupp 2021.
Proprioception Hold the digit at the sides, demonstrate up and down with vision, then move a few degrees with vision blocked and ask the direction. Holding the digit top and bottom lets the patient answer from the pressure change and is the commonest false negative on the sensory exam. Convention ISNCSCI treats joint movement appreciation and position sense as optional, with no place on the worksheet except the comments — but it does grade them, on the same absent / impaired / normal scale, where 0 means the patient cannot correctly report joint movement on large movements of the joint Rupp 2021. Use that scale rather than inventing one.
Stereognosis and graphaesthesia Only once light touch and pin prick are intact: identify a familiar object in the hand, and a number traced on the palm. Failure with intact primary sensation is a parietal finding. Convention Performed on a hand with impaired light touch it tests nothing, which is why it sits below the primary modalities rather than beside them.
Double simultaneous stimulation Touch both sides at once after establishing each is felt alone. Extinction — feeling only one side when both are touched, having felt each alone — is a parietal or attentional finding and links directly to the neglect examination. Convention
Sensory level Work from the area of loss toward normal skin, in both modalities, and mark where each becomes normal. The sensory level is the most caudal dermatome with normal function for both pin prick and light touch Rupp 2021. A level in one modality only is not a sensory level — it is a tract finding, and it is more informative than a level, because it names which tract.
Not testable, and not determined When a point cannot be tested — a dressing, an amputation, a patient who cannot report — record it rather than inferring it. A point that cannot be examined is recorded NT; a classification parameter that cannot be derived is recorded ND Rupp 2021. An inferred score is indistinguishable from a measured one once it is written down, which is the entire reason the standard carries two separate codes for the two kinds of missing.
Sensation below the level Where a cord lesion is suspected, test the lowest sacral segments specifically rather than stopping at the trunk. Light touch or pin prick at S4–5, or deep anal pressure, is what separates a complete from an incomplete lesion Rupp 2021. Stopping the sensory exam at the level you have already found is the commonest way an incomplete injury is recorded as complete.
Why sensation comes before motor. A patient with dense proprioceptive loss will fail heel-to-shin, look unsteady in standing, and move a limb clumsily — all of which read as cerebellar or as weakness if the sensory examination has not been done yet. Testing sensation first means the coordination and balance findings arrive already interpretable. Doing it the other way round produces a confident cerebellar impression that the sensory exam then contradicts.
Two ways to record a normal sensory exam, only one of which is true. “Sensation grossly intact” records that you did not test formally. If that is what happened, write that. If you did test, write the modality, the points, the score and the control — “light touch 2 at all C5–T1 key points bilaterally, cheek as control.” The first is not a shorter version of the second; it is a different claim.

Tone, reflexes, and the upper-versus-lower motor neuron decision

speed is the variable

Every row here turns on one manipulation: change the speed, and see whether the resistance changes with it. That single contrast separates spasticity from rigidity and from contracture, and it is the reason tone is tested twice — once slowly and once fast — rather than once. Where a grade is wanted, the Modified Ashworth is the usual one, and the guide quotes what its studies actually measured rather than what it is assumed to measure Bohannon 1987 Pušnik 2026.

ItemHow it is performedThe finding
Tone at rest Limb fully supported, patient not assisting, joint moved slowly through range. Slow movement removes the velocity that provokes spasticity, so what remains is the resting state. Convention Normal resting tone does not exclude spasticity — a limb can be entirely normal slowly and catch hard at speed.
Velocity-dependent resistance The same movement performed fast; note the resistance and where in range it appears. Resistance only at speed = spasticity; the same at every speed = rigidity or contracture. Convention A catch that appears at the same point in range regardless of speed, and does not release, is more likely to be a contracture — and a contracture will not respond to anything aimed at tone.
Modified Ashworth grading Grade the resistance felt through a single fast passive movement. Interrater agreement 86.7%, Kendall's tau 0.847 — in elbow flexors only, in 30 patients with intracranial lesions, which is the whole of its origin study Bohannon 1987. In 23 children with cerebral palsy, intrarater ICC 0.91–0.99 and interrater 0.80–0.89, with the authors warning against individual-level interpretation Pušnik 2026. Agreement between raters is not evidence the scale measures spasticity rather than passive stiffness.
Deep tendon reflexes Limb relaxed, tendon slightly stretched, compared side to side; reinforce if absent. 0 absent; 1+ small, less than normal, including a trace or a response only with reinforcement; 2+ brisk, within the median normal range; 3+ enhanced, high normal or hyperreflexia; 4+ enhanced, more than normal, including intermittent clonus Morimoto 2024. Asymmetry outweighs the absolute grade Convention — a symmetric 3+ may be that person's normal.
Clonus Brisk ankle dorsiflexion, held. Sustained clonus is an upper motor neuron sign; a few unsustained beats can occur without pathology. Convention Count and record the beats rather than writing “clonus present”, since sustained and unsustained carry different weight.
Plantar response Stroke the lateral sole from heel to forefoot, then across the metatarsal heads, with a blunt point. Specificity 99% (95% CI 97.7–100) but sensitivity 50.8% (95% CI 41.5–60.1) for pyramidal tract dysfunction Morimoto 2024. An upgoing toe settles the question; a downgoing toe settles nothing — a sensitivity of 50.8% means about half the patients with pyramidal dysfunction have a normal plantar response. That inference is this guide's, drawn from the figure. The source's own sentence on the consequence reads “the absence of the Babinski sign does not always indicate pyramidal tract dysfunction” Morimoto 2024, which is not what a low sensitivity implies and is best read as a slip in its wording. Intra-observer reliability 0.467–0.571, so record the movement, not the interpretation.
Putting it together Combine tone, reflexes, plantar response, atrophy and fasciculation. Increased tone + hyperreflexia + upgoing toe, without atrophy = upper motor neuron. Decreased tone + hyporeflexia + atrophy + fasciculation = lower motor neuron. Convention A mixed picture is a real finding — it points at two lesions, or at a disorder that affects both, rather than at a testing error.
Ashworth against Tardieu Where both are available, compare what each is grading. Only a moderate positive correlation between the two scales across elbow, knee and plantar flexors Pušnik 2026 — they are not measuring the same thing, and the Tardieu's separation of the fast and slow catch is the part the Ashworth collapses. Both figures come from 23 children with cerebral palsy Pušnik 2026.
Spinal shock inverts the whole table. In the days to weeks after an acute cord injury, an upper motor neuron lesion presents with flaccidity and absent reflexes — the lower motor neuron picture. A tone examination performed in that window and read at face value gives exactly the wrong level. Date every tone finding, and re-examine.

Patterned versus selective movement, and what strength means here

grade the joint, not the limb

A manual muscle test asks how much force a muscle can produce. After an upper motor neuron lesion that is often the wrong question: the limb may produce plenty of force and be unable to produce it in isolation. This section is about the second question — what the patient can move on its own — and about when a strength grade is worth recording at all.

ItemHow it is performedThe finding
Patterned movement Ask for one joint's movement and watch the neighbouring joints. The finding is which joints are dragged along. Convention Obligatory coupling — shoulder abduction pulling elbow flexion, hip flexion pulling knee flexion and dorsiflexion — is patterned movement, and it means the observed “movement” at any one joint is not under separate control. The classical named stages describing this progression are no traceable source in freely readable form.
Selective movement Stabilize the proximal segment so it cannot substitute, then ask for the isolated movement. Selective control is present when the joint moves alone through some arc; record the arc. Convention Stabilizing proximally is not a refinement — without it, trunk and pelvic substitution reproduce the movement and the test reads normal.
Grading it per joint Repeat at each joint of the limb rather than forming an impression of the whole limb. Selective control is not uniform down a limb Convention — full selective hip control with none at the ankle is common and has entirely different implications from the reverse. Published ordinal instruments exist but are validated in children with cerebral palsy rather than adults (see sources.md), so an adult ordinal grade here is no traceable source.
Upper motor control test Move the limb passively through range, then ask for the same movement actively; note where control is lost. The finding is the arc over which control holds, and the comparison between the passive and active arcs. Convention Equal arcs mean the limitation is control-independent; a shorter active arc means the deficit is neural rather than a range problem. Control is typically lost approaching the position that demands the most selective activation.
Manual muscle testing Key muscles against gravity, then against resistance, in a muscle-specific position with the joint supported. 0 total paralysis; 1 palpable or visible contraction; 2 full ROM gravity eliminated; 3 full ROM against gravity; 4 full ROM against moderate resistance; 5 full ROM against full resistance, as expected from an otherwise unimpaired person Rupp 2021. Ten key muscles per side, C5–T1 and L2–S1 Rupp 2021.
When a grade is not interpretable Before recording a grade, ask whether tone or synergy produced the movement. Through significant spasticity the resistance you feel is tone, and through patterned movement the movement you grade is the synergy. Convention Record “not interpretable — movement occurs only in flexor pattern” rather than a number. A wrong grade is used by everyone downstream; an honest omission is not.
Motor level Find the lowest key muscle with a grade of at least 3, with the segment above it normal. The motor level is defined by the lowest key muscle function graded at least 3 on manual testing in the supine position Rupp 2021. The grade-3 threshold is the convention that makes levels comparable between examiners; a level quoted from a grade-2 muscle is not the same measurement.
The order inside this section is deliberate. Selective control is tested before strength, because the answer decides whether the strength test means anything. A limb that moves only in synergy cannot be meaningfully graded muscle by muscle, and grading it anyway produces a table of numbers that will be treated as real by the next reader.

Coordination, and the one manipulation that makes it interpretable

test it twice — with and without vision

Nothing in this section has a published norm; the whole battery is convention. What makes it worth performing is a single contrast that costs nothing: run each test with vision and again without it. Vision substitutes for proprioception, so the difference between the two attempts separates a sensory ataxia from a cerebellar one — which the tests alone cannot do.

ItemHow it is performedThe finding
Finger-to-nose Patient's finger from their nose to your finger and back, your finger moved between repetitions; then repeated with eyes closed. Roughly equal inaccuracy with and without vision = cerebellar; markedly worse without vision = sensory. Convention Moving your target between repetitions is the part that makes it a test — a stationary target can be reached by a memorized trajectory. Norms for accuracy are no traceable source.
Heel-to-shin Heel placed on the opposite knee and run down the shin, then repeated with eyes closed. The finding is whether the heel wanders off the line and whether closing the eyes makes it worse. Convention Weakness produces a heel that falls off the shin from lack of control against gravity; ataxia produces one that oscillates across it. Watch which.
Rapid alternating movements Rapid forearm pronation and supination on the thigh, or finger tapping, compared side to side. Breakdown of rhythm and amplitude on one side is the finding; absolute speed is not, because it varies with age, hand dominance and effort. Convention Normative speeds are no traceable source.
Rebound Resist elbow flexion, then release suddenly with a guard across the patient's chest. A limb that does not check its own movement on release is a cerebellar finding. Convention Guard first — this is the coordination test most likely to result in the patient striking themselves.
Romberg Feet together, arms at sides, 30 seconds eyes open, then eyes closed, with a guard. Positive only if steady with eyes open and unsteady with them closed. Convention That pattern isolates proprioception, because vision had been compensating. A patient already unsteady with eyes open has a cerebellar or vestibular problem and has not performed a Romberg at all. Accuracy figures for the test are no traceable source.
Standing and walking balance, measured Where a number is wanted, use the core set rather than an ad hoc test. Berg Balance Scale for static and dynamic sitting and standing balance — strong recommendation on level I evidence; Functional Gait Assessment for balance while walking Moore 2018. These carry published change values; the bedside battery above does not.
A dense proprioceptive loss will fail every test above. That is why this section sits after sensation rather than before it. If joint position sense is impaired, the coordination battery measures the sensory loss and says nothing about the cerebellum — and the eyes-closed contrast will be dramatic for a reason that has no cerebellar content at all.

Fields, neglect and praxis — three ways to fail a task without a motor deficit

a field cut and a neglect are not the same lesion

Every row here describes a patient who cannot do something and whose motor system is intact. They are grouped because they share a failure mode in the notes: each gets recorded as “unable”, and “unable” routes to the wrong treatment for all three.

ItemHow it is testedThe finding
Visual field defect Confrontation in four quadrants of each eye, one eye at a time. A homonymous defect respecting the vertical midline is retrochiasmal and contralateral to the lesion. Convention The patient with a field cut usually knows and turns the head to compensate — which is the behavioural difference from neglect.
Unilateral spatial neglect Cancellation or line bisection on paper, plus observation of how they scan a tray, a room and their own body. Found in 38% of people with right-hemisphere damage and 18% with left-hemisphere damage, with figures varying by lesion site, time since onset and assessment method Salti 2026. The patient does not compensate, because they do not experience anything as missing.
Neglect versus hemianopia Ask the patient to find an object placed on the affected side, and watch whether they search for it. Searching and finding = field cut; not searching = neglect. Convention The two frequently coexist, and their co-occurrence worsens functional impact Salti 2026, so finding one does not end the examination for the other.
Paper versus daily-life testing Use both a pencil-and-paper task and an observation of real activity. They disagree often enough that both are needed. The Behavioural Inattention Test pairs six conventional paper-and-pencil tasks with nine behavioural ones, and the Catherine Bergego Scale rates neglect during everyday activities rather than on paper Salti 2026. A normal cancellation sheet does not clear a patient who eats half a plate.
Personal versus peripersonal versus extrapersonal Test grooming and dressing, a tabletop task, and a task at a distance across the room. Neglect can be confined to one spatial domain, and the domain that is spared is not evidence the others are. Convention Testing on a tabletop only can miss a neglect that appears at conversational distance Salti 2026.
Praxis Ask for a familiar action to command, then with the real object in hand. Correct with the object and incorrect to command is apraxia — a failure of the plan, not the muscles. Convention An apraxic patient who is treated as weak will be given strengthening for a problem strength cannot reach.
“Unable to follow instruction” is not a finding. It is a placeholder that hides at least four different lesions: aphasia, neglect, apraxia and inattention. Each has a different treatment and a different prognosis. Whenever that phrase is about to go into a note, replace it with which of the four you ruled out and how.

Task analysis, and the measures that carry published change values

the phase, then the number

The impairment examination tells you what is wrong; the task tells you what it costs. Run the task first where you can — what breaks down in it decides which impairment measures are worth taking. Then attach a number that someone else can compare against at discharge, and take it from the core set rather than inventing one. The measures named here are the ones the Academy of Neurologic Physical Therapy's core-set guideline recommends Moore 2018.

ItemHow it is doneThe finding, or the published value
Whole-task observation Watch the task once at the patient's own pace, without cueing, before analysing anything. Name the phase in which it breaks down. Convention “Loss of control in mid-stance on the left” is actionable; “poor gait” is not. Cueing on the first repetition destroys the observation you came for.
Sitting and standing balance Berg Balance Scale, administered to its protocol at admission and discharge. Strong recommendation, level I evidence Moore 2018. Interrater ICC 0.95 and weighted κ 0.92 in acute stroke; test-retest ICC 0.92; ICC 0.953 in chronic SCI Moore 2018.
Berg — what counts as change Compare against the measurement error for that population, not against any change at all. MDC₉₅ of 7 in acute stroke; 4.66 to 6.7 points in chronic stroke; 5 in Parkinson disease; 1 in premanifest Huntington disease rising to 4–5 in later stages Moore 2018. No MCID exists for the Berg — the CPG states that none of its five level I studies reporting SEM or MDC also reported one Moore 2018.
Berg — where it stops working Check where the patient sits in the range before choosing it again. Ceiling effects rise with time after stroke — 4.9% at 14 days, 11.8% at 30 days, 21.5% at 90 days, 28.8% at 180 days; floor effects fall the other way, 35% at 14 days to 5% at 180 days Moore 2018. A measure at its ceiling cannot show the improvement you are working for.
Balance while walking Functional Gait Assessment, 10 items scored 0 to 3, maximum 30, under 20 minutes. Strong for acute and chronic stable conditions, moderate for chronic progressive Moore 2018. A score near 30 of 30 means switch to something harder; a patient unable to walk is documented as 0 rather than omitted Moore 2018. No MCID has been reported Moore 2018.
Balance confidence Activities-specific Balance Confidence Scale — self-reported, not observed. It measures confidence, which can move independently of measured balance Moore 2018. A patient whose Berg improves and whose ABC does not has a different problem from one where both move.
Walking speed 10 metre Walk Test, same protocol each time. MDC 0.18 m/s in chronic stroke; 0.18 m/s comfortable and 0.25 m/s fast in Parkinson disease; 0.20 to 0.46 m/s across stages of Huntington disease Moore 2018. The household / limited-community / community speed bands everyone quotes are no traceable source here.
Walking distance 6-Minute Walk Test, for patients with distance goals and capacity to change. Recommended for walking distance Moore 2018. Distance and speed answer different questions — a patient can walk faster and no further, and the goals decide which one you are treating.
Transfers Document the transfer rather than score it. Documentation should include the type of transfer, level of required assistance, equipment or context adaptations, and time to complete Moore 2018. The 5 Times Sit-to-Stand may be used for sit-to-stand goals — permissive language in the CPG, not the “should use” attached to the balance and gait measures Moore 2018.
Goals, documented Record the patient's stated goals in their words, and revisit them. Documenting patient goals is its own action statement, and the whole core set is conditioned on goals plus capacity to change Moore 2018. “Sixty-eight percent of consumers surveyed” named balance an important goal and a primary reason for seeking physical therapy Moore 2018.
Functional Gait Assessment — reliability Administer to the CPG's protocol, and establish consistency between raters in your service annually. Excellent internal consistency in acute and chronic stable conditions and excellent reliability across acute, chronic stable and chronic progressive conditions Moore 2018. The guideline recommends each facility adopt one testing protocol and re-establish rater consistency annually, since no single protocol has been validated Moore 2018.
Choosing between the balance measures Match the measure to the task the patient's goal names. The Berg covers static and dynamic sitting and standing balance; the FGA covers balance while walking Moore 2018. They are not interchangeable, and a patient with a walking-balance goal scored only on the Berg has been measured on the wrong axis.
Measure at admission, or the discharge number means nothing. The CPG's recommendation is not “use these measures” but use them under the same test conditions at admission and discharge, and where feasible in between, passing the results to the next level of care Moore 2018. A discharge score with no admission comparator documents a level of function, which is not what anyone needed.

Findings to level — the table the whole examination was for

one lesion should explain the list

Every phase above produced findings. This is where they resolve into a level. Read it as a decision table: the first column is what you found, the second is the qualifier that makes it discriminating, and the last is where the lesion is. Where two levels remain possible, the qualifier column names the test that separates them.

ItemThe qualifier that discriminatesWhere the lesion is
Weakness with increased tone, hyperreflexia, upgoing toe No atrophy, no fasciculation; distribution is a whole limb or a whole side rather than a nerve or a root. Upper motor neuron — cortex, internal capsule, brainstem or cord. Convention The upgoing toe carries 99% specificity for pyramidal dysfunction Morimoto 2024, so its presence is close to decisive; its absence is not, at 50.8% sensitivity Morimoto 2024.
Weakness with decreased tone, hyporeflexia, atrophy Fasciculations present; distribution follows a root, a plexus or a named nerve. Lower motor neuron — anterior horn, root, plexus or peripheral nerve. Convention Which of the four is decided by the distribution, not by the tone.
Face and body weak on the same side Lower face only, forehead spared. Contralateral hemisphere, above the facial nucleus. Convention Forehead sparing is the discriminator; testing only the smile cannot produce it.
Face weak on one side, body on the other Crossed findings, with any cranial nerve palsy on the face side. Brainstem, at the level of that cranial nerve nucleus. Convention Crossed signs are the most specific localization the bedside examination produces, because the tracts have crossed and the nerve has not.
A sensory level on the trunk Both pin prick and light touch become normal at the same dermatome, with weakness below it. Spinal cord, at or above that level. The sensory level is the most caudal dermatome normal for both modalities Rupp 2021. A level in one modality only is a tract finding, not a level.
Vibration and position lost, pin preserved Loss follows the dorsal columns; Romberg positive with a steady eyes-open stance. Dorsal column — posterior cord, or a large-fibre neuropathy. Convention The dissociation between modalities is the finding; either modality alone would be uninformative.
Pin and temperature lost, vibration preserved Loss is contralateral to the weakness, or in a cape distribution. Spinothalamic tract — anterolateral cord. Convention Contralateral pin loss with ipsilateral weakness and dorsal column loss is a hemicord picture.
Stocking-glove sensory loss Symmetric, distal, longest nerves first, with reduced or absent ankle reflexes. Length-dependent peripheral neuropathy. Convention No level will be found on the trunk, and looking for one wastes the examination.
Dysmetria equally bad with eyes open and closed Steady in stance with eyes open; no proprioceptive loss on testing. Cerebellum, ipsilateral to the limb. Convention The eyes-open/eyes-closed contrast is what separates this from sensory ataxia, and it costs one extra repetition.
Clumsiness that worsens sharply with eyes closed Joint position sense impaired on formal testing; Romberg positive. Sensory ataxia — dorsal column or large-fibre peripheral. Convention Not cerebellar, and treating it as cerebellar misses that vision is the compensation being relied on.
Inattention to one side that the eyes can see The patient does not search; paper and daily-life tasks both affected. Contralateral hemisphere, most often right parietal. Present in 38% of right-hemisphere and 18% of left-hemisphere damage Salti 2026. Distinguish from hemianopia by whether the patient searches Convention.
Fatigable weakness with normal sensation and reflexes Worse with sustained or repeated effort, better with rest; often ocular or bulbar first. Neuromuscular junction. Convention The examination is normal at the start and abnormal at the end, so a single-repetition test will miss it entirely.
A finding that does not fit is information, not noise. The temptation at this table is to pick the level that explains most of the list and let the rest go. A finding that does not fit means one of three things — a second lesion, a pre-existing deficit, or a testing error — and each has a different next step. Name which one you think it is in the note, so the next examiner can check it.
Spinal shock, again. In the first days to weeks after an acute cord injury the upper motor neuron rows of this table read as lower motor neuron ones — flaccid, areflexic, no upgoing toe. Localize with the date of injury in front of you.

Rapid drill

Twenty-two questions over the whole examination. Answer before you open one — the answer is hidden until clicked, and the point of the format is the attempt, not the reading.

What to practice

Built from the cells you marked missed while running recall. Nothing here is pre-filled - if this is empty, go and quiz yourself first. The practice method is chosen by what the item is, not by what kind of learner you are: a number, a pair of hands and a decision are three different jobs and they need three different kinds of rehearsal.

Why this is not a learning-styles quiz. Sorting students into visual, auditory or kinesthetic learners and matching teaching to the label is one of the most durable ideas in education that the evidence does not support - the studies that test it properly find no benefit from the match Pashler 2008. What does hold up is the method fitting the material: retrieving a fact from memory beats re-reading it, and spacing the attempts beats massing them Dunlosky 2013. So the plan below sorts your misses by the kind of thing they are.
QuestionAnswer
What single question in the interview separates a vascular from a demyelinating mechanism? Time to maximum deficit. Seconds to minutes is vascular; hours to days is inflammatory or demyelinating; weeks to months is compressive; years is degenerative. Convention
Light touch is scored 0, 1 or 2. What does each mean, and what is the control? 0 = absent; 1 = altered, including hyperaesthesia; 2 = normal or intact — normal meaning it feels the same as on the cheek. The cheek is the control Rupp 2021. Points that cannot be tested are NT.
How many sensory points are there per side, and how do they divide? 112 per side — 56 light touch and 56 pin prick, across 28 dermatomes from C2 to S4–5 Rupp 2021.
Why must sharp/dull be asked as “sharp or dull?” rather than “is this sharp?” Without the dull comparator, a patient who feels only pressure scores as intact. Inability to distinguish the two scores 0 even when touch is felt Rupp 2021.
A Babinski sign is absent. What have you ruled out? Almost nothing. Sensitivity is 50.8% (95% CI 41.5–60.1) against a specificity of 99% (95% CI 97.7–100) Morimoto 2024 — its presence is close to decisive, its absence is not.
Give the NINDS reflex scale from 0 to 4+. 0 absent; 1+ small, less than normal, including a trace or a response only with reinforcement; 2+ brisk, within the median normal range; 3+ enhanced, high normal or hyperreflexia; 4+ enhanced with intermittent clonus Morimoto 2024.
What did the original Modified Ashworth paper actually test? Elbow flexors, in 30 patients with intracranial lesions, by two raters: 86.7% agreement and Kendall's tau 0.847 Bohannon 1987. Nothing about other joints, other populations, or whether the scale measures spasticity rather than passive stiffness.
How do you tell spasticity from rigidity at the bedside? Change the speed. Resistance that appears only with fast movement is spasticity; resistance that is the same at every speed is rigidity or contracture Convention.
Forehead spared on a weak face — what does that tell you? Upper motor neuron: the upper face has bilateral cortical supply, so a hemispheric lesion weakens the lower face only Convention. Forehead involvement means the lesion is at or below the facial nucleus.
Which way does the tongue deviate, and which way does the uvula? The tongue points toward the weak side; the uvula points away from it Convention. Both follow from the intact muscle winning — one pushes, the other lifts.
A patient is unsteady in stance with their eyes already open. Is Romberg positive? No — they have not performed a Romberg. It is positive only if stance is steady with eyes open and unsteady with them closed, which is what isolates proprioception Convention. Unsteady from the start is cerebellar or vestibular.
What separates a cerebellar from a sensory ataxia on finger-to-nose? Removing vision. The cerebellar patient is about as inaccurate either way; the sensory patient is markedly worse with the eyes closed, because vision had been substituting for proprioception Convention.
How do you distinguish neglect from hemianopia? Whether the patient searches. A field cut prompts head-turning and searching; neglect does not, because nothing feels missing Convention. They coexist often, and coexistence worsens functional impact Salti 2026.
How common is spatial neglect after stroke? 38% after right-hemisphere damage and 18% after left-hemisphere damage, with figures varying by lesion site, time since onset and assessment method Salti 2026.
What change on the Berg Balance Scale exceeds measurement error in acute stroke? MDC₉₅ of 7 points; in chronic stroke 4.66 to 6.7 Moore 2018. There is no MCID for the Berg — the CPG says none of its five level I studies reporting SEM or MDC also reported one Moore 2018.
Why is a Berg score of 54 at 6 months less useful than the same score at 14 days? Ceiling effects rise with time after stroke — 4.9% at 14 days to 28.8% at 180 days Moore 2018. Near the ceiling the scale cannot show the improvement you are working for.
Which core-set measure is recommended with permissive rather than directive language? The 5 Times Sit-to-Stand: it “may be used” for sit-to-stand goals, while transfers themselves are to be documented — type, assistance, equipment or context adaptations, and time to complete Moore 2018.
The original stroke examination scale found its most reliable item was also nearly useless. Which, and why does it matter? Pupillary response — the most interrater-reliable item had low validity, while less reliable items such as limb motor function were more valid Brott 1989. Reliability and validity are different properties, and an examination optimized for agreement can be optimized away from meaning.
When is a manual muscle grade not worth recording? Through significant spasticity or patterned movement: the resistance you feel is tone and the movement you grade may be the synergy Convention. Record why it is not interpretable instead — a wrong grade gets used by everyone downstream.
What defines the motor level? The lowest key muscle function graded at least 3 on manual testing in the supine position, with the segment above normal Rupp 2021. Ten key muscles per side, C5–T1 and L2–S1 Rupp 2021.
Crossed findings — face weak on one side, body on the other. Where is the lesion? The brainstem, at the level of that cranial nerve nucleus Convention. The long tracts have already crossed and the nerve has not, which is why crossed signs are the most specific localization the bedside exam produces.
Why does the tone examination mislead in the first weeks after a cord injury? Spinal shock: an upper motor neuron lesion presents flaccid and areflexic Convention, so the findings read as lower motor neuron. Date every tone finding and re-examine.
A sensory point cannot be tested because of a dressing. What do you write? NT — not testable Rupp 2021. A parameter that cannot be derived from the examination is ND, not determined. The standard carries two codes for missing data precisely because an inferred score reads as a measured one afterwards.
What separates a complete from an incomplete cord lesion on the sensory exam? Sensation in the lowest sacral segments: light touch or pin prick at S4–5, or deep anal pressure Rupp 2021. Stopping at the trunk level already found is how an incomplete injury gets recorded as complete.
Do the Ashworth and Tardieu scales measure the same thing? No — only a moderate positive correlation between them across elbow, knee and plantar flexors in 23 children with cerebral palsy Pušnik 2026. The Tardieu separates the fast from the slow catch; the Ashworth collapses that distinction.
Your patient's goal is to walk safely in a crowded corridor. Berg or FGA? The FGA — the Berg covers static and dynamic sitting and standing balance, the FGA covers balance while walking Moore 2018. Scoring a walking-balance goal only on the Berg measures the wrong axis.
